### (1) Reading of the founder parameters

**«Pause zählt nicht als Arbeitszeit» (R-h, Pensum without the gap): consistent, with one condition.**
- Settled: ArG Art. 15 Abs. 2: pauses count as working time «wenn die Arbeitnehmer ihren Arbeitsplatz nicht verlassen dürfen». ArGV 1 Art. 13 Abs. 1: working time is the time at the employer's disposal. ArGV 1 Art. 15 Abs. 1: on-call service in the establishment is working time in full.
- Consequence: the gap between block 1 and block 2 is not working time **only if** the person may leave the workplace and is not on on-site call during it. NAVORA cannot know this; for a real home it is an employer fact (owner / Phase B partner). If staff must stay on site or on internal call, the gap is working time and Pensum, S5 and R-i are understated.
- UNKNOWN: none of the texts read contains a rule specific to the «geteilter Dienst» or a minimum gap length; I found no source either way. Not invented.
- IMPLEMENTED (code read): the founder rule as built (`split.ts`, `private.institution_shift_minutes` per the report); the screen text «Die Pause zählt nicht als Arbeitszeit» with the mark «Arbeitszeitgesetz nicht abschliessend geprüft».

**«Ruhezeit beginnt nach Block 2» (R-f, K1): consistent.**
- Settled: ArG Art. 15a Abs. 1: daily rest of at least eleven *consecutive* hours. The gap between two blocks of one day does not satisfy it and is not rest; measuring from the end of block 2 to the next shift (and from the previous shift to block 1) is the reading the text supports.
- The built rule is the more protective one: it keeps 11 h without the reductions the law allows (ArG Art. 15a Abs. 2: once a week to 8 h, 11 h on a two-week average; ArGV 2 Art. 9 via Art. 15/16 for hospitals and homes: to 9 h, 12 h average). Not building these is not a defect.

**Related point, not in scope (S6):** ArGV 1 Art. 18 Abs. 2: pauses «um die Mitte der Arbeitszeit»; a part of more than 5½ hours needs an additional pause; the Art. 15 Wegleitung says «Pausen» at the start or end are not real pauses. So the gap does not replace the pause inside a block longer than 5½ h. Brief: S6 out of scope. Proposal for real data: follow-up (hint when a block exceeds 5½ h).

### (2) R-i (50 h/week) and R-j (14 h span)

**R-j (14 h): the rule that matters most for a split shift; should apply before real data.**
- Settled: ArG Art. 10 Abs. 3: an individual's day and evening work lies «mit Einschluss der Pausen und der Überzeit innerhalb von 14 Stunden». The gap counts into the span. A split shift is the shift type most likely to breach it (e.g. 06:00–10:00 and 17:00–21:00 = 15 h). Today nothing checks it (V1–V5 allow any blocks within one day).
- Homes: ArGV 2 Art. 16 Abs. 1 does **not** list Art. 5 (17-h span); hospitals (Art. 15) do, under a 12-h weekly-average rest and 8-h minimum rest. So for a nursing home the law value read is 14 h; a hospital may have up to 17 h under conditions.
- Proposal: R-j as a home parameter, value **14 h** as the ArG value for homes; the founder sets the start value (FE-127). Without a value, real data stay blocked for split shifts.

**R-i (50 h/week): may apply; value 50 h, as a warning, not as an absolute cap.**
- Settled: ArG Art. 9 Abs. 1 lit. b: 50 h «für alle übrigen Arbeitnehmer»; the Art. 9 Wegleitung places health-sector, hospital and home staff in the 50-h group (a Wegleitung reading; «Pflege» is not named in the article). Measured per calendar week.
- Not settled here: overtime beyond the maximum is possible within the ArG overtime rules (Art. 12; ArGV 2 Art. 8: 140 h/year cap per the Art. 15/16 Wegleitungen; I did not read Art. 12 ArG itself). So a hard block at 50 h would be stricter than the law; a warning with audit (as HD-3a does for rule breaches) fits.
- ArGV 2 Art. 7 Abs. 2 (7 days in a row) requires 50 h on a two-week average and ≤ 9 h a day; NAVORA keeps ≤ 6 days as a hard rule, so this condition is not engaged now.

**Applicability (both R-i and R-j), UNKNOWN until the Phase B partner is known:** ArG Art. 2 Abs. 1 lit. a and ArGV 1 Art. 7 exclude public-law bodies whose staff are mostly in public-law employment from the working- and rest-time rules (cantonal or communal personnel law applies instead; not read); private-law staff of such a body stay covered (ArGV 1 Art. 7 Abs. 2).
