### CGR-199 (5) · May a referrer confirm the client's consent?

- **Settled on the sources relied on:**
  - Consent belongs to the person concerned. Under DSG Art. 6 Abs. 6-7 it is valid only from that person (or, where she cannot consent, from her representative, D-9 g5), for specific processing, after adequate information, and expressly for health data.
  - A referrer (doctor, hospital discharge desk, relative, home) is in general **neither** the person concerned **nor** her representative. A tick by the referrer is therefore at most the referrer's **statement that the client consented**, not the consent itself.
  - Today's gate already rests on the protective reading: intake confirms, who and when stored (IMPLEMENTED). D-9 option D (recommended, `awaiting-approval: owner`) puts the case on the care contract and keeps consent for the steps that need it.
- **Reading:** `cra`'s condition («Einwilligung nicht bestätigt» must not become a tick box that makes the referral ready) is right **in data-protection law too**. A referrer's statement may be recorded as information, but it must not close the consent gap.
- **Verdict:**
  - (a) **Protective design: compliant under every plausible interpretation** (Phase A and, for this step, real data), when all of (K-a) to (K-d) hold:
    - **(K-a)** The referrer view never offers «Einwilligung bestätigen» or any control that clears the consent gap. The gap is cleared only by the existing intake confirmation (who, when), as today.
    - **(K-b)** The referrer may record «Klientin hat laut Zuweiser zugestimmt» (or «unbekannt») as a **referrer statement**, stored with author and time, shown as such, and it does **not** count as consent in `case_readiness`. pgTAP: the statement leaves the consent fact missing.
    - **(K-c)** The intake confirmation states who consented (client or representative, D-9 g5) and what for; the referrer's statement never fills these fields.
    - **(K-d)** No referral text or button reads as if the referrer had given consent («Einwilligung erteilt», «Ich willige ein»).
  - (b) **Referrer who is herself the representative** (for example a relative with a power of attorney): **unresolved: needs legal adviser.** Exact question (joins D-9 question 1): «Who may consent to the processing and disclosure of a client's health data for home care or a nursing-home stay when the client lacks capacity (ZGB Art. 360 ff., 378; DSG Art. 6 Abs. 7), and what proof of the representation must the provider keep before relying on it?» Until answered, (K-a) holds for her too: she gives the consent to intake, not by a tick in the referrer view.
