### Ask 1 · Billing day of a visit across midnight

- **Settled on the sources relied on:** KLV Art. 7a Abs. 2 pays home-care services per time unit; how minutes form units and what one *Einsatz* is are already OPEN (D-18/1, D-18/9). **No source I read says to which calendar day a visit that starts before and ends after midnight is attributed.** That is **UNKNOWN**; it is set, if anywhere, by the administrative or tariff contract of the payer, the Forum Datenaustausch invoice standard, or the cantonal residual-financing rules (none read, none available before a partner, Phase B).
- **Protective design (compliant under every plausible interpretation, Phase A and real data):**
  - **(M-a)** Store the actual start and end of a visit as instants (`timestamptz`), and show the date of both in Europe/Zurich time when they differ. Never store only a clock time with a guessed day (consistent with CGR-200 and the PO 01:39 row).
  - **(M-b)** Never split, move or round a visit's minutes to a day in the care record. The record keeps the facts; a billing day is derived only at invoicing, by a rule that a decision names (none today; Phase A invoices nothing, IMPLEMENTED).
  - **(M-c)** No screen, export or measure labels a cross-midnight visit as belonging to one billing day until that decision exists.
- **Unresolved: needs legal adviser and partner (route P, A).** Exact question, proposed for D-18 (it extends D-18/9): «Under the partner's administrative and tariff contracts and the Forum Datenaustausch standard (current versions), to which calendar day is a home-care visit attributed that starts before and ends after midnight: the start day, the end day, or split per day; and does the answer change for the 5-minute units and the 10-minute minimum of KLV Art. 7a Abs. 2?» For nursing homes the question is different (daily care-level contributions, not per visit) and not asked here.

### Ask 2 · AUT-108 (1): phone keyboard dictation

- **Reading:** a nurse may use the phone's own keyboard dictation in any NAVORA text field. Whether that audio or text leaves the device to the OS vendor depends on the device, settings and vendor terms: **UNKNOWN** (not read). If it does, client or staff data reach a speech-recognition service outside NAVORA's ledger.
- **On the sources relied on:** that would breach founder rule **D-35 rule A** (no client or staff data to any AI service) once real data exist, whatever the law says. Legally it is a disclosure to a third party outside any processing contract (StGB 321 for the professional secret; DSG on processing by third parties, not re-read). Dictation in the client's presence also raises D-35 term 10 ([S1], StGB 179bis/179ter).
- **Verdict:**
  - **Phase A (synthetic data):** compliant under every plausible interpretation; no real data can leave.
  - **Real data: unresolved: needs owner (organisational policy) and legal adviser.** Protective conditions to carry into the real-data gate (with D-35 and D-41): **(V-a)** NAVORA does not invite dictation in free-text fields that may hold health content (no microphone button, no «diktieren» hint); **(V-b)** the provider's device policy (MDM or written instruction) switches off cloud keyboard dictation on work devices, or allows only a mode the provider has verified stays on the device; **(V-c)** the information to staff names this. Whether a web app can technically block OS dictation is `eng`'s question (UNKNOWN). Exact adviser question: «Is a nurse's use of the phone vendor's cloud keyboard dictation to enter client notes into the provider's care system a disclosure of a professional secret (StGB 321) or processing by a third party that requires a contract and cross-border safeguards under the DSG, for which the provider is responsible; and does a device policy that allows only on-device recognition suffice?»

### Ask 3 · Region derived from the postal code of the place of care (PO 03:39 row)

- **Personal?** Yes, in combination: a postal code on a referral or case of an identifiable client is personal data (DSG Art. 5 lit. a, as cited), and it sits in a record that already concerns health (care need; D-9 treats the referral as sensitive). It adds a location fact; it is not itself health data.
- **Minimal and necessary?** On DSG Art. 6 Abs. 2-3 (as cited): yes, **if** the postal code is the one NAVORA already needs for delivering care (the place of care), and nothing new is collected for the derivation. Deriving the region from it is then **more** protective than a free choice, because it removes a silent mis-routing (patient safety) without a new datum.
- **Protective design (compliant under every plausible interpretation, Phase A; real data subject to D-9 and D-4, OPEN):**
  - **(P-a)** Use only the postal code of the place of care already captured; no extra field, no home address when the place of care differs.
  - **(P-b)** Store the derived region and the directory version with the case; the postal code keeps the retention of the address it belongs to (D-4), no separate copy.
  - **(P-c)** Teams that have not committed see the region, never the postal code or address (R-5, (C-a)).
  - **(P-d)** Measures and exports report at region level; never per postal code with small counts (a postal code with age, care type and start date can identify a person in a small municipality).
  - **(P-e)** Unknown or out-of-pilot codes go to «Ausserhalb der Region» or Operations, as the row says; the audit row of such a routing holds from, to, who, when, as in #1164.
- **UNKNOWN, not mine:** the licence and update terms of the postal-code directory (`eng`, as the row says). If the terms restrict use or redistribution, that is a contract question for the owner before Phase B, not for Phase A.
