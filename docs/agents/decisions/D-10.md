# D-10 — Countersignature of care-assistant visit reports

## Decision summary
Must a registered nurse (RN) countersign every visit report when a care assistant performed the visit, and may a countersigner return a report to its performer unsigned, with a reason? Today every such report is countersigned (safe default, #35). The PO's first triage: a countersignature rule is a clinical rule (documentation and supervision of delegated care), so it needs the Clinical Owner; the package prepares a risk-based option for approval. **Preliminary route: `clinical`.** Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory, Legal & Compliance.

## Context and problem statement
- **IMPLEMENTED:** F6 (accepted 2026-10-23 15:25): a report performed by a care assistant needs a countersignature by an RN of the team before it is `final` (#35). Since 0040 a report awaiting countersignature cannot be changed; `correct_report` accepts only `final`, so a countersigner who finds a wrong time must countersign and then open a correction (PO 2026-10-23 08:20). Care assistants cannot start category B or needs-assessment visits (#32), so D-10 concerns A/C work.
- **What it blocks:** only a relaxation of F6's default (F6 is accepted). Challenge-review row "A countersignature for every care-assistant visit": doubtful, costs an RN's time on every C visit.
- **Safe default in force:** every care-assistant report countersigned by an RN of the team; countersign, then correct (no silent change).
- **Care-model relevance:** value 1 (direct care time) and 5 (administration) against patient safety and accountability for delegated care. The PO does not know the RN time spent per countersignature: UNKNOWN; AN2 could measure it (signature events).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Every care-assistant report countersigned (today) | simplest; safest documentation trail | RN time on every A/C visit; countersigning becomes a rubber stamp (attention dilutes) |
| B | Risk-based: countersign the first visits of each care assistant on a case, every report with an observation, a quality event or a time correction, and a random sample of the rest | RN attention where risk is; less administration | the "first visits" count, the sample share and the triggers are clinical rules (Clinical Owner); needs evidence |
| C | No countersignature for A/C; RN review only on a signal (observation, quality event) | least administration | supervision of delegated care may be required by cantonal or contract rules (`lca`); weakest trail |
| R (sub-question) | A countersigner may return a report to its performer unsigned with a reason; the performer corrects and signs again | avoids "countersign, then correct"; a cleaner record | a new state in F6; who signs when the performer has left (link to D-30) |

## Clinical impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (po).* Asked: hazards of B and C (missed observation, unsupervised delegated care), which triggers and counts are clinical rules, whether R is safer than "countersign, then correct", and what only the Clinical Owner can approve.

**cra 2026-10-24 18:26 (copied from `reports/CLINICAL_GOVERNANCE.md` by po; source: open PR #306, branch `gov/review-2026-10-24-1826`, not yet on `recovery/main`).** Advisory (D-27); not a licensed clinician.

- **What the countersignature controls.** It is the trace that a registered nurse supervised delegated A/C care. It also marks the point from which a report counts as documentation and as a billing basis (`report_not_final`, 0043:391). The risks it controls: a missed or wrongly recorded observation, care outside the care assistant's competence (CR-010, D-29), and wrong times billed.
- **Hazards of B (risk-based).** (1) An observation the care assistant does not recognise as relevant is never flagged, so a trigger "report with an observation" misses the case that matters. (2) The first-visits count, the sample share and the triggers are clinical rules (Clinical Owner); none is set here. (3) The supervision must be shown afterwards: the sample must be drawn reproducibly and stored (as `lca` and Engineering say). B is not a pilot option; I agree with the PO.
- **Hazards of C (review only on a signal).** Supervision depends on the supervised person's own report. That is no supervision of care work done without professional independence. Not protective; I agree with the PO and `lca`.
- **Is R safer than "countersign, then correct"? Yes**, on three points. (1) *Documentation integrity:* today a countersigner who sees a wrong time must countersign a version she knows is wrong. That version becomes `final`, and a billing fact is built from it (J5, 0043). It stays the billing basis until the performer signs a correction and it is countersigned again (0044: only the performer signs a correction). R keeps the wrong version out of `final`. (2) *Billing:* under R a disputed report never becomes a billing basis; under today's path it is one for a time. (3) *Meaning:* the countersignature again means "checked and agreed" (C-7).
- **Hazards R adds, and the controls it needs** (testable): (a) a returned report can be stranded when the performer is off or has left. It must stay visible as open until `final`, and `report_not_final` must keep raising (CR-053), so nothing is lost silently. pgTAP: returned report → in the performer's list and the team queue; `report_not_final` present. The "performer left" case follows D-30. (b) A return reopens the visit's facts to the performer (facts are locked only while `signed`/`final`, 0040). An observation the team already saw in HAND1 (0033:144-151 reads observations directly) must change only by a superseding row, never in place. pgTAP: after a return, a changed observation → a new row with `supersedes_id`; the earlier one still readable. (c) The returned version, reason, returner and time are append-only (`lca` condition a). (d) The reason is free text written by a nurse about care. It is health data: visible to the performer and the team's registered nurses only, never in a push, AN1/AN2 or an export.
- **Classification (reviewer's view, not a decision):** R changes no clinical rule. Who must countersign (CR-011) and what `final` means stay the same. R adds a workflow step that keeps an unchecked version out of `final`. In my reading it is a documentation-integrity workflow inside CR-011/CR-043 that the PO may decide under (a)-(d). The Clinical Owner should be informed, not asked to approve. Whether every care-assistant report must be countersigned at all (A vs. B) stays `clinical` (with the licence holder's supervision concept, D-12).
- **Precondition for every option:** D-29's real-data gate. A countersignature by an unverified `registered_nurse` label attests nothing (H-04).
- **Evidence to collect (AN2):** countersignature latency (`signed_at` → `countersigned_at`), the share corrected after `final`, and with R the share returned. RN minutes per countersignature are not recorded (UNKNOWN).

**Settled (on the sources read)**
- No source read requires a registered nurse to **countersign** each report of a care assistant: not federal law ([K1], [S1]), not [Z1], not [G1]. A countersignature is an organisational control that the licence holder chooses.
- What the sources do require is **supervision of delegated care under the responsibility of a qualified nurse**: tasks only within the staff member's training, delegation "under the responsibility" of the competent nurse, supervision of staff who are not professionally independent ([Z1] Ziff. 5.2, 5.4); a nurse available for guidance during care hours ([G1]). The responsibility sits with the licence holder's responsible head of nursing ([Z1] Ziff. 5.1; [G1]). **How** that supervision is done (countersignature, sampling, review on a signal) is not prescribed in what I read.
- **Attribution:** every entry must show who made it ([G1]); a record kept for at least ten years ([G1], one canton, 2017). F6 versioning keeps author and countersigner (CR-043, IMPLEMENTED per the registers).
- **Billing:** no source read makes a nurse's countersignature a precondition of OKP billing for A or C services performed by a care assistant. The federal condition read is trained staff for the field of activity ([L8] quoting KVV Art. 51) and a needs assessment as the basis (Art. 8, [S1]).

**UNKNOWN**

- Whether the administrative or tariff contract the organisation signs (D-18) requires documentation to be validated by a nurse.
- Which A and C tasks a FaGe EFZ, FaBe or Pflegehelfer/in SRK may perform without a nurse checking the result (CR-010, D-29 qualification classes): education ordinances and the Spitex competence framework not read.
- Whether the licence holder (D-12) must lay down its delegation and supervision concept in the Betriebskonzept submitted to the canton ([Z1] Ziff. 7.3 requires a Betriebskonzept; its supervision content not read in detail).

**Options**
- **A (every care-assistant report countersigned; countersign, then correct): compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read.** It is stricter than any rule read and leaves a full attribution trail. One legal remark on "countersign, then correct": the countersigner attests a version she knows is wrong; the correction keeps both versions and their authors (CR-043), so the record stays truthful, but the countersignature's meaning ("checked and agreed") is weakened. R removes that.
- **B (risk-based: first visits, every report with an observation, quality event or time correction, a sample of the rest): unresolved: needs legal adviser and the licence holder's delegation concept (D-12).** The sources set supervision as a responsibility, not a method, so a documented risk-based scheme can plausibly meet it. Whether it does depends on (i) the canton (D-11), (ii) the contract (D-18), (iii) whether the responsible head of nursing adopts the scheme as her supervision concept, and (iv) the triggers and counts, which are clinical rules (Clinical Owner). The sample must be drawn reproducibly and recorded (who was sampled, who reviewed, when), or the supervision cannot be shown afterwards.
- **C (no countersignature; review only on a signal): unresolved: needs legal adviser; the least protective option.** Review only when the care assistant herself records an observation or event relies on the supervised person to trigger her own supervision. That is hard to reconcile with "supervision of staff who are not professionally independent" ([Z1] Ziff. 5.2) and "under the responsibility of the competent nurse" ([Z1] Ziff. 5.4) under every interpretation.
- **R (countersigner returns the report unsigned, with a reason; the performer corrects and signs again): compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read, under three conditions:** (a) the returned version, the reason, the returner and the time are kept and cannot be deleted; (b) only the performer changes the content (the countersigner does not write into another's report), so attribution stays exact ([G1]); (c) a returned report is visible as open until final (CR-053 style), so a return cannot strand a record. Because the pilot canton's documentation rule is UNKNOWN, confirm (a)-(c) against it once D-11 is answered. The case "the performer has left the team" is D-30's question, not R's.
