# D-32 — Safety-signal delivery outside the app

## Decision summary
May a P1 visit safety signal (SIG1) reach its recipients when NAVORA is not open on their device, and if so through which channel (web push, SMS, e-mail), which provider and region, and with which message content? **Decided by po 2026-10-24 09:12 (see *Recommended default*): option B, narrow variant** (web push only to Google and Microsoft push endpoints, after the official-list check), fixed text, opt-in. The extension to Safari/iOS and Firefox is a separate decision, **D-33** (`awaiting-approval: owner`, legal question 1). Earlier PO direction (product, not legal): a fixed text with no client data, delivered by web push first, with the delivery and the tap recorded in the existing SIG1 record. The PO decides if `lca` finds the chosen variant compliant under every plausible interpretation and no paid contract is needed; a paid provider contract goes to `owner`.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** SIG1 outside the app; go-live condition SIG1-8 (until an outside channel exists, the pilot plans visits only at hours in which a named person has `/field`, `/team` or OPS1 open). **Finding:** CGR-37 (open, S2; S1 if visits are planned when nobody watches): every visit safety signal is pull-only.

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):** P1 exceptions are derived in the database (OPS1, `team_visit_alerts`) and shown only when a page is loaded. **PLANNED (SIG1, approved 03:10):** a delivery row per recipient, shown in `/field` and `/team` within 60 seconds while the page is open, taken with one tap, Operations after a PROVISIONAL 15 minutes untaken. Nothing leaves the app. NAVORA stores members' e-mail addresses (ADM1 invitations); it stores no phone numbers and no device tokens. **Safe default in force:** in-app only, plus SIG1-8.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A In-app only (today's default) | SIG1 as approved; SIG1-8 stays a go-live condition | no new personal data, no processor | a P1 at 07:15 reaches nobody whose app is closed; SIG1-8 limits the hours visits can be planned |
| B Web push (browser push service) | the member opts in on her device; NAVORA stores a push subscription per device; the message is a fixed text, the tap opens the visit in NAVORA after login | no phone numbers; no SMS cost; the tap lands in the existing SIG1 flow | push is relayed by the browser vendor's push service (region and contract UNKNOWN, `lca`, `eng`); support and reliability on the members' phones UNKNOWN (`eng`); a member can switch it off |
| C SMS through a CH/EU provider | NAVORA stores a phone number per member (opt-in); fixed text | reaches every phone, no app needed | new personal data; a paid provider contract (→ `owner`); processor contract; cost per message |
| D E-mail | fixed text to the e-mail already stored | no new personal data | delivery time not suited to a 15-minute response; easily missed |
| E B, then C for an untaken signal | web push first; SMS only when the signal is still untaken after the SIG1-5 threshold | cost only when push fails | combines the questions of B and C |

**Message content in every option (PO proposal):** the fixed text "NAVORA: dringender Hinweis in deinem Team, bitte öffnen". No client name, address, visit time, signal kind or any health data in the message; everything else is read inside NAVORA after login, under the existing RLS.

## Clinical impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (po → cra).* Please state: whether a content-free text is enough to make a nurse open NAVORA in time (safety benefit versus alert fatigue); the hazard of a team relying on a channel that silently fails (should SIG1-8 stay until delivery is shown to work, e.g. by AN2 measure 10?); what only the Clinical Owner must approve (response duties stay D-28).

**cra 2026-10-24 06:30 (copied from `reports/CLINICAL_GOVERNANCE.md` by po, verbatim, from open PR #259 at `98541a0`). Confirmed `cra` input; advisory, not a clinical rule (D-27, G-1).**

- **Is a content-free text enough?** It is enough to make a member open NAVORA. It does not let her judge urgency without opening it. That is acceptable, because the only thing that leaves the app is a P1, which is urgent by definition. The larger hazard is **alert fatigue**: SIG1-1 sends to every active member of the team, so outside the app every member's phone rings for every P1, including members off duty. Clinically safer:
  - outside the app, first the responsible member and the members on duty;
  - the whole team only when nobody has taken it after the SIG1-5 threshold;
  - Operations as today.

  Who is "on duty" has no data today (PO 05:15: no shift data). The time steps are D-28 (`clinical`).
- **Hazard of a channel that fails silently:** a team that believes it will be called, but whose push is off (permission revoked, browser or OS restrictions; `eng` to confirm iOS and Android behaviour), is less safe than a team that knows it must look. Clinical position:
  - **SIG1-8 stays a go-live condition until delivery is shown to work**: a test delivery when a member opts in, and a per-member indication in `/team` of whether outside delivery is active;
  - SIG1-6 and AN2 measure 10 show the share of P1s taken and the time to taking. The threshold for "works" is for the Clinical Owner.
- **Clinical Owner must approve:** response duties and times (D-28); whether off-duty members are ever called; the evidence threshold that ends SIG1-8. Provider, region and contract are `lca`/`owner`.

**(1) Does a content-free fixed text disclose health data?**
- Settled [P1]: health data are sensitive personal data (Art. 5 lit. c Ziff. 2).
- Reading: the proposed text ("NAVORA: dringender Hinweis in deinem Team, bitte öffnen") names no client, place, time or signal kind. On its own it identifies no person whose health it could concern. It does tell the recipient (and anyone who sees her lock screen) that she works in a NAVORA team and that something urgent is open. That is personal data about the member, not health data about a client. I found no source suggesting otherwise. **Condition:** the text must stay fixed. A variable part (a client initial, a street, a visit time, "Sturz") would change this reading.
- Settled [P3] for web push (B): the payload is end-to-end encrypted between NAVORA's server and the browser. The push service cannot read it, but "the timing and length of communication cannot be hidden from the push service". It also "will see which application server is talking to which user agent and the subscription that is used". So the push vendor learns that a NAVORA server sent a message to this device at this time. It learns nothing about the content.

**(2) Processor, disclosure abroad, information, records, DSFA**
- Settled [P1]: a processor may process only as the controller itself may, and only if no statutory or contractual secrecy duty forbids the transfer (Art. 9 Abs. 1). The controller must make sure the processor can ensure data security (Art. 9 Abs. 2). Sub-processors need the controller's prior approval (Art. 9 Abs. 3). Disclosure abroad is allowed where the Federal Council has found the protection adequate (Art. 16 Abs. 1). For the USA that covers only certified companies [P2]. Members must be informed appropriately when their data (push subscription, phone number) are collected (Art. 19 Abs. 1-2). Controllers keep records of processing (Art. 12 Abs. 1), with exemptions for companies under 250 staff whose processing carries a low risk (Art. 12 Abs. 5). A DSFA is needed where processing may bring a high risk, notably for extensive processing of sensitive data (Art. 22 Abs. 1-2).
- Settled [P5]: an employer may process employee data only as far as they concern suitability for the job or are needed to perform the employment contract (OR Art. 328b).
- Reading for each option:
  - **B (web push):** new personal data per member (the push subscription). The push service is chosen by the member's browser, not by NAVORA. Whether the browser vendor is then NAVORA's processor or a third party is not settled in the sources read. On either reading the data cross the border unless the vendor is in Switzerland or an adequate state: Google (Chrome, Android) and Apple (Safari, iOS) are US companies, so [P2] applies only if they are DPF-certified. **That is UNKNOWN (list not read).** The disclosed data are small (endpoint, time, encrypted blob) and contain no health data (see 1).
  - **C (SMS):** new personal data (phone number); a processor contract under Art. 9 with a CH/EU provider; the SMS text crosses the carrier networks unencrypted, so the fixed-text rule is essential; a paid contract (→ `owner`).
  - **D (e-mail):** no new data, but the address was collected for invitations (ADM1). Using it for alerts is a new purpose that members must be told about (Art. 19; purpose limitation, DSG Art. 6 Abs. 3, as read for D-29). The sending service is a processor (Art. 9); provider and region UNKNOWN (`eng`). E-mail content is often not encrypted in transit, so here too the fixed text is essential.
  - **Records and DSFA:** these are platform-level duties. NAVORA as a whole processes health data at scale, so the Art. 12 Abs. 5 exemption is unlikely to cover it (reading, DSV not read) and a DSFA under Art. 22 is likely needed for NAVORA as a whole (D-3, D-9). The delivery channel adds one entry to both; it does not create the duty on its own.

**(3) Employment law: messages outside working time**
- Settled [P4]: Pikettdienst is readiness for possible assignments in addition to normal work (ArGV 1 Art. 14). In a four-week period a person may be on Pikett on at most seven days, followed by two weeks without (Art. 14 Abs. 2), with an exception of up to 14 days (Abs. 3). Outside the premises only actual assignments and travel count as working time (Art. 15 Abs. 2). **If an assignment requires the person to be ready in less than 15 minutes, the cantonal authority decides whether this is Pikett or normal working time** (ArG Art. 41 Abs. 3 with ArGV 1 Art. 15, per [P4]).
- Reading: a message alone is not Pikett. Expecting a member to react to it outside her planned working time is. SIG1-5 sends a signal to Operations after a PROVISIONAL 15 minutes untaken, so a team that has to react within 15 minutes would fall in the band where the canton decides.
- Protective reading: send outside-app messages only to recipients **within their planned working time** (the responsible member on her shift, the team's members on duty, Operations). If the organisation wants members to be reachable outside their shifts, that is a Pikett arrangement under ArGV 1 Art. 14-15: planned, limited and paid under the employment contract. NAVORA does not decide it. A member who opts in to messages outside her shift is not thereby on Pikett, but nobody may rely on her answering (`cra`: a channel that silently fails).
- UNKNOWN: whether ArG applies to the pilot employer (public-law employers are partly exempt; ArG Art. 2 not read; D-12); the pilot canton's practice under ArG Art. 41 Abs. 3 (D-11); the employment model of the members (D-12, D-23).

**Options against the sources read**
- **A (in-app only, SIG1-8 stays): compliant under every plausible interpretation.** No new personal data, no processor, no message outside the app. Its limit is clinical and operational (CGR-37), not legal.
- **B (web push, fixed text, opt-in, only to recipients within their planned working time, members informed, entry in the records of processing): unresolved: needs owner (a fact check, no lawyer needed if it comes out positive).** The only open point is the disclosure abroad to the push vendors. **Missing facts and who supplies them:** whether the push services of the browsers the pilot members use (Chrome/Android, Safari/iOS, Firefox) are run by companies certified under the Swiss-U.S. DPF or located in an adequate state (owner or `eng`, from the DPF list and the vendors' terms); the sending provider for D or C and its region (`eng`, owner for a contract); the members' planned working times and whether the employer runs Pikett (D-12, D-23); the pilot canton (D-11).

**Exact questions for a legal adviser** (only if the fact check is negative, or if members are to be reached outside their shifts):
1. Is sending a web-push message (end-to-end encrypted payload, RFC 8291) through a US push service that is not DPF-certified a disclosure abroad under DSG Art. 16, given that the endpoint and the time are visible to it? If so, which safeguard under Art. 16 Abs. 2 fits?
