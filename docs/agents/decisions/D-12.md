# D-12 — Legal entity for the cantonal operating licence and the OKP admission

## Decision summary
Which legal entity holds the cantonal operating licence for home care and the admission to bill the mandatory health insurance (OKP): NAVORA itself as a licensed home-care organisation, an existing licensed partner organisation that runs NAVORA, or the teams as their own organisations (or independent nurses) with NAVORA as their platform. **No recommendation yet.** This is a regulatory-liability and strategic decision: it stays with the `owner` whatever the inputs say. The package prepares the options so the owner answers one question.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** every invoice (with D-18 and D-22); the choice of tariff and administrative contract (D-18: the contract follows the admitted provider and its association membership, per `lca` on D-22); quality and licensing duties (D-25 for nursing homes, D-16); employment model and working-time records (D-23); the platform fee model (D-14). **Findings:** none directly; CGR-31 depends on it through D-18.

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):** an `organization` in the data model with teams and members; J5 derives billable facts and an estimate; nothing is invoiced; no entity, licence number, ZSR or GLN of a provider is stored. **Safe default in force:** nothing is invoiced; data are synthetic.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A NAVORA operates as a licensed home-care organisation | NAVORA employs or contracts the nurses, holds the licence and the ZSR, signs the contracts | one accountable entity; fastest path to a pilot with own teams | NAVORA carries provider liability and quality duties; employer obligations |
| B Partner organisation holds licence and admission | an existing licensed Spitex organisation runs its teams on NAVORA; NAVORA is the software and model | no licence needed by NAVORA; existing contracts | the care model depends on the partner's governance; less autonomy for teams |
| C Teams as their own organisations | each team (or a cooperative of teams) is a licensed organisation; NAVORA is the platform | maximum team autonomy and nurse income | licensing per team is heavy; quality duties per team; slowest |
| D Independent nurses (freiberuflich) with OKP admission, organised in NAVORA teams | each nurse bills under her own admission; NAVORA coordinates | income goes to the nurse | coordination and liability spread; different tariff rules may apply (UNKNOWN) |

**What every option has in common (settled on the sources read)**
- OKP billing needs an **admission by the canton where the care is given**, by contestable cantonal ruling, since 1 January 2022. A further canton needs its own procedure ([E1], KVG Art. 36).
- A home-care organisation needs a **cantonal operating licence** and **the required professional staff** (KVV Art. 51 lit. a and c, as quoted in [E1], [E2]). It must meet the **basic quality requirements** of KVV Art. 58g ([E1], [E2]) and comply with the **quality contracts** under KVG Art. 58a, whether or not it belongs to an association ([E1]); the home-care contract is not yet approved ([E6]).
- The tariff and administrative contract follows the admitted provider and its association membership (D-22 [S3], [S4]; D-18).
- Canton-specific (example [E2], Thurgau): an approved *Bereichsleitung Pflege* (and a deputy) meeting KVV Art. 49 lit. a, b and GesBG Art. 12; minimum staffing (in Thurgau 230 % of HF/FH nurses to cover 07:00-19:00 every day, a nurse on site within 45 minutes, a Bereichsleitung of at least 50 %); operator liability insurance of at least CHF 5 million per case; an interRAI HC contract; palliative-care qualifications; concepts (hygiene, data protection, complaints, abuse). **Whether the pilot canton sets similar thresholds is UNKNOWN (D-11).** They decide whether small teams can be licensed at all.

**Option A: NAVORA operates as a licensed home-care organisation**
- Requires: everything in the common block, held by NAVORA (licence, admission, approved nursing management, staff, quality system, insurance, contracts). NAVORA is the controller of the health data under the DSG.
- Liabilities on NAVORA (reading; liability law not read): provider liability to clients and payers; employer duties (ArG, OR Art. 328b, as read for D-32); the cantonal licence conditions; the accuracy of every invoice.
- Canton-specific: the licence, the staffing thresholds, the residual financing.

**Option B: a licensed partner organisation runs its teams on NAVORA**
- Requires of NAVORA: no licence or admission. NAVORA processes health data for the partner, so it is the partner's **processor** (DSG Art. 9 [E5]). Processing is allowed only as the partner may do it itself, and **only if no statutory or contractual secrecy duty forbids the transfer** (Art. 9 Abs. 1 lit. b). Sub-processors (hosting, e-mail) need the partner's prior approval (Abs. 3). The secrecy condition touches professional secrecy (StGB Art. 321, not read). Whether a health professional may entrust client data to an IT provider without each client's consent is the **main open legal question** of B.
- If NAVORA itself employed the nurses and placed them with the partner, that would be staff hiring (*Personalverleih*): placing employees with client firms on a commercial basis needs a cantonal licence, and a federal one from SECO across the border ([E4]; AVG articles not read). Pure software with the partner as employer does not raise this.
- Liabilities on NAVORA (reading): contractual (software, availability, data security), not provider liability. The care model depends on the partner's governance and its approved nursing management (see D-16).

**Option C: teams as their own licensed organisations (or a cooperative of teams)**
- Requires of each team or the cooperative: everything in the common block. Each needs its own licence and admission in each canton, an approved nursing management, the quality system and insurance. Thresholds like the Thurgau minimum staffing [E2] could make a small team unlicensable. In a cooperative the cooperative would hold the licence (reading).
- NAVORA: the processor of each organisation (as in B), with the same secrecy question.

**Option D: independent nurses with their own OKP admission, organised in NAVORA teams**
- Settled (cantonal example [E3]): a licence to practise "in eigener fachlicher Verantwortung" (GesBG Art. 12); an HF or FH/UH nursing diploma (foreign diplomas recognised by the SRK); NAREG entry for FH degrees; liability insurance (recommended CHF 5 million); a criminal-record extract. For OKP admission additionally: **two years of practice** under an admitted nurse or in an institution or home-care organisation led by a qualified nurse; **working independently and on own account**; the KVV Art. 58g quality requirements.
- Consequence (reading): "on own account" conflicts with NAVORA directing the work, setting the schedule or collecting the fees. If NAVORA did that, the nurses could be found to be employees (social insurance, labour law), or NAVORA could be found to be hiring out staff ([E4]). Neither was analysed (AHVG, AVG not read).
- UNKNOWN: which tariff and administrative contract applies to independent nurses (the contracts read are for organisations of Spitex Schweiz/ASPS); whether independent nurses may form a team with shared cases and cover without the team itself needing a licence.

**Verdict:** no option can be called "compliant under every plausible interpretation" before the pilot canton (D-11) is known. **Unresolved: needs owner (choice, D-11) and legal adviser.** The current state is compliant: nothing is invoiced and the data are synthetic (IMPLEMENTED).

**Exact questions for a legal adviser:**
1. (B, C, D) May a licensed home-care organisation or an independent nurse entrust client health data to NAVORA as a processor (DSG Art. 9) without each client's consent, given professional secrecy (StGB Art. 321) and the pilot canton's health law? Which contractual safeguards are needed?
2. (A) In the pilot canton, what are the licence conditions for a new home-care organisation: approved nursing management, minimum staffing and hours, liability insurance, concepts? Can a newly founded organisation get admitted without its own track record?
3. (C) Can a cooperative of self-organised teams hold one licence and admission, with each team working under the cooperative's approved nursing management?
4. (D) Under which conditions does an independent nurse working in a NAVORA team (shared cases, cover, platform fee) still work "independently and on own account" for KVV purposes and for social insurance? At what point does NAVORA's role become employment or staff hiring (AVG)?
5. (all) Which administrative and tariff contract applies to each option (organisation vs. independent nurse; association membership), and does it change the order and billing rules read for D-22 and D-18?

**Note to po:** (1) Option B is the only one that needs no licence by NAVORA, but question 1 (processor and professional secrecy) applies to B, C and D alike. It is worth asking first because it decides whether NAVORA can host real client data for anyone else. (2) The pilot canton's staffing thresholds (see the Thurgau example) may decide between C and A/B more than any legal principle; D-11 should collect them. (3) The PO's sequence idea (pilot entity first, team ownership later) is not excluded by any source read, but each change of entity means a new licence, a new admission and possibly a new contract (KVG Art. 36 per [E1]).

**Question 1 (processor under professional secrecy): new evidence, still not settled.**
- [E8]: nurses and **their auxiliary persons** (*Hilfspersonen*) are bound by the criminal-law secrecy; *Hilfspersonen* are "Personen, welche die Fachpersonen bei der Erfüllung ihrer Aufgabe unterstützen"; within the treating team consent is presumed. [E8] does not mention IT providers.
- [E7]: on the authors' reading of Swiss case law and prevailing doctrine, an IT or cloud provider is a *Hilfsperson* of the secret-holder, and "Hilfspersonen sind nie unberechtigte Dritte im Sinn von Art. 321 StGB" (p. 32). Conditions they name: careful selection, instruction and supervision (p. 29); a contractual confidentiality duty and use of the data only to perform the contract (p. 32); for a provider abroad, a case-by-case risk assessment including access by foreign authorities (p. 30). Client consent is "not strictly required" but recommended as an additional safeguard (p. 30). **The view is contested:** a narrower view (Wohlers) treats handing data to a provider that can decrypt them as disclosure (fn. 13, 20, 22).
- Reading (not a determination): this points towards B being workable with a processor agreement plus a confidentiality agreement, without each client's consent, **but** (i) [E7] concerns lawyers; (ii) the narrower view exists, so B is not compliant *under every plausible interpretation* on secrecy alone; (iii) the pilot canton's health law is unread; (iv) NAVORA's own sub-processors (hosting, e-mail) and their country are not decided (T12, ADR-011), and [E7] makes a provider abroad a separate risk assessment.
- **Question 1, narrowed for the adviser:** "Does the *Hilfsperson* reading of [E7] apply to an IT provider of a licensed home-care organisation (and of its nurses) in the pilot canton? Is information of the client in the care contract enough, or is consent needed? Which hosting countries and sub-processors are acceptable, and which clauses must the processor and confidentiality agreement contain?"

**The PO's three partnership conditions (product conditions, read against the sources):**
1. *Teams decide acceptance, planning, cover and absence; no dispatch or approval layer added by the partner.* In B the partner holds the licence and admission (KVV Art. 51, as quoted in [E1], [E2]). Its approved nursing management signs that the requirements are met from licensing onward ([E2]; D-16 [Q2]), and it carries the basic quality requirements (KVV Art. 58g, [E1]). A clause that **excludes** the licence holder's nursing management from intervening may conflict with duties the licence gives it. **Unresolved: needs legal adviser** (with D-11) as worded. A wording consistent with every source read: *teams decide by default in NAVORA; the partner adds no standing approval step; the approved nursing management keeps its non-delegable duties (supervision, quality, serious events, D-16) and may intervene in an individual case; every intervention is recorded and visible to the team.* Whether the pilot canton lets a nursing management leave acceptance and planning to teams: UNKNOWN (D-11).
2. *Pilot measures and challenge rows shared with the partner and the teams.* Team-level, aggregated measures without client data raise no question in the sources read. Figures per member are the staff member's personal data (OR Art. 328b and DSG, as read for D-32 and D-4); sharing them with colleagues or the partner needs a purpose tied to the employment and proportionality. **Consistent with every source read if** measures are aggregated per team, contain no client data, and per-member figures go only to the member and to roles that need them; otherwise unresolved.
3. *A path to C: teams may form a cooperative and take over the licence after the pilot.* On the sources read a licence and an admission are not transferred: a new entity needs its own licence and its own admission (KVG Art. 36 per [E1]; my 04:50 note 3). Clients and their records would move to a new provider; that is a disclosure that needs each client's consent (D-9, BE Leitfaden 3.2.3 as read). So "take over the licence" is not possible as such; "the partner will support a new licence application of the cooperative and the transfer of clients who consent" is consistent with every source read.

If NAVORA also uses pilot data for **its own** purposes (product measures across organisations, hypotheses, D-14 fees), it is, on my reading, a controller for that use. That needs its own basis and, for health data, anonymisation or the client's consent. Which measures in AN1/AN2 NAVORA itself would read across partners: UNKNOWN to `lca` (`eng`/`po`).

## Recommended default for NAVORA
**PO 2026-10-24 09:12 (escalated, `awaiting-approval: owner`). Recommendation: B for the pilot (a licensed partner organisation holds the licence and OKP admission and runs its teams on NAVORA), with three conditions written into the partnership; A only if no partner is found in the pilot canton; C (a cooperative of teams) as the stated target after the pilot.**
- *Why B:* it is the fastest way to a first billed visit without NAVORA carrying provider liability, and the cheapest in the code (Engineering impact: fits the model as built). It lets the pilot test the care model, not the licensing process. A needs NAVORA to become a care provider (nursing management, employer duties) before anything is proven; C and D are slow and costly before the model is shown to work (D: per-nurse billing; C per team: cross-organisation cover and matching).
- *The risk of B is governance:* a partner's hierarchy can undo team autonomy. So the partnership must state, as conditions (product, owner to negotiate): (1) teams decide acceptance, planning, cover and absence handling in NAVORA as built (J2, PLAN2, COVER1, ABS1), with no dispatch or approval layer added by the partner; (2) the pilot measures (AN1, AN2) and the challenge rows are shared with the partner and the teams; (3) a path to C: the partner agrees that the teams may form a cooperative and take over the licence after the pilot if the measures are met.
- *First legal question regardless of option:* `lca` question 1 (may a provider entrust client health data to NAVORA as a processor under professional secrecy, StGB Art. 321, and cantonal health law) applies to B, C and D; it decides whether NAVORA can host real client data for anyone else. Ask it with the canton (D-11).
- **Asked of the owner:** "approve B for the pilot (with the three conditions), or choose A, C or D", and name the partner or say none is in view. **Until then:** nothing is invoiced; synthetic data only.

Earlier: Pending inputs. **PO view (product, not legal):** by the value order (nurse income, team autonomy, less administration), C or D fits the care model best in the long run; for a first pilot, A or B is faster.
