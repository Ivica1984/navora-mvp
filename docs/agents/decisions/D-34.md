# D-34 — Is NAVORA a medical device (MepV)?

## Decision summary
Whether NAVORA, as its manufacturer would state its **intended purpose**, is software that the Swiss Medical Devices Ordinance (MepV) treats as a medical device, and if so what every release must document. Raised by `lca` on 2026-10-24 12:55 in the D-8 package (no row existed). The PO's first triage: the intended purpose is a manufacturer's statement with regulatory liability, so the final answer is **`owner`**, after a regulatory adviser; but the *design line* that keeps NAVORA on one side of it is a product choice the PO can recommend now. Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory, Legal & Compliance. **PO working view, not a decision:** state now an intended purpose that keeps NAVORA an organisation, communication and documentation tool for care teams; keep every feature inside it; have it assessed before any real client; and build any future feature that interprets a client's clinical information (AI assessment drafts, interRAI outputs) as a separately qualified module.

## Context and problem statement
- **Source (advisory, `lca` 12:55, D-8 [S4]):** Kanton Zürich, *Besonderheiten bei Medizinprodukten* (30 January 2026, an orientation page, not the MepV): software is a medical device when its intended purpose, set by the manufacturer, is medical for individual persons (diagnosis, prevention, **monitoring**, prognosis, treatment); software limited to storage, archiving, simple search or communication generally is not; software that **summarises, prioritises or interprets clinical information** is a grey zone. The MepV text and Swissmedic guidance were **not read**: UNKNOWN.
- **IMPLEMENTED features near that line (repository, `recovery/main`):**
  - CSR1 / OPS1 exceptions and SIG1 safety signals (0060): raised from **visit and schedule facts** (a visit overdue, stuck, not opened, uncovered, an order expiring), ranked P1-P3 and routed to people. They do not read a client's clinical state.
  - QG1 quality events and hold (team-recorded events; the hold is a billing block).
  - ORD0 (0061): "Verordnung ohne Enddatum", "Hausärztin/Hausarzt informieren": administrative facts about the order.
  - J1 safety alerts before the first visit: D-17 recommendation "nothing diagnostic".
- **PLANNED or proposed features further toward the line:** challenge-review rows "Intake with AI extraction" (Gate 4 hypothesis), "Primary nurse confirms an AI draft built from continuous evidence" (nursing homes, NH2/NH3), interRAI HC/LTCF assessments and their outputs (D-13, D-24), "NAVORA proposes a care-plan review" (hypothesis), SIG2 (push of the SIG1 signals).
- **Why it matters for the care model:** the operating-system strategy (assessment from continuous evidence, AI drafts the nurse confirms, signals instead of coordinators) moves NAVORA toward "interprets clinical information". If that makes the whole platform a medical device, every release carries device documentation and conformity duties, and the speed of self-organised improvement drops. Deciding the boundary early keeps the core free and puts the clinical-interpretation features where they can be qualified properly.
- **Safe default in force (PO, product authority, until answered):** no feature is built that interprets a client's clinical information to suggest a clinical action (diagnosis, risk score, treatment proposal); signals point to a visit and a person; D-17 stays "nothing diagnostic". Nothing is released to real clients before the adviser's assessment (with D-1, D-3, D-9, D-29 real-data gates).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Intended purpose stated as organisation, communication and documentation of care by teams (no medical purpose for an individual); every feature stays inside it; a regulatory adviser assesses the statement and the feature list before any real client | keeps the core free of device duties if confirmed; cheap now | limits what the core may do (no clinical scoring or recommendations) |
| B | Treat NAVORA as a medical device now: quality system, technical documentation, classification, conformity route | maximal certainty for later clinical features | large fixed cost before the pilot; slows every release; may be unnecessary for the core |
| C | A for the core, plus a rule: any future feature that interprets clinical information (AI assessment drafts, interRAI outputs, risk scores) is a separate module with its own intended purpose and qualification before it ships | keeps the core fast and the clinical ambition possible | two products to govern; the boundary must be enforced in review |
| D | Defer until the pilot partner or a real client | nothing now | the question returns at go-live, when changing features is most expensive |
