# D-25 — Nursing-home leadership licensing rules

## Decision summary
What do the pilot canton's operating-licence rules require of nursing-home leadership (a named home director, a nursing-care lead, ward leadership, their qualifications, staffing ratios or a qualification mix), and does a self-organised ward team without a ward leader, with a network clinical lead, satisfy them? The PO assumes no rule. The PO's first triage: this is a question of cantonal law for a horizon that is not being built (D-24 working view A: not now), so it is tier 4 and cannot be answered before D-11 names the canton. The package fixes the design question so that the answer, when it comes, can be applied without a new round: which roles NAVORA treats as **accountable roles the licence requires** (kept, named, never removed by software) and which as **operational layers** the teams can absorb. **Preliminary route: `legal`** (cantonal law; no legal adviser named), then `owner` if a pilot needs a cantonal permit. Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory, Legal & Compliance.

## Context and problem statement
- **Source in the repository:** `docs/product/NURSING_HOME_TRANSFORMATION.md` (po, 2026-10-22, PLANNED). It reads the interRAI LTCF CH handbook as an organisation chart: up to five levels above the nurse at the bedside in a 96-bed home; the home director (*Heimleitung*) kept "as an accountable licence holder" and the head of care (*Leitung Betreuung und Pflege*) kept as a function, both with "cantonal requirements UNKNOWN, D-25"; ward leadership is UNKNOWN in the source. Proposals NH0 (ward-team pilot), NH1 (shift self-planning) and NH8 (network expert pool) are gated by D-25.
- **What it blocks:** NH0, NH1 and NH8; stage "1. Organise" of the transformation path (one ward self-organised, the ward-leader role suspended for the pilot). Nothing that is built or approved.
- **Related decisions:** D-24 (enter nursing homes at all; working view A now, B one ward after D-11, D-12, one pilot month and D-25), D-11 (canton), D-12 (who holds the licence), D-23 (working-time record; 24-hour rosters raise rest-period and night-work questions), D-13 (instrument).
- **What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):** nothing nursing-home specific. Teams, members with a qualification label (D-29 verification open), PLAN2 recurring planning, COVER1 cover offers (switched off), QG1 quality events. No ward, shift, resident or roster entity.
- **The home-care analogue already read by `lca`** (D-10, 11:55, ZH Spitex leaflet of November 2023): one responsible head of nursing with a practice licence and management experience carries responsibility for nursing matters; staff who are not professionally independent are supervised. That is a home-care source and says nothing about nursing homes; it shows the shape of the question only.
- **Safe default in force:** no nursing-home feature is built; any role the licence may require is treated as required until `lca` and, if needed, a legal adviser say otherwise.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | No ward pilot until the pilot canton's rules are known (today) | assumes nothing | Horizon 3 waits on a legal answer that itself waits on D-11 |
| B | **Accountable roles kept, operational layer removed:** every role the licence names (licence holder or home director, nursing-care lead, any qualification it requires) stays named and accountable; only ward leadership, if the canton does not name it, is replaced by the self-organised ward team, with a named RN per shift; any staffing ratio or qualification mix becomes a hard constraint of the NH1 roster | keeps the licence intact; removes the layer with the most coordination work; the roster enforces the mix instead of a ward leader | needs the canton's text; if ward leadership is named, B shrinks to "the ward leader as a team member with the accountability, not the scheduling" |
| C | B plus a **network nursing-care lead** shared across homes instead of one per home | removes a second layer; scales | only if the canton allows a shared or part-time nursing lead; the heaviest legal question |
| D | Ask the canton for a pilot permit or conditions for one ward (*Bewilligung mit Auflagen* or a pilot arrangement) | the canton itself answers; evidence for others | a commercial and regulatory step (owner); time |

## Engineering impact
*requested 2026-10-24 18:12 (po).* Asked: (1) confirm nothing nursing-home specific is built (no ward, shift, resident or roster entity); (2) whether a staffing ratio or qualification mix, once a canton names one, can be a hard constraint of a PLAN2-style roster (refuse a roster below the mix, with the rule versioned and its source recorded) and roughly what that costs; (3) whether a named accountable role (licence holder, nursing-care lead) can be represented so that no team command can remove or vacate it (as ADM1 protects the last administrator).

## Clinical impact
*requested 2026-10-24 18:12 (cra).* Asked: (1) the clinical-governance hazards of a ward without a ward leader (24-hour responsibility, medication rounds, night-shift escalation, admission fit) as a structure for the Clinical Owner, not as rules; (2) what a network clinical lead (C) must be reachable for, as hazards; (3) which of these only the named Clinical Owner can approve (D-27, G-1). Overlaps the D-24 request (16:20); one answer may serve both.

## Legal/compliance impact
*requested 2026-10-24 18:12 (lca).* Involved because it is a question of cantonal licensing law and governance. Asked, with sources and for the candidate cantons already read (ZH, BE, SO) until D-11 names one: (1) which leadership roles a nursing-home operating licence names (home director, nursing-care lead, ward leadership) and their qualifications; (2) whether a staffing ratio or a qualification mix is prescribed, and in which instrument (law, ordinance, licence condition, cantonal guideline); (3) whether a nursing-care lead may be shared across homes or part-time (option C); (4) whether a ward without a ward leader, with a named RN per shift, is compatible under every plausible interpretation (option B); (5) whether a pilot permit or licence condition is a known route (option D). Labour-law limits on 24-hour team rosters stay with D-23 or a new decision.

## Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| A ward pilot removes a role the licence requires | B keeps every named role; nothing built before `lca` and, if unresolved, a legal adviser |
| Software vacates an accountable role (for example a team removes its nursing lead) | Engineering question 3: accountable roles cannot be removed by a team command |
| A qualification mix is kept by a ward leader's judgement today and by nobody tomorrow | a prescribed mix becomes a hard roster constraint (Engineering question 2), versioned with its source |
| The answer is read for the wrong canton | D-11 first; ZH, BE, SO only as orientation, marked as such |

## Recommended default for NAVORA
Pending inputs. **PO working view, not a decision:** **A** stays in force. The design target is **B**: the licence's named roles are accountable roles and stay; the ward leader's operational work (rosters, handover, admission fit) moves to the team and the software; any prescribed mix is a hard constraint. **C** only if `lca` finds a shared nursing lead permitted for the pilot canton; **D** is an owner step if a canton requires a permit. No brief and no NH0 before D-24 moves past A.
