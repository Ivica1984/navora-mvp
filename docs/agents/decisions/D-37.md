# D-37 — interRAI HC as NAVORA's clinical foundation

## Decision summary
Owner direction 2026-10-27: *interRAI should become an important clinical foundation for care planning, continuity, reassessment and governance, within licensing and regulatory limits.*

D-13 (po-decided 2026-10-25, option B) made NAVORA **instrument-neutral**:
- the provider chooses the instrument;
- NAVORA records only that an assessment happened;
- integration (option A) was left as a later owner decision.

This package asks whether NAVORA designates **interRAI HC** as its clinical foundation in home care, and through which route.

## Context and problem statement
- **IMPLEMENTED:** nothing. D-13 B is decided but not built. NAVORA stores no interRAI item, scale, CAP or score (Clinical Rule Register, *Not in the code*).
- **Related decisions:**
  - **D-36:** licence scope; `inputs-requested`; option A in force.
  - **D-34:** intended purpose. Statement v2 excludes computed interRAI outputs from the core; computed outputs need a separately qualified module (C).
  - **D-12:** which provider and tool.
  - **D-24:** nursing homes, interRAI LTCF; a different instrument.
- **interRAI reference sections** in D-15, D-16, D-17, D-28, D-30 and D-31 (2026-10-27): interRAI informs D-15, D-16, D-17 and D-31 substantially or partly. It removes no clinical approval. All lines are UNKNOWN until verified.
- **What the owner's direction changes:** interRAI stops being "one instrument a provider might use". It becomes the instrument NAVORA's care planning, continuity, reassessment and governance features are designed around. Choosing an assessment instrument is not the same as computing its outputs: the computation (scales, CAPs) stays behind the licence and the D-34 module rule.

## Options considered

|---|---|---|---|

Assessment results enter NAVORA as **signed facts from the licensed tool**: date, assessor, the problem areas the tool reports, the assessor's own care-planning conclusions. NAVORA computes nothing until the module and licence allow it | follows the direction; no NAVORA computation (stays out of device level H); continuity and reassessment become possible; the governance aggregates come later | needs an integration agreement with the tool vendor or provider (UNKNOWN who); providers on another instrument fit less well |
| C B plus NAVORA computes scales and CAPs in a qualified clinical module | as B, and NAVORA's own module derives outputs under its own licence | full clinical foundation; independent of the provider's tool | needs an interRAI licence for NAVORA as software vendor (UNKNOWN whether granted); medical-device conformity (D-34 C); highest cost; not before horizon 2 |
| D Own instrument | NAVORA writes its own assessment | — | rejected in D-13 (no compliant route; invents clinical content) |

**Sources**
- [I1], [I2] interRAI licensing pages, read by `lca` 2026-10-27 12:42 (citations in D-36): use of forms needs a written licence; buying a manual is no licence; licences per instrument and nation; items copyrighted, "scales, algorithms, and case-mix measures … are not copyrighted"; royalty-free licences exclude incorporation into products sold or paid for by others; software vendors usually must offer sublicensing and **data export**; no logo without approval; attribution "© interRAI <year>".
- [HCD] Swiss RDL, University of Bern, "Home Care Data (HCD)" (page metadata 12 September 2023; read 2026-10-27): HCD is "the data pool of Spitex Switzerland"; it uses interRAI instruments adapted to Swiss conditions; "several IT companies" built the interRAI modules; the **certified** modules have had an interface to the SwissRDL platform since 2013. The page names no licensor, no certifying body and no vendor.
- [M2] Swissmedic Merkblatt "Medizinprodukte-Software" v3.0 (§§4, 5.1, 5.3) and [M3] MDCG 2019-11 rev.1 (§§3.3, 4.2.1, 7; Annex II example 2), read by `lca` 2026-10-24 (citations in D-34): software limited to storage, archiving, simple search, communication or lossless compression is not a device; software producing information for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is class IIa or higher under rule 11; modules must be delineated or the whole system falls under device law.
- [P1] DSG Art. 5 lit. c, Art. 6, Art. 9 (secondary text, as read 2026-10-24, citation in D-35).
- **Not read:** the owner's licence; any Swiss licence, distributor or certification terms for interRAI HCSuisse; any tool vendor's export or interface terms; the URG in the primary text; MDR Annex VIII in the primary text.

**Answers to the four questions**
1. *Does B need a licence for NAVORA itself?* **UNKNOWN.** On [I2], a licence attaches to *using* an instrument; under B NAVORA does not administer the instrument, reproduce items or compute outputs, the provider does so in its licensed tool. That points to "the provider's licence plus the vendor's export is enough". Two things on the same sources point the other way: licences are contracts and may restrict what the licensee does with outputs; and [HCD] shows a Swiss ecosystem of *certified* modules, so a Swiss licensor or certification body may set its own interface rules. Neither can be settled without the terms. The vendor's duty to "offer data export" ([I2]) supports that data leaving the tool is foreseen, but says nothing about who may receive it.
2. *Is importing reported problem areas (names, no item text) a use of licensed content?* **Copyright: on [I2] probably not**, because the items are protected and the algorithms are not, and a problem-area name with its trigger state is an output, not an item (a reading; URG not read). **Contract: UNKNOWN**, because the provider's licence may restrict passing outputs to third-party software. **Data protection: settled on [P1]:** the imported areas are health data of a named client (Art. 5 lit. c); NAVORA receives them as the provider's processor, on the provider's instruction (Art. 9), and imports only the fields a feature actually uses (Art. 6). The master record stays in the provider's tool; NAVORA's copy carries source and date (the package's own mitigation) so that it is not mistaken for the current assessment.
3. *Does C need a vendor licence?* **On [I2] very probably yes** (computing interRAI outputs inside a product sold to providers is the case the royalty-free licence excludes and for which vendor licences exist), plus possibly Swiss certification ([HCD]). Whether interRAI or a Swiss licensee would grant it is UNKNOWN. C also brings the device question: outputs that trigger care processes for an individual match [M3] Annex II example 2 and rule 11 (plausibly class IIa or higher).
4. *Interaction with D-34 statement v2 and the module rule.*
   - **Storing and showing** the tool's reported areas, losslessly, with source and date, is storage and communication ([M2] §4, [M3] §3.3): consistent with the core on the sources read.
   - **Any rule that reads them** is input class *clinical content* under the D-34 control: a significant-change prompt, "the care plan must address area X", ordering or highlighting clients by area. Such rules cannot ship in the core; they wait for the module (C) and the regulatory adviser, even under B. So B's clinical value beyond display arrives only with C.
   - **A reassessment due date** computed only from the assessment date and a fixed interval the Clinical Owner sets is date arithmetic on a recorded fact, plausibly administrative like scheduling; an interval that depends on the reported areas is clinical content. Which one the Clinical Owner sets is `cra`'s and `clinical`'s call; the class must be stated in ASM1.
   - **Wording:** "designed around interRAI HC" in product or sales texts is use-related information under the D-34 two-limb test ([M4]/[M5] in D-34). Statement v2 should name interRAI HC as the instrument whose results NAVORA *stores and shows*, not as a basis of NAVORA's own clinical function, and D-36 terms (a)-(c) apply (name only, no logo, no claim of licence or endorsement).

**Verdicts**
- **First build (ASM1: the D-13 B record with "interRAI HC" as the instrument name, date, assessor, reference; no item, score or CAP): compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read**, on D-36 terms (a)-(c).
- **B (import of reported areas from the provider's tool): unresolved: needs owner** (the licence terms and the vendor's export terms) **and, if the terms are silent, a legal adviser.** Exact question: "May a licensed provider pass the problem areas and trigger states reported by its interRAI HCSuisse tool, for named clients, to a third-party operations platform acting as its processor, and does that platform need its own licence, sublicence or certification for receiving and displaying them?"
- **C: unresolved: needs owner** (a vendor licence from interRAI or a Swiss licensee) **and a regulatory adviser** (D-34 C, rule 11).

**Missing facts and who supplies them:** the licence terms and the licensor for Switzerland (`owner`, D-36); the provider's tool and vendor and their export terms (`owner`, with the partner, D-12); whether a Swiss certification applies to interfaces (`owner`, from the licensor); the reassessment interval and its class (`clinical`, `cra`).

## Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| interRAI content copied or computed outside the licence | B imports signed facts only; nothing computed (D-34 statement v2) until C |
| Reported problem areas read as NAVORA's clinical judgement | they are shown as "aus Bedarfsabklärung vom <Datum>, <Name>"; the assessor's signature, not NAVORA, stands behind them |
| A provider without interRAI HC cannot use NAVORA | the D-13 B record stays instrument-neutral as the fallback for other instruments |
| Integration depends on one tool vendor | the import format is specified by NAVORA; any licensed tool can deliver it |
