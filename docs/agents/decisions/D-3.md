# D-3 — Read scope of health data per role (permission matrix), including the cover read

## Decision summary
Who may read which health data of a client, and for how long: (a) confirm option B (team-scoped clinical read, built) as the rule for the pilot; (b) whether a nurse who accepts a cover for another team's visit may read the visit card of that one visit (address, care goals, safety alerts, recent HAND1 entries) from acceptance until its report is final, audited, and nothing else of the case (the PO recommendation of 2026-10-22 14:30, the last step before COVER1 can be switched on); (c) when option C (purpose-based access with care relationship and break-glass) is required; (d) the final role names. **No recommendation yet beyond the PO's earlier product position.** Expected route (INDEX): the PO may decide (a) and (b) if `lca` finds them compliant under every plausible interpretation (data protection, professional secrecy) and `cra` raises no clinical-safety objection; otherwise `owner`. (c) before production likely needs `owner` (it sets the production privacy posture).

| **B Team-scoped clinical read (built)** | nurse: own teams' cases with health data; coach/operations: organisation-wide without health columns | team autonomy and minimisation; built and tested | covers between teams impossible; no break-glass |
| B+cover **B plus a bounded cover read (PO 2026-10-22)** | after `accept_cover`, the covering nurse reads that one visit's card (address, care goals, safety alerts, recent HAND1 entries) from acceptance until the report is final; every read audited; nothing else of the case; ends on withdrawal, absence (CGR-28) or final report | removes the dispatcher from cover; smallest read that makes a safe visit possible | a nurse outside the care team reads health data; legal basis and secrecy to confirm (`lca`) |
| C Purpose-based access with care relationship and break-glass | access only with an active care relationship; emergency access audited and reviewed | strongest | needs the care-relationship model and an audit review process; before production |

**Settled**
- Health data are "besonders schützenswerte Personendaten" (DSG Art. 5 lit. c Ziff. 2 [L1]). Processing must be lawful, proportionate and purpose-bound (Art. 6 Abs. 1-3). By default it is limited "auf das für den Verwendungszweck nötige Mindestmass" (Art. 7 Abs. 3). Data security must match the risk (Art. 8 Abs. 1).
- A DSFA is required in advance when processing may carry a high risk. A high risk arises in particular from "umfangreiche Bearbeitung besonders schützenswerter Personendaten" (Art. 22 Abs. 1-2 [L1]).
- Logging (DSV Art. 4 Abs. 1, 4, 5 as reproduced in [L2]): a private controller must log at least storing, changing, **reading**, disclosing, deleting and destroying when sensitive data are processed automatically "in grossem Umfang" **and** "die präventiven Massnahmen den Datenschutz nicht gewährleisten" können. A log entry names the person, the type, date and time of processing, and the recipient. Logs are kept at least one year, separate from the system, and are readable only by those who check data protection. The EDÖB does not define "grosser Umfang" [L2].
- Disclosing sensitive data to third parties is a personality violation unless justified: by consent, an overriding interest or law (DSG Art. 30 Abs. 2, Art. 31 Abs. 1 [L1]).
- Professional secrecy applies "grundsätzlich gegenüber allen Personen und Institutionen", with the patient as the only exception. Consent may be express or implied. A disclosure is limited to what is relevant and strictly necessary ([L5] sections 2.3-2.4). Hilfspersonen are bound like the principal ([L5], [L6] section 4).
- UNKNOWN, with conflicting sources: whether nurses are themselves named in StGB Art. 321 Ziff. 1. [L5] section 2.3 lists Pflegefachpersonen among the professions bound by it. [L6] lists only doctors, dentists, chiropractors, pharmacists, midwives and psychologists, and treats nurses as Hilfspersonen or under cantonal GesG. [L7] adds "Pflegeexperten APN" to Art. 321, which suggests the list already names other health professions; that is a reading, not a determination. **For D-3 this does not change the outcome:** either way the nurse is bound by secrecy.

**(a) Option B for a pilot with real clients**
- The team scoping is consistent with Art. 6 Abs. 2-3 and Art. 7 Abs. 3 [L1]: a nurse reads health data only for the cases of her teams.
- Three points are not settled:
  - (i) **Read logging.** NAVORA logs writes, not reads (package; `eng` to confirm). Whether DSV Art. 4 applies depends on "grosser Umfang" and on whether team scoping counts as a sufficient preventive measure. Both are undefined in the sources read.
  - (ii) **DSFA.** A home-care platform holding care plans, observations and visit reports plausibly is "umfangreiche Bearbeitung" of health data, so a DSFA is plausibly required before real data.
  - (iii) **What coach and operations see organisation-wide.** They read without health columns, but being a client of a home-care organisation, with a name and address, may itself reveal health data. Whether that read is proportionate for every coach and operations user is a question for the adviser.
- **Verdict (a): unresolved: needs legal adviser** (questions 1, 3, 4). **The most protective variant is B, plus read logging with the content of DSV Art. 4 Abs. 4-5, plus a DSFA before the first real client, plus coach and operations reading client identity only where their task needs it.** On the federal sources read, it is **compliant under every plausible interpretation**. Cantonal law stays UNKNOWN in two places: the pilot canton's patient-record rules, and cantonal data-protection law if the organisation acts as a public body or under a public mandate (D-11, D-12).
- While the data is synthetic (today), B raises no data-protection question: compliant under every plausible interpretation.

**(b) B+cover read**
- Reading, not a determination: a nurse employed by the same organisation (the same controller, DSG Art. 5 lit. j) who has accepted a visit is plausibly not a "Dritte" under Art. 30 Abs. 2. Her access is then an internal processing step, and Art. 6 and 7 govern it. On those terms, the bounded read (one visit card, from acceptance to the final report, logged) looks proportionate: it is the minimum for performing the visit. Which fields are the minimum is for `cra`.
- Not settled: whether, under professional secrecy, giving that card to a nurse outside the client's usual team needs the client's consent. If the client's consent is implied, does it reach substitutes from other teams? The sources read say consent may be implied [L5], but they do not cover substitutes or other teams of the same institution. The pilot canton's rule is UNKNOWN (D-11).
- **Verdict (b) as specified (no client information or consent step): unresolved: needs legal adviser** (question 2).
- **The most protective variant (b′):** the B+cover read, plus a statement at intake (D-9) that visits may be performed by nurses of other teams of the same organisation, who then read that visit's card (DSG Art. 19 Abs. 2: categories of recipients); plus the client's consent to it, which the client may refuse, in which case no cover offer is made for that client; plus read logging. On the federal sources read it is **compliant under every plausible interpretation**. The pilot canton's law is still UNKNOWN (D-11).
- While the data is synthetic, switching cover on raises no data-protection question. Whether it may go on is a safety matter (CGR-21, CGR-28, PS-12), not a legal one.

**(c) Option C before production**
- No source read requires a "care relationship" model or break-glass by name. The DSG is risk-based (Art. 7 Abs. 2, Art. 8 Abs. 1). What the sources read do make plausibly necessary is need-to-know access, read logging when DSV Art. 4 applies, and a DSFA (Art. 22).
- **Verdict (c): unresolved: needs legal adviser**, informed by the DSFA. Whether B with read logging and the (b′) consent suffices, or C is needed, is what the DSFA has to show.

**Exact questions for a legal adviser:**
1. Does DSV Art. 4 Abs. 1 require read logging for a home-care organisation's care platform at pilot scale, given team-scoped access as the preventive measure?
2. May a nurse of another team of the same organisation who has accepted a cover read that one visit's card without the client's express consent? Is information at intake enough, or is consent needed (StGB Art. 321 and the pilot canton's health law)?
3. Is the fact of being a client of the home-care organisation (name, address) health data under DSG Art. 5 lit. c Ziff. 2? Is an organisation-wide read of it by coach and operations proportionate?
4. Is a DSFA (DSG Art. 22) required before the pilot with real clients?

**Verdicts:**
- (a) B: **unresolved: needs legal adviser**. B with read logging, a DSFA and need-to-know client identity: **compliant under every plausible interpretation** on the federal sources read; cantonal law UNKNOWN (D-11, D-12).
- (b) B+cover as specified: **unresolved: needs legal adviser**. (b′): **compliant under every plausible interpretation** on the federal sources read; cantonal law UNKNOWN (D-11).
- (c): **unresolved: needs legal adviser**, after a DSFA.
