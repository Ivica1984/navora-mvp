# D-18 — Billing time rules of the applicable tariff contract

## Decision summary
Which time rules turn measured care minutes into billable OKP time: time units and rounding, a minimum per visit, what counts as care time versus travel and documentation, and (CGR-31) which residence canton applies to a visit when the residence is corrected later. **No recommendation yet.** The rules come from the tariff contract the pilot organisation signs; NAVORA invents none. Likely route: the answer cannot be settled before D-11 (pilot canton) and D-12 (admitting entity) name the contract; the package then waits for that contract, not for an opinion.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** invoicing (J5 stays an estimate), re-seeding of rates (CGR-08, BL-1, BL-2), CGR-31 (S1 before invoicing). **Findings:** CGR-31 (open, advisory S3, S1 before invoicing), CGR-07, CGR-08 (closed).

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):**
- CR-031 (PROVISIONAL): care time = started → completed minus pauses; travel and documentation are not care time; exact minutes, no rounding (0023:10-11, 28-50).
- CR-030 (UNVALIDATED): billable minutes never exceed measured care minutes; below plan the planned split is scaled (0023:98-110).
- CR-029 (UNVALIDATED): minimum 10 billable minutes per visit, region `CH` (0022:59-60; 0023:124); source a Basel-Stadt leaflet; the code reading differs from the brief (BL-2); marked `validated = false` since 0043 (CGR-08).
- CR-045: facts take times and minutes from the final signed report version (0043).
- CGR-31: a correction of the residence canton never re-derives a fact, while the preview prices with the live canton.

**Safe default in force:** J5 is labelled an estimate; exact minutes; no invoice; reference rows unvalidated.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Model the contract's rules as versioned, validated reference data per contract (time unit, rounding, minimum, what counts), applied by the existing fact engine | one engine, many contracts; the `validated` flag exists (CGR-08) | scales to cantons and contracts; no code change per contract | needs the contract text |
| B Hard-code the pilot contract's rules | rules written into the functions | fastest for one pilot | rework for each new canton and contract |

The question for the people is not which option (A is a product/engineering choice) but **what the contract says**.

**(1) Time unit, rounding, minimum**
- Settled (federal): the OKP contributions for home care are fixed per hour by category: a CHF 76.90, b CHF 63.00, c CHF 52.60 (Art. 7a Abs. 1 [S1]). **"Die Vergütung der Beiträge nach Absatz 1 erfolgt in Zeiteinheiten von 5 Minuten. Zu vergüten sind mindestens 10 Minuten."** (Art. 7a Abs. 2 [S1]). This is federal and applies in every canton; the minimum is not a Basel-Stadt rule.
- Settled (contracts read): invoices state "erbrachte Minuten (dezimal) je Leistungsart" per calendar day, monthly and electronically ([S3] Art. 9.1; [S4] Ziff. 8.1.5); billing follows the services actually given ([S3] Art. 8; [S4] Ziff. 7.2). Neither contract sets rounding or a minimum.
- UNKNOWN: (i) how measured minutes map to 5-minute units (round up, down, to nearest, or summed per day or per invoice before rounding); (ii) what "mindestens 10 Minuten" attaches to (a visit, a category within a visit, a day); (iii) **its effect: the literal wording reads as a floor on remuneration ("at least 10 minutes are to be paid"), whereas CR-029 makes a visit under 10 minutes not billable.** That is a reading of the text, not a determination; the opposite reading (a visit must reach 10 minutes to be billed) is not supported by the wording read but may exist in practice or in guidance.
- Options: **C (exact minutes, estimate, no invoice; today): compliant under every plausible interpretation** while nothing is invoiced; it cannot over-bill. Any invoicing rule: **unresolved: needs legal adviser** on (i)-(iii).

**(2) Travel and documentation**
- Settled: the hourly contributions pay the services listed in KLV Art. 7 Abs. 2 (assessment, advice and coordination; examination and treatment; basic care) [S1]; travel is not named there. The contracts read neither include nor exclude travel or documentation time [S3, S4].
- UNKNOWN: whether any part of documentation (e.g. care planning, coordination with doctors, which belong to category a) counts as category-a time; whether travel is paid by cantonal residual financing (cantonal law, D-11).
- Option verdict: **CR-031 (travel and documentation are not OKP care time) is the protective reading: compliant under every plausible interpretation for OKP**, because it can only under-bill, never over-bill. Whether it under-bills (category-a coordination, cantonal travel flat rates) is **unresolved: needs legal adviser and the pilot canton's residual-financing rules (D-11)**.

**(3) Which canton, and from when (CGR-31)**
- Settled (federal, as amended in 2017, in force 1 January 2019 [S2]): "Die Kantone regeln die Restfinanzierung." The canton **in which the insured person has their Wohnsitz** is responsible for setting and paying residual financing; **"Im Bereich der ambulanten Pflege gelten die Regeln der Restfinanzierung des Standortkantons des Leistungserbringers."** The OKP contributions and the 10-minute minimum are federal [S1] and do not depend on any canton.
- Consequence for NAVORA (a reading, not a determination): the residence canton decides **who pays** the residual financing; the **rules** (amounts, conditions) of the residual financing for home care are those of the **provider's location canton**. NAVORA's facts take the client's residence canton for the minimum-minutes rule and the tariff versions, and the preview prices with the live residence canton (CGR-31, 0043:179, 221, 244, 373-378). If that holds as described, the residence canton is the wrong key for the residual-financing rules and irrelevant for the OKP part; this is a modelling question for `eng`/`po` before invoicing, not a rule I set.
- UNKNOWN: (i) whether the residence relevant for a visit is the residence at the visit date (Wohnsitz is a factual civil-law status, so a later correction of a wrong record plausibly corrects the past too; a real move changes it only from the move); (ii) the current consolidated wording of Art. 25a Abs. 5 KVG (only the 2017 amendment was read); (iii) cantonal procedures for claiming residual financing (D-11).
- Option verdict for CGR-31: the strict default the `cra` named (a residence correction re-derives the fact, or marks it not billable with a reason; the preview prices with the fact's own canton) is **compliant under every plausible interpretation** while nothing is invoiced. The final time rule ("as of the visit date" vs "as corrected") is **unresolved: needs legal adviser**, but on the sources read the more likely answer is "the true Wohnsitz on the visit date", which is "as corrected" for a corrected error and "as of the visit date" for a real move. NAVORA cannot tell the two apart today (UNKNOWN whether intake records a reason for a residence change).

**Missing facts and who supplies them:** the pilot canton and its residual-financing law (D-11); the admitted provider and its location canton (D-12); the administrative/tariff contracts it signs and any rounding/minimum annex (D-18 itself); the current fedlex texts of KLV Art. 7a and KVG Art. 25a (a human check).

**Exact questions for a legal adviser:**
1. KLV Art. 7a Abs. 2: how are measured minutes converted into 5-minute units (rounding direction, per visit/day/category), and does "Zu vergüten sind mindestens 10 Minuten" mean a visit under 10 minutes is paid as 10 minutes, or that it is not paid?
2. Is any documentation or coordination time billable as KLV Art. 7 Abs. 2 lit. a, and does the pilot canton pay travel through residual financing?
3. For a home-care client, are the residual-financing rules those of the provider's location canton while the residence canton pays (KVG Art. 25a Abs. 5)? When the recorded residence is corrected after a visit, which residence governs that visit?

**Verdict on the most protective option (C, as built; CR-031; CGR-31 strict default): compliant under every plausible interpretation** while no invoice is produced. **Every invoicing rule: unresolved: needs legal adviser** (questions 1-3) and the facts from D-11 and D-12.
