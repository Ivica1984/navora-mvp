# D-38 — Transfer terms of the partnership letter of intent

## Decision summary
D-12 is decided (owner 2026-10-27): B as an operating-model partnership with a licensed, OKP-admitted Zurich provider, a 12-month sunset, and the team-owned provider (C) prepared from month 0. The terms that make the sunset real are written nowhere. This package proposes the terms the partnership letter of intent (LOI) should contain **before the owner signs one**. Raised by `csa` as STR-15 (issue #539, high).

**Recommendation (po 2026-10-27 20:15, Medium; E drafted by po under the two-run rule, C and L in): awaiting approval: owner.** Approve terms **T1-T14** as the minimum content of the partnership letter of intent; no LOI naming the sunset is signed before. ## Context and problem statement
- **IMPLEMENTED:** nothing. No partner is named; no LOI exists; NAVORA holds synthetic data only.
- **Why it matters (care model):** the sunset is how a self-organised team becomes its own provider, keeps its clients' continuity and earns the provider margin instead of a salary inside a hierarchy (value order 2, 3, 4). Without written terms the transfer depends on the partner's goodwill, and NAVORA drifts into being the partner's supplier (`csa` mandate drift).
- **What is already settled on the sources `lca` read (D-12, 13:52, advisory):**
  1. A licence and an OKP admission are not transferred; the new provider needs its own licence, admission and ZSR.
  2. Clients move only by their own free choice among admitted providers (KVG Art. 41 Abs. 1, secondary source).
  3. Records stay with the partner (controller; retention). A copy to C needs the client's consent as a rule; NAVORA may not move data between organisations on its own.
  4. During employment the nurses owe loyalty to the partner; preparing C is plausibly allowed, approaching clients or colleagues to leave is not.
- **Timing (`csa` estimate, STRATEGY §4):** two procedures of about 8 weeks each after a complete file; the C file must be complete by about month 7 to be admitted within the sunset.
- **Related:** D-11 (canton and municipalities), D-14 (fee), D-18 (tariff contract), D-22 (orders), D-4 (retention), D-29 (qualification verification), D-35 (AI path, data minimisation), LEAVE1 (access ends when employment ends).
- **Safe default in force:** the D-12 decision stands; nothing is transferred; synthetic data only.

**T5 (client information naming a possible later provider)**
- *Settled:* the client chooses freely among admitted providers ([K1]); NAVORA and the team cannot move a client. Telling the client this is consistent with the transparency of DSG Art. 19 ([L1]).
- *Settled:* the intake information **cannot serve as consent** to a later copy of her records to C. Consent is valid only "für eine oder mehrere bestimmte Bearbeitungen nach angemessener Information freiwillig", and for health data it must be express ([L1] Art. 6 Abs. 6, 7 lit. a). At intake C does not exist, the recipient and the data set are unknown. Consent to a copy must be asked at the cut-over, by the partner, for that copy (T3).

The partner's written consent (T1) and the partner as the sender reduce the first risk; I cannot exclude it.

**T11: what the new evidence changes**
1. **The KLV text read does not tie an order to a provider.** [K2] Art. 8 speaks of the doctor's order and its validity; it names no provider. So the KLV wording read does not itself require a new order for C. What does tie billing to C is [A1] (15:48 note 1): C's own *Bedarfsmeldung*, a needs assessment by C's qualified nurse ([A1] Art. 7; [K2] Art. 8a Abs. 1), no retroactive validity. Whether insurers accept the partner's still-valid order for C's services is a contract and practice question: **UNKNOWN, narrowed.**
2. **Every order expires.** At most nine months for ordinary home care ([K2] Art. 8 Abs. 2 lit. a); after any extension or renewal a new needs assessment is due ([K2] Art. 8a Abs. 7). With a 12-month sunset, most clients' orders will be renewed at least once during the pilot anyway. Aligning the cut-over with a renewal is a protective option.
3. **Since July 2024 part of the care needs no order** ([K3]: assessment, counselling, coordination, basic care). For these, the carry-over question falls away; it remains for treatment care (*Behandlungspflege*). The conditions are in the current KLV and the tariff partners' control mechanism, neither read.

**CGR-79 seen from the legal side**
- *(1) No day without a provider.* T11's default protects billing; on its own it can leave a client without anyone allowed to visit. If the care relationship is a mandate (not established), the partner ending it at the cut-over before C can visit could be an ending "zur Unzeit" with a duty to pay damages ([O3], OR Art. 404 Abs. 2); independently, the partner's Leitung Pflege is responsible for proper care of its clients until they leave ([Z1] A.3.4.2). The protective term: **the partner's care continues for each client until C's first valid visit; the client's cut-over date (T4) is set only once C's *Bedarfsmeldung*, needs assessment and, for treatment care, a valid order are in place.**
- *(2) Open items.* Until a client's cut-over, the partner stays controller and licence holder for that client; every open item listed by `cra` (unsigned or returned reports, open follow-ups, holds, primary nurse) is the partner's to close or reassign to its own staff **before** the team's employment ends ([Z1] A.3.4.2; D-30). No legal objection to `cra`'s remedy; it is required by the partner's responsibility, not only advisable.
- *(3) Safety facts to C.* Passing the care plan, open alerts or the last assessment to C is a disclosure of health data to another controller and needs the client's express consent as a rule ([L1] Art. 6 Abs. 6-7; [B1] as cited in D-12 13:52). The protective form is to ask for that consent **in the same conversation in which the client chooses C**, for a named data set, with the partner as sender (T3). For a client who cannot consent, who may consent for her (ZGB Art. 378, not read) is UNKNOWN.
