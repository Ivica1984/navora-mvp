# D-20 — Who admits members to a team

## Decision summary
Today only an organisation administrator invites members and adds them to a team (D-5, ADM1). The row proposes that **teams invite and admit colleagues themselves** by a team decision (as J2 decides on cases), while NAVORA verifies identity and qualification before the qualification is active (D-29). That removes a central hierarchy step from a self-organised model (value order 4, team autonomy; 5, less administration). PO working view: **team admission by team decision, qualification active only after D-29's confirmation, the administrator keeps removal for cause and an audit view**. The PO may decide it if `lca` finds no employment-law, contract or liability limit (who is the employer and who is accountable, D-12) and Engineering shows it is reversible. Inputs requested: Engineering and `lca`; `cra` only on one point (qualification mix in a team, question 3).

## Context and problem statement
- IMPLEMENTED (ADM1, 0035-0037, 0059): `invite_member` (role `admin`, `coach`, `operations` or `nurse`; team and qualification together), `add_team_member` and `remove_team_member` require `private.is_org_admin` (0035:302-333); removal refused while the person is primary nurse or deputy of an open case (J6); audit rows for each command.
- D-26 (package `inputs-requested`): the invitation e-mail needs the service-role key on a server path; a team-level invite reuses the same path.
- D-29 (`awaiting-approval: owner`): how a qualification is verified before it is active (option B: evidence recorded by someone the licence holder authorised). A team decision cannot replace that check.
- Challenge-review row: "Central administration admits members to teams | doubtful".
- R-11 (risk register, S1): professional accountability without line management unassigned.
- Safe default in force: admin-only admission (D-5).

**Legal check (step 4, po 2026-10-24):** yes. Who may hire or admit a person into care work is tied to the employer and the licence holder (D-12): an admission by colleagues must not create an employment relationship, a contract or a professional responsibility nobody signed for. So `lca` is asked.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (as built) | admin invites and admits | one accountable step | a hierarchy step per hire; the team waits for an admin |
| B | the team proposes, the admin confirms | team choice visible | still two steps, admin remains a gate |
| C | the team admits by team decision (J2 rule: a quorum of present members, objections recorded); NAVORA activates the qualification only after D-29's confirmation; the admin can remove for cause and sees every admission | team autonomy; no admin step for a normal hire; qualification safety kept | needs D-26 (invite path), D-29, and `lca` on the employer question |
| D | C, but the employment contract stays with the licence holder and the team's admission is a recommendation the licence holder executes | separates team choice from the legal act | if the licence holder always executes, it is B again |

**1. Can a team decision admit a person without creating an employment relationship?**
- An employment contract binds the **employer** and the employee ([L6] Art. 319 Abs. 1). It needs no written form and can arise tacitly once work is done for pay (OR Art. 320 Abs. 2, as summarised by [L7]).
- **Consequence:** if a team admission lets a person start working for the organisation, a contract with the employer can arise without anyone at the employer having reviewed it (pay D-14, working time D-23). The team members are not the employer. Whether they can bind it depends on authority the employer gave them (OR Art. 32 ff., not read: UNKNOWN).
- So C does not avoid an employment relationship; it can create one nobody signed for. That is the risk the PO named. It goes away if three acts are kept apart: (i) **the contract** (employer, outside NAVORA or recorded in NAVORA as a fact by a person the employer authorised); (ii) **team membership** (may be a team decision); (iii) **qualification activation** (D-29). Membership should only take effect for a person whose contract fact is recorded.

**2. Does the licence holder's accountability allow the team to choose?** For ZH (orientation until D-11): one nursing lead, a single person with a ZH practice licence, and a deputy ([L3] 5.1); a *Standortleitung Pflege* per site supervises staff without a licence (5.2); tasks only within training, and assistants only by individual delegation (5.4); staffing in proportion to the operating concept, with a staffing plan by qualification and function (5.3, 7.4); the operator sets up suitable organisational arrangements (3.2); changes of the overall or nursing leadership and of a *Standortleitung* are reported in advance for approval (11). **Who selects staff is not regulated** in the text read. The payer contract sets staff qualifications in Anhang 4a, and "die Verantwortung für den Pflegeprozess liegt immer bei einem/r Absolventen/in der Tertiärstufe" ([L2] Art. 18 Abs. 1; Anhang 4a).
- **So:** nothing read forbids the team from choosing a colleague. What must stay with the licence holder: verifying qualifications (D-29), supervision and delegation, and keeping the staffing plan in proportion. Protective conditions for C: the qualification stays inactive until D-29; a team command can never add, remove or vacate a **lead role** (nursing lead, deputy, *Standortleitung*), because those changes need cantonal approval; the nursing lead or *Standortleitung* sees every admission and can remove for cause; nobody plans visits before the contract fact is recorded.

**3. Data protection.** "Der Arbeitgeber darf Daten über den Arbeitnehmer nur bearbeiten, soweit sie dessen Eignung für das Arbeitsverhältnis betreffen oder zur Durchführung des Arbeitsvertrages erforderlich sind" ([L5]). Whether this also covers candidates before hiring is UNKNOWN on the text read (it speaks of the *Arbeitnehmer*). Under DSG minimisation (as cited in D-9) the result is the same in either case. Team members who see candidate data act for the employer. Protective shape: the team sees only what the decision needs (name, the qualification claimed, availability; no references, health data, pay or private contact data beyond the invite); an objection is recorded as a reason code, never as free-text judgements about a person; a declined candidate's data is deleted after the decision (period: D-4).

**4. What depends on other decisions:** D-12 (who the employer and licence holder is, and whether its internal rules can give teams authority to hire); D-11 (canton: only ZH read); D-29 (qualification check); D-26 (invite e-mail, information duty); D-23, D-14 (terms a tacit contract would carry).

**(1) Automatic access end the day after a recorded contract end; release and the 14-day list.**
- **Access end (ADM2-13).** Ending a former employee's access on the day after her contract ends is what DSG proportionality and data security point to (Art. 6 Abs. 2, Art. 8 Abs. 1 [A3']); it closes the residual I named in D-30 and D-10 ("access ends only when an administrator records it") by data. No source read requires access to continue after the employment; ending it cannot breach a duty on any text read. The actor `system` with reason `contract_end` is attributable: the date that triggers it was recorded by a named administrator (audited, ADM2-11). Data accuracy (Art. 6 Abs. 5 [A3']): a wrong date ends access early; `restore_member_access` and the change-before-it-passes rule correct it. **Organisational, not code (D-12):** a contract that ends earlier than recorded (immediate termination, leave of absence with release from work) still needs the manual `end_member_access`; the employer's offboarding policy must say so.
- **Release of later visits when the date is recorded (ADM2-11).** A scheduling fact; no read is widened and no client data move. No legal question found.
- **The LEAVE1-12 list to the team's RNs from 14 days before (ADM2-12).** It tells colleagues that she is leaving and which reports she has open (kind, date, team). That is employee data used for the continuity of care she owes under her contract and the employer owes its clients; it stays within "zur Durchführung des Arbeitsvertrages erforderlich" ([L5], as read) and within proportionality (Art. 6 Abs. 2 [A3']) as long as it shows no reason for the departure. ADM2 records only a date and the code `contract_end`, which is the protective state: **no field for who ended the contract or why** should be added later.
- **Verdict (1):** **compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read**, subject to the step in (2).

**(2) Is a step required before the access end that LEAVE1 does not provide? Yes, one: the export reminder (G-19 item 4).**
- The employer keeps the working-time record (D-23 C plus D: NAVORA is not the record) and must keep it available to the authorities, per the SECO commentary for five years ([N1]; ArGV 1 Art. 73 not read). In NAVORA a confirmed day reaches that record only when she exports it herself; after her access ends she cannot, and an export on her behalf needs a legal adviser (G-19 item 4).
- As built, the administrator sees her unexported-days count only by opening the manual access-end step (`prepare_member_access_end`, 0087) or in the `end_member_access` result. **An access end without a command (ADM2-13) bypasses both**, so the reminder the information text promises ("damit sie dich vor dem Ende deines Zugangs an den Export erinnern kann") never happens. The text would then be inaccurate (DSG Art. 6 Abs. 3, 5 [A3']), and the employer's record can miss days without anyone noticing.
- **Protective shape (no new legal or labour-law rule; only who sees an existing count, when):** (a) from 14 days before the recorded end date, she sees on `/work` that her access ends on that date, with her own count and the existing export prompt; (b) in the same window the administrator's per-person view of the leaver (the ADM2-12 entry point) can open the recorded `prepare_member_access_end` read, so the count stays inside the access-end path and every read leaves the 0087 audit row (WORK1-C3 (b), COND2-1 unchanged); recording the end date may return the count once as well, but a count months ahead goes stale. (c) The employer's final information text (G-19 item 2) adds one sentence: access ends automatically on the day after the recorded contract end. pgTAP: day 15 no notice, day 14 notice and administrator count; no count readable outside the path.
- **Retention:** nothing else is needed before the end. 0074 deletes nothing but push subscriptions; her reports, signatures, audit rows and confirmed days stay (D-4 provisional no-deletion rule). Her right of access (DSG Art. 25) after she leaves runs through the employer, as for any former employee; no source read requires self-service access.

**(3) Period for the candidate's three fields (D-4).**
- **No fixed period was found in the texts read.** The DSG sets none; data are destroyed or anonymised once no longer needed for the purpose (Art. 6 Abs. 4 [A3]). The only time limit that bears on a rejection is GlG Art. 8 Abs. 2: three months from the employer's communication of the rejection ([N2]); secondary practice derives three to four months for the employer's applicant file ([N5]). That is an **interest** of the employer in keeping evidence, not a duty to keep it, and it is served by the employer's own application file (the application the candidate gave the team lies outside NAVORA). NAVORA neither communicates a rejection nor knows when the employer did, so it could not count that period reliably.
- **Declined or lapsed:** deletion at the outcome (ADM2-8 default 0 days) is **compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read**, on the organisational condition that the employer keeps whatever it needs to answer an Art. 8 Abs. 1 request in its own application file (D-12 policy). If the employer wants NAVORA to hold the three fields longer, a period of up to three months after the decision is plausibly proportionate for that stated purpose ([N2], [N5]), but then the owner must state the purpose and the period, and the team's reason codes would become part of that evidence; whether that is wanted is **owner (D-12)**.
- **Admitted, after "Vertrag erfasst":** the account and membership carry what the employment needs; the three fields are redundant. Deletion in the same transaction is **compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read**. The claimed qualification is not evidence of the verified one; if D-29 wants to keep what was claimed next to what was verified, that belongs to D-29's verification record, not here.
- **Information duty (G-23 item, organisational):** a team member who enters the three fields processes the candidate's data for the employer, so the employer must inform her (DSG Art. 19 Abs. 1 [N3]); if the fields come from someone other than the candidate, within one month (Abs. 5). Deleting the data quickly does not remove the duty; of the exceptions read, only "already has the information" (Art. 20 Abs. 1 lit. a [N4]) fits, through the employer's applicant information. Content and channel: **owner (D-12)**, with D-26.
- **Verdict (3):** 0 days for both cases: compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read, with the employer's applicant information and its own application file (D-12). A longer NAVORA period: **unresolved: needs owner** (purpose and period). Other cantons and payer contracts add nothing here on what I read; D-11 remains open only for the admission and staffing rules (G-23).
