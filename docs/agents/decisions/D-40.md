# D-40 — Client- and relative-facing AI (Family Companion Layer, Check-In Services)

**Owner freeze (directive 2026-11-03 14:00, post-PR #822): specification only.** No implementation, no database table, no migration, no production architecture until the owner approves this package. Allowed: requirements analysis, specification, decision support. Source: AI domains 3 and 4 in [`AI_FIRST_ARCHITECTURE_UPDATE.md`](../../product/AI_FIRST_ARCHITECTURE_UPDATE.md) §5.2.

## Decision summary
May NAVORA offer clients and named relatives a channel in which AI shows logistics facts, routes messages and drafts replies (Family Companion), and may it run scheduled check-ins with clients between visits (Check-In)? If so, with which consent, transparency, representation rules and limits on what AI may answer? **Recommendation: not before the owner approves it (frozen).** In every option, AI gives no clinical advice to clients or relatives, and any non-logistics answer is sent by a team member (owner directive 2026-11-03: no automatic clinical recommendations to patients or relatives). Approval: `owner`.

## Context and problem statement
- **Why:** relatives phone the team to ask when the nurse comes (challenge row, HYPOTHESIS). Between visits nobody knows how the client is.
- **What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):** no client- or relative-facing access; the visit window and the responsible member are recorded per visit.
- **Safe default in force:** no such channel; D-9 (legal basis at intake), D-3 (read scope), D-35 A, D-1.
- **Blocks:** AI domains 3 and 4 (roadmap stages 1-3).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | No client- or relative-facing channel in the pilot | no new access path | phone calls stay |
| B | Logistics only, rule-based, no AI: a consented relative sees visit window, who comes, changes | fewer calls; level L | consent per relative; representation of persons without capacity |
| C | B plus AI routing and reply drafts; a team member sends every non-logistics reply | less writing for the team | D-35 provider; transparency that an AI drafted |
| D | Check-ins with question sets the Clinical Owner approved; answers stored as client-reported facts; a deviation becomes a team task | contact between visits | clinical question sets (Clinical Owner); possible device function if interpreted (D-34) |

Needs a D-35 provider.
- **Check-ins (D):** question sets and bands as Clinical Rule Register rows; answers stored as client-reported facts; a deviation becomes a team task with the Clinical Owner's response time. Not designable before G-1 and the D-34 adviser.

**Sources (read 2026-11-04; numbering shared with my D-34 and D-39 sections of this run):**
- [N9] Kanton Basel-Stadt, "Leitfaden zur beruflichen Schweigepflicht von Gesundheitsfachpersonen" (Stand März 2023): Ziff. 2.2 p. 4: secrecy applies towards everyone except the patient; relatives cannot release it or demand disclosure, the right is personal to the patient. Ziff. 3.1.2 p. 7: a legal representative of a patient without capacity may consent to disclosure, in the patient's interest; the ZGB Art. 378 representatives' role concerns consent to medical measures. Ziff. 3.2 p. 7-8: presumed consent only where the patient cannot express a will (emergency), judged by the patient's presumed will. Ziff. 4.2 p. 9: § 27 Abs. 2 GesG BS gives health professionals a **right (not a duty)** to give necessary information to co-treating professionals and next of kin unless the patient or the representative has forbidden it.
- [N12] ZGB Art. 378 Abs. 1 (order of representatives for medical measures: person named in a patient decree or care mandate; deputy with that power; spouse or registered partner in a shared household or giving regular personal support; cohabitant; descendants, parents, siblings who give regular personal support) and Abs. 3, through droit-bilingue.ch (page generated 16 January 2021; not an official publication; **whether the text changed since is unverified**).
- [N2] Commission FAQ on AI Act Art. 50 (as in my D-34 section of this run): Art. 50(1) applies where the AI itself communicates directly with a person.
- As read earlier, not re-read: [A1] EDÖB on AI (a person using a conversational system must be told it is a machine; transparency on purpose, functioning, data sources); [P1] DSG Art. 5, 6, 19, 22; [N6] DSG Art. 21 (this run, D-34).
- **Not read:** OR on liability (Art. 41, 97, 101); the care-mandate (*Vorsorgeauftrag*) and deputyship articles beyond Art. 378; Basel-Landschaft health law; the D-9 consent texts drafted on 2026-11-03 were not re-read for this section.

**Settled (on the sources read)**
- **Relatives are third parties.** Anything from the treatment relationship, including that a named person receives home care and when a nurse comes, is shown to a relative only with the client's consent or a statutory exception ([N9] Ziff. 2.2). That the visit window itself is covered by secrecy is my reading: it reveals that the person receives care.
- **The cantonal exception is a professional's case-by-case right**, not a standing channel: § 27 Abs. 2 GesG BS lets a health professional inform next of kin with necessary information unless the patient forbade it ([N9] Ziff. 4.2). A permanent, self-service platform view is a different thing; I would not rest options B to D on § 27 Abs. 2 (reading).
- **Clients without capacity.** A legal representative may consent to disclosure in the client's interest ([N9] Ziff. 3.1.2); the order of representatives for medical measures is ZGB Art. 378 ([N12]). Whether a representative under Art. 378 (whose role is consent to medical measures) may also authorise an ongoing information channel to other relatives is **not settled** by the sources read.
- **Transparency that AI is involved.** In Switzerland: the EDÖB expects people to be told when they deal with a machine and how AI is used ([A1]); for relatives this is an information duty of the care organisation, and relatives become data subjects themselves (account, messages; [P1] Art. 19). In the EU (relatives living in Germany or France, likely in Basel): Art. 50(1) where the AI itself answers ([N2]); in option C, where a team member sends each non-logistics reply, the AI does not communicate directly, so Art. 50(1) is plausibly not engaged (reading). Whether the Act is engaged at all is D-34 adviser question 8 (d).
- **Automated decisions.** None of the options makes a decision with a legal consequence for the client or relative ([N6]); routing a message or creating a team task is not one.
- **AI service.** Option C (AI routing and drafts) sends client- and relative-written text, which will contain health data, to an AI service: D-35 stage-1 conditions and the D-34 provider terms apply in full.

**UNKNOWN and why**
- Form of consent per relative (written, electronic, revocation) and whether a client with capacity can delegate who may be added later: DSG consent articles not re-read; D-9.
- Whether an Art. 378 representative, a deputy (*Beistand*) or a person under a care mandate may consent to the channel for a client without capacity, and to which relatives (ZGB beyond Art. 378 not read).
- Liability for a wrong logistics answer (for example "the nurse comes at 10" when the visit is uncovered) or a late reply to an urgent-sounding message: contract and tort law not read; it depends on what the provider promises (H-28 statement).
- Option D: device status (D-34 adviser question 10), check-in bands and response time (Clinical Owner).
- Basel-Landschaft equivalents of §§ 26-27 GesG BS: not read.

**Options against the sources read**
- **A (no channel): compliant under every plausible interpretation.**
- **B (rule-based logistics for a consented relative): unresolved: needs legal adviser** on two narrow points: the form and scope of consent (and its revocation), and representation for clients without capacity. On the sources read, the protective variant is: logistics only (visit window, who comes, changes, how to reach the team); shown only after the team knows a change (`cra`); only to relatives named one by one by a client with capacity, or by her representative in her interest ([N9] 3.1.2); revocable at any time with immediate effect; reads audited; information to the relative on what is processed. I found no source that forbids that variant; it is not "every plausible interpretation" because the consent and representation questions are open.
- **C (B plus AI routing and drafts sent by a team member): unresolved: needs legal adviser and owner**, for everything in B plus D-35 stage 1 (provider, Art. 321 *Hilfsperson* question) and the information that AI drafts are used.
- **D (check-ins with approved question sets): unresolved: needs regulatory adviser** (MepV "Überwachung", D-34 question 10), **the Clinical Owner** (bands, response time) **and owner**; the "not an emergency line" statement mitigates but does not settle liability.
- **The rule "AI gives no clinical advice to clients or relatives; any non-logistics answer is sent by a team member" (owner prohibition): compliant under every plausible interpretation**; it removes the AI's direct communication and keeps a professional accountable for every substantive reply.

**Verdict on the recommended default (A until the owner approves; B as the expected next step): A: compliant under every plausible interpretation. B: unresolved: needs legal adviser** (questions 1-2 below); with the protective variant above it is the least exposed of the channel options.

**Exact questions for a legal adviser**
1. In Basel-Stadt (and BL), what form of consent does a client with capacity give so that named relatives may see visit window, assigned nurse and changes in an app, and how is revocation handled? Does § 27 Abs. 2 GesG BS add anything for such a standing channel?
2. For a client without capacity: may a representative under ZGB Art. 378, a deputy or a person under a care mandate consent to such a channel, and for which relatives?
3. What does the care organisation have to tell a relative (and, if the relative lives in the EU, under which rules) when AI routes or drafts messages that a team member sends?
4. What liability does the organisation carry for a logistics answer that is wrong or late, and what must the client and relative terms say?
5. (Regulatory adviser:) is a scheduled check-in with approved questions and bands (option D) a medical device function (D-34 question 10)?
