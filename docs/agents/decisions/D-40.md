# D-40 — Client- and relative-facing AI (Family Companion Layer, Check-In Services)

**Owner freeze (directive 2026-11-03 14:00, post-PR #822): specification only.** No implementation, no database table, no migration, no production architecture until the owner approves this package. Allowed: requirements analysis, specification, decision support. Source: AI domains 3 and 4 in [`AI_FIRST_ARCHITECTURE_UPDATE.md`](../../product/AI_FIRST_ARCHITECTURE_UPDATE.md) §5.2.

## Decision summary
May NAVORA offer clients and named relatives a channel in which AI shows logistics facts, routes messages and drafts replies (Family Companion), and may it run scheduled check-ins with clients between visits (Check-In)? If so, with which consent, transparency, representation rules and limits on what AI may answer? **Recommendation: not before the owner approves it (frozen).** In every option, AI gives no clinical advice to clients or relatives, and any non-logistics answer is sent by a team member (owner directive 2026-11-03: no automatic clinical recommendations to patients or relatives). Approval: `owner`.

## Context and problem statement
- **Why:** relatives phone the team to ask when the nurse comes (challenge row, HYPOTHESIS). Between visits nobody knows how the client is.
- **What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):** no client- or relative-facing access; the visit window and the responsible member are recorded per visit.
- **Safe default in force:** no such channel; D-9 (legal basis at intake), D-3 (read scope), D-35 A, D-1.
- **Blocks:** AI domains 3 and 4 (roadmap stages 1-3).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | No client- or relative-facing channel in the pilot | no new access path | phone calls stay |
| B | Logistics only, rule-based, no AI: a consented relative sees visit window, who comes, changes | fewer calls; level L | consent per relative; representation of persons without capacity |
| C | B plus AI routing and reply drafts; a team member sends every non-logistics reply | less writing for the team | D-35 provider; transparency that an AI drafted |
| D | Check-ins with question sets the Clinical Owner approved; answers stored as client-reported facts; a deviation becomes a team task | contact between visits | clinical question sets (Clinical Owner); possible device function if interpreted (D-34) |

**Settled (on the sources read)**
- **Relatives are third parties.** Anything from the treatment relationship, including that a named person receives home care and when a nurse comes, is shown to a relative only with the client's consent or a statutory exception ([N9] Ziff. 2.2). That the visit window itself is covered by secrecy is my reading: it reveals that the person receives care.
- **The cantonal exception is a professional's case-by-case right**, not a standing channel: § 27 Abs. 2 GesG BS lets a health professional inform next of kin with necessary information unless the patient forbade it ([N9] Ziff. 4.2). A permanent, self-service platform view is a different thing; I would not rest options B to D on § 27 Abs. 2 (reading).
- **Clients without capacity.** A legal representative may consent to disclosure in the client's interest ([N9] Ziff. 3.1.2); the order of representatives for medical measures is ZGB Art. 378 ([N12]). Whether a representative under Art. 378 (whose role is consent to medical measures) may also authorise an ongoing information channel to other relatives is **not settled** by the sources read.
- **Transparency that AI is involved.** In Switzerland: the EDÖB expects people to be told when they deal with a machine and how AI is used ([A1]); for relatives this is an information duty of the care organisation, and relatives become data subjects themselves (account, messages; [P1] Art. 19). In the EU (relatives living in Germany or France, likely in Basel): Art. 50(1) where the AI itself answers ([N2]); in option C, where a team member sends each non-logistics reply, the AI does not communicate directly, so Art. 50(1) is plausibly not engaged (reading). **(1) Consent of a client with capacity: what is settled, on the sources read**
- **Consent is the justification.** That a named person receives home care, and when a nurse comes, is health data (sensitive, [P1]) and within professional secrecy ([N9] Ziff. 2.2; my reading for the visit window, as at 05:20). Showing it to a relative is a disclosure of sensitive data to a third party ([N14] Art. 30 Abs. 2 lit. c), lawful only with a justification ([N14] Art. 31 Abs. 1). The cantonal right to inform next of kin (§ 27 Abs. 2 GesG BS, [N9] Ziff. 4.2) is a professional's case-by-case right, not a basis for a standing view (reading, unchanged). So FAM1 rests on the client's consent, as the brief assumes.
- **Requirements that follow** (each maps to a FAM1 criterion):
  - **express** ([N14] Art. 6 Abs. 7 lit. a): an active "yes" by the client to this view for this relative, not inferred from silence or from the intake consent (FAM1-2; the D-9 intake consent does not cover it unless it names this disclosure specifically);
  - **specific** ([N14] Art. 6 Abs. 6; [N13]): one relative, one client, the fixed scope "Einsätze: Zeit, Stand, zugeteilte Person" (FAM1-2 already has this; a scope change needs a new consent);
  - **informed** ([N14] Art. 6 Abs. 6; [N13]): before she agrees the client is told what the relative will see (seven days of planned times and status, the assigned person or "Ihr Pflegeteam"), that the relative will see when visits happen, that she can withdraw at any time by telling any team member, and that refusing changes nothing in her care. The last two points address CGR-126 (the timetable effect) at the consent step: the client decides knowing it;
  - **free** ([N13] Kap. 2): refusal or withdrawal has no effect on care or planning; nothing in NAVORA may prompt, rank or count clients by whether they consented (a testable negative);
  - **no form required, but documented** ([N13] Kap. 2): the FAM1-2 record (who consented, who recorded, when, scope) is the evidence. A signature is not required on the sources read; whether the canton or the partner's own policy requires writing is UNKNOWN (D-12, partner policy);
  - **revocable at any time, without reason, by a simple route** ([N13] Kap. 2): FAM1-3 (any team member records the withdrawal; access ends at the next request) fits.
- **Information to the relative and to staff** ([N14] Art. 19 Abs. 1-2): the relative becomes a data subject (sign-in, read log FAM1-14) and is told by the controller (the care organisation, D-12) who processes what for which purpose; staff are told that relatives of consented clients see their display name and the visit window (see (3)).
- **Who decides capacity** ([N16]; CGR-126): capacity is judged per decision; the professional obtaining the consent judges it in the conversation. Recording in FAM1-2 that the recording RN considered the client able to decide **this** consent (a fact with author and time, no score) fits the evidence purpose of a documented consent. **NAVORA sets no capacity test**; how to judge it is the Clinical Owner's and the organisation's (CGR-126), not a legal rule I can write.
- **AUT-44 (consent record = invitation):** legally neutral, with one data-security condition (my reading; DSG Art. 8 as quoted in D-26 [I3], secondary, not re-read): the contact address that receives the sign-in is taken from the client or the relative in that conversation and bound to that one relative, because a sign-in sent to a wrong address discloses sensitive data to a stranger. The invitation text names no client and no health fact before the relative has signed in. Identity verification itself stays open (engineering question 2; I check the proposal once it exists).

**(2) Client without capacity: representation**
- **Settled on the sources read:** a representative may act for a person without capacity only for **relatively** highly personal rights ([N15], [N16], ZGB Art. 19c as summarised); a legal representative may consent to disclosure in the client's interest ([N9] Ziff. 3.1.2); a deputy represents only within the powers the KESB expressly gave ([N17]); the Art. 378 order governs **medical measures** ([N12], [N17]).
- **Not settled:** (a) whether consent to a standing information view for relatives is a relatively highly personal right (I would expect so, but have no source); (b) which representative may give it: a person under a care mandate whose scope covers personal care, a deputy whose KESB powers cover personal matters or health, or an Art. 378 representative whose role is consent to medical measures, which a schedule view is not; (c) the **self-nomination conflict**: the representative is often the relative who wants the view; whether she may consent for herself, and whether others must agree, is UNKNOWN; (d) whether a consent the client gave while she had capacity survives a later loss of capacity until revoked (DSG consent as read has no expiry; whether the representative may then revoke it, and whether the team must re-check, is UNKNOWN).

A client without capacity gets no relative view until the legal adviser answers question 2 below; the relative keeps the existing route (phone, and the representative's own information rights outside NAVORA). This invents no rule and discloses nothing; it narrows the owner's scope and is the po's and owner's call. **(4) CGR-126 and CGR-127 questions addressed to `lca`**
- **May the team refuse or end the view on a protective concern, also against a representative's consent?** Consent permits the disclosure; on the sources read it does not oblige the organisation to provide a self-service view, and § 27 Abs. 2 GesG BS is a right, not a duty ([N9] Ziff. 4.2). Ending or refusing the view therefore discloses nothing and, on the sources read, breaches no duty (reading). Two limits: the representative's own statutory information rights stay in force by other routes (Art. 377 not read: UNKNOWN in scope), and the client's or relative's terms must not promise the view as an entitlement (D-12 contract). The CGR-126 protective reason preset, visible to the team and `admin`, never to the relative, is compatible with this; the reason is itself sensitive and is processed only within the organisation (D-3 read scope).
- **One access-end sentence for every cause (CGR-127 (1)) and no cancellation reason (CGR-127 (2)):** these keep a hospital stay, a death or a move from reaching the relative through the page; they follow from the specific scope of the consent ([N14] Art. 6 Abs. 6: the consent covers schedule and status, not reasons). Supported; nothing in the sources read requires the opposite. After a death the view ends with the same sentence; whether secrecy continues after death was not read (UNKNOWN), and the protective rule does not depend on it.
- **"Stand: <time>" and no cached list (CGR-127 (4)):** a data-accuracy point (DSG Art. 6 Abs. 5 as summarised in D-34 [L9], secondary, not re-read); supported.
- **Status words (CGR-125):** a clinical display rule (`cra`, Clinical Owner); legally relevant only in that a false "durchgeführt" or "geplant" is the clearest liability exposure of the view (H-28, question 4 at 05:20). The neutral word for every other combination is the more protective option; no legal objection.
