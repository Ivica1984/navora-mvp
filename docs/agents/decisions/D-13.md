# D-13 — Assessment instrument for home care

## Decision summary
Which needs-assessment instrument does NAVORA's home-care product work with, and in what way: does NAVORA integrate a licensed instrument (the row names interRAI HC for home care, interRAI LTCF only for a nursing-home offer, D-24), or does it stay **instrument-neutral**, recording that an assessment was done, with which instrument, by whom and with which resulting minutes per KLV category, while the assessment itself is done in the provider's licensed tool? **PO working view, not a decision:** instrument-neutral now (option B); an integration (A) only once the pilot provider (D-12) and its licensed tool are known; never NAVORA's own instrument (C). Licence cost and any partnership are `owner`. Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory, Legal & Compliance.

## Context and problem statement
- **IMPLEMENTED today (J3, migration 20261021000014):** a needs assessment is a visit flag (`care_visits.is_needs_assessment`); completing it sets `cases.needs_assessment_done_at` once (`coalesce`); planned minutes per category A/B/C are entered on the care plan; the KLV category mapping is D-15 (`clinical`). NAVORA stores **no instrument, no items, no scores** and computes nothing clinical from an assessment. Read 2026-10-24, not tested.
- **Why it matters:** the needs assessment is the billing basis (KLV Art. 8a as read by `lca`, advisory) and the start of continuity (the care plan). Re-typing an assessment from another tool into NAVORA is duplicate documentation (value 5); missing it blocks billing.
- **Constraints already in force:** no invention of assessment, PAG, MQI or LTCF logic (CLAUDE.md, `DOMAIN_RULES.md`); D-34 safe default: no feature that interprets a client's clinical information to suggest an action; interRAI content is licensed and is not reproduced (`NURSING_HOME_TRANSFORMATION.md`, "licence" items).
- **What it blocks:** nothing built; it shapes a future assessment brief, ORD0 question 6 (sending the assessment result to the GP, D-22) and the nursing-home track (D-24 NH2-NH5).
- **Safe default in force:** as built (no instrument in NAVORA).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Integrate a licensed home-care instrument (e.g. interRAI HC as the row names): import or link the assessment result from the licensed system into the case | no re-typing; assessment and plan stay in step | licence and integration rights needed (cost, partnership); the instrument the pilot provider uses is UNKNOWN; scores inside NAVORA move it toward D-34's grey zone |
| B | Instrument-neutral: NAVORA records date, instrument name and version (from a short reference list), the assessing registered nurse, a document reference and the resulting minutes per category; the assessment runs in the provider's licensed tool | no licence cost; works with any provider (D-12 B); keeps NAVORA an organisation and documentation tool (D-34) | the result is typed once (minutes per category); no item-level data for quality learning |
| C | NAVORA builds its own assessment form | full control | invents clinical rules (forbidden); not accepted by payers without validation; medical-device risk (D-34) |
| D | interRAI LTCF for everything | one instrument for home and nursing home | built for residential care; nursing homes are a later horizon (D-24) |

**1. Does the law or the contract require a specific instrument? (settled for the sources read)**
- Federal: the assessment is done by a nurse under KVV Art. 49 together with the patient, covers the overall situation and the environment, follows **uniform criteria**, and its result goes on a **uniform form** developed jointly by providers and insurers, with the expected time ([L1] Art. 8a Abs. 1, 3, 4). The instrument must allow quality-indicator data (KVG Art. 59a Abs. 1 lit. f) to be captured from data routinely collected in the assessment ([L1] Abs. 5, 2019 wording). **No instrument is named for home care.** The time-study conditions of Art. 8b apply to nursing homes only (D-24).
- Contract: "Es steht den Leistungserbringern offen, welches standardisierte Bedarfserfassungsinstrument (interRAI HC oder andere)" they use ([L2] Art. 7 Abs. 2). The result goes on the *Bedarfsmeldeformular* (Art. 7 Abs. 4), within 14 calendar days of the start (Art. 7 Abs. 5). Providers sharing a patient coordinate and agree one time budget, with no duplicate assessment (Art. 7 Abs. 6). The Bedarfsmeldung carries the **GLN of the nurse who did the assessment** (Art. 8 Abs. 6 lit. e), the minutes per service type (lit. j) and the *Leistungsplanungsblatt* or service list (lit. k). When something is unclear the insurer may request the assessment itself, "e.g. MDS" (Anhang 6 Ziff. 1.2.1), and inspect files at the provider (Art. 19 Abs. 1; Anhang 6 Ziff. 2.2).
- Canton (ZH only, orientation until D-11): nursing measures rest on a written assessment made with an instrument agreed by the tariff partners or commonly used in Switzerland, and the licence application must show that a recognised Swiss instrument is used ([L3] Ziff. 3.4, 7.3).
- **So:** a standardised instrument is required, and choosing it is the provider's matter (contract) and part of its licence (ZH). NAVORA does not have to pick one. That supports B. UNKNOWN: the instrument clauses of the HSK and CSS contracts, and other cantons.
- **Consequences for B's record (protective shape, not rules):** (a) store the assessing nurse's GLN or a link to her verified qualification (D-29), because the payer form names her; (b) the minutes per category are the lit. j content; (c) the document reference must let the provider produce the full assessment for a payer check, so the full assessment stays in the provider's tool and NAVORA's record never replaces it (retention: D-4); (d) **more than one assessment per case** must be storable: a lasting change of needs requires a new assessment ([L2] Art. 9 Abs. 1, read 15:05), and so does a renewed order ([L1] Art. 8a Abs. 7, 2019 wording). That is Engineering question 1 and matches my D-22 note of 16:05.

**2. Licence for a licensed instrument (A).** interRAI licenses its content to software vendors, and "you must be licensed to use any version of interRAI forms" ([L4]). The guidelines do not say whether a system that stores only the fact of an assessment (date, instrument name, minutes), without items, scales or outputs, needs a licence: UNKNOWN, but **B reproduces no interRAI content**; naming the instrument is a factual reference. For A (importing or showing items, scales or outputs), a written licence covering that use in NAVORA is required; its terms and who must hold it are UNKNOWN until a vendor is named (D-12).

**3. Data protection.** Item-level results are health data (DSG Art. 5 lit. c, as cited in D-9). Minimisation (DSG Art. 6, as cited in D-9 and D-17) favours B: planning and billing need the minutes per category, the date and the assessor, not the items. Importing items (A) widens D-9 (basis), D-3 (read scope) and D-4 (retention), and quality learning would be a new purpose that needs its own basis. The payer may only ask for the elements that concern Art. 7 Abs. 2 services ([L1] Art. 8a Abs. 6), and the contract's information duty is proportionate ([L2] Art. 19 Abs. 2).

**4. What depends on other decisions:** D-12 (the provider, its instrument and which payer contracts it joined), D-11 (the canton's licence requirement on the instrument), D-18 (instrument clause of the pilot's tariff contract), D-29 (assessor qualification), D-4 (retention of the record and the reference).
