# D-24 — Enter nursing homes (Horizon 3)

## Decision summary
Should NAVORA enter nursing homes, when, with which design-partner home, and on which licence path (interRAI LTCF CH, RAIsoft or export rights)? **PO working view, not a decision:** yes as a direction, **not now as a build**. The first step, when it comes, is a design partnership with one ward on the organisational change only (NH0, NH1: a self-organised ward team, team rosters, handover from the record), which needs no LTCF licence; LTCF features (NH2-NH7) only after a licence. **Timing:** not before the home-care pilot has its canton and entity (D-11, D-12) and its first month of AN1/AN2 pilot data, and not before D-25 says whether a ward without a ward leader can satisfy the canton's licensing rules. Strategy and partnership are `owner`. Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory, Legal & Compliance.

## Context and problem statement
- **Analysis already written:** `docs/product/NURSING_HOME_TRANSFORMATION.md` (PO, 2026-10-22; strategic, PLANNED): proposals NH0-NH9, phases 0-4, the roles that can shrink (ward leader, scheduling, coordinator, quality steering group), and the licence boundary (PAG, MQI and LTCF logic only from the licensed specification).
- **IMPLEMENTED today:** nothing nursing-home specific. The home-care core (J2, PLAN2, COVER1, HAND1, ABS1, SIG1, AN1, AN2) is the reusable base; it plans visits, not 24-hour shifts.
- **Why now as a package, not as a build:** home care is not yet complete for a real client (billing D-18, order form D-22, entity D-12, canton D-11, staging D-2, live-database facts D-1). By value order, a second horizon now would add platform breadth (value 8) before home care delivers direct care time (value 1) to any real nurse.
- **Challenge (from scratch):** is the nursing home the right Horizon 3, or should transitions of care (REF2, hospital discharge) and integrated networks come first? The PO's answer: transitions first (Horizon 2, REF2 draft), because the hospital discharge feeds home care directly; nursing homes later, because station leadership is the largest hierarchy layer NAVORA could remove, but it needs a canton's licence conformity (D-25) and a licence (LTCF).
- **What it gates:** NH0-NH9 (none approved), D-25 (`legal`), D-13 option D.
- **Safe default in force:** no nursing-home build; NH proposals stay PLANNED.

**(1) Licence: settled on the sources read**
- interRAI instruments may be used only under a written licence; a software vendor (NAVORA would be one if it implements or displays the instrument's items) is a commercial user and generally pays royalties ([N1]). Vendors are licensed for approved territories ([N2]); in Switzerland, BESA QSys AG is named by a canton as the provider of interRAI LTCF CH ([N3]). Who grants Swiss software rights (interRAI directly, or a Swiss licensee) is **UNKNOWN**.
- Scales, algorithms and case-mix measures are not copyrighted; the items are ([N1]). **This does not make a NAVORA implementation of the scales licence-free in practice**: computing a scale needs the items, and the Swiss CH-Index and the care-level mapping used for KLV Art. 7a contributions ([N4], [N5]) are Swiss adaptations whose rights holder is UNKNOWN. So the register's rule (no interRAI, PAG or MQI content except through a licence) remains the protective line.
- The vendor duties in [N1] (sublicensing to users, export routines to interRAI, user reports for scales, no open-web mounting) are **product requirements**, not only fees. Note: "avoid mounting interRAI licensed products on the open web" needs an interpretation for a cloud web application with login; that is a question for the licensor.
- interRAI LTCF CH is, or is becoming, the instrument cantons require of listed homes for KLV Art. 8b ([N3], [N4], [N5]). A home therefore already has an instrument and a vendor; option D (partner with that vendor) reflects the market structure, and option C competes with or integrates into it.

**(2) Labour law for team-made 24-hour rosters: settled and UNKNOWN**
- A private nursing home's employees are, as a rule, under the ArG; a home within a cantonal or municipal administration, or a public-law body with mostly public-law staff, is not, and follows its staff law instead ([W5]).
- Whether a nursing home benefits from the hospital special rules of ArGV 2 Art. 15 (night and Sunday work without a permit, 17-hour span, 9-hour rest) depends on whether it is "ärztlich betreut" ([N6] Abs. 2) or on another ArGV 2 rule for homes that I did not read: **UNKNOWN**. This decides which rest, night and Sunday limits a roster validator must enforce.
- Whoever sets the roster (a self-organised team), **the employer stays responsible** for compliance with working-time limits (reading of the ArG's structure as employer duties, D-23 [W1]-[W3]; ArG Art. 6 not re-read). A team-made roster therefore needs the employer's rules built in as hard constraints (rest, night-work limits, Sunday quota) and a record the employer can produce (D-23: ArGV 1 Art. 73, retention five years). This is the D-23 question widened from visits to 24-hour shifts; **a new decision is not needed if D-23's answer is written for both** (Note to po).

**(3) What depends on D-25 and D-11**
- Whether one ward can work without a ward leader is a cantonal licensing question (D-25; for ZH: one nursing lead for the home, no job-sharing, skill mix, RN cover; no ward leader named, as read there). **Option B is unresolved until D-25 is answered for the partner home's canton**, which need not be the home-care pilot canton (D-11).
- Cantonal duty to use interRAI LTCF CH and the care-level mapping: canton-specific ([N3], [N4]).
- Whether the home is a public body: decides cantonal data-protection law and the ArG exemption ([W5]).

**(4) Medical-device position of NH4 and NH5 (linked to D-34)**
- On the D-34 analysis (MDCG 2019-11 rev.1, Annex II example 2, rule 11), a coding draft that proposes interRAI item codes from documentation (NH4) and a sentinel that flags a resident's status change for reassessment (NH5) process individual clinical information to support care decisions; both **plausibly qualify** as medical device software, possibly class IIa or higher. Not settled; **unresolved: needs regulatory adviser** (D-34 question 1, to be extended to NH4 and NH5).
- They also use LTCF items, so they need the licence ([N1]) **and** the D-34 answer; the package's PO view (C only after a licence and D-34) matches this.

**Legal risks per option**
