# D-24 — Enter nursing homes (Horizon 3)

## Decision summary
Should NAVORA enter nursing homes, when, with which design-partner home, and on which licence path (interRAI LTCF CH, RAIsoft or export rights)? **PO working view, not a decision:** yes as a direction, **not now as a build**. The first step, when it comes, is a design partnership with one ward on the organisational change only (NH0, NH1: a self-organised ward team, team rosters, handover from the record), which needs no LTCF licence; LTCF features (NH2-NH7) only after a licence. **Timing:** not before the home-care pilot has its canton and entity (D-11, D-12) and its first month of AN1/AN2 pilot data, and not before D-25 says whether a ward without a ward leader can satisfy the canton's licensing rules. Strategy and partnership are `owner`. Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory, Legal & Compliance.

## Context and problem statement
- **Analysis already written:** `docs/product/NURSING_HOME_TRANSFORMATION.md` (PO, 2026-10-22; strategic, PLANNED): proposals NH0-NH9, phases 0-4, the roles that can shrink (ward leader, scheduling, coordinator, quality steering group), and the licence boundary (PAG, MQI and LTCF logic only from the licensed specification).
- **IMPLEMENTED today:** nothing nursing-home specific. The home-care core (J2, PLAN2, COVER1, HAND1, ABS1, SIG1, AN1, AN2) is the reusable base; it plans visits, not 24-hour shifts.
- **Why now as a package, not as a build:** home care is not yet complete for a real client (billing D-18, order form D-22, entity D-12, canton D-11, staging D-2, live-database facts D-1). By value order, a second horizon now would add platform breadth (value 8) before home care delivers direct care time (value 1) to any real nurse.
- **Challenge (from scratch):** is the nursing home the right Horizon 3, or should transitions of care (REF2, hospital discharge) and integrated networks come first? The PO's answer: transitions first (Horizon 2, REF2 draft), because the hospital discharge feeds home care directly; nursing homes later, because station leadership is the largest hierarchy layer NAVORA could remove, but it needs a canton's licence conformity (D-25) and a licence (LTCF).
- **What it gates:** NH0-NH9 (none approved), D-25 (`legal`), D-13 option D.
- **Safe default in force:** no nursing-home build; NH proposals stay PLANNED.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Not now: finish the home-care pilot first, revisit with pilot data | focus; evidence before expansion | the ward-leadership hypothesis stays untested |
| B | Design partnership with one ward, organisational change only (NH0, NH1), no LTCF integration | needs no licence; tests the largest hierarchy removal; reuses PLAN2, COVER1, HAND1 | 24-hour shift planning is new work; D-25 licence conformity UNKNOWN; labour-law rules for rosters (`legal`) |
| C | Full entry with the LTCF licence and RAIsoft integration (NH2-NH7) | the assessment and quality burden is where most administration sits | licence cost and partnership; licensed specification needed; medical-device question (D-34) for coding drafts and sentinels |
| D | Partner with an existing LTCF software vendor; NAVORA only for the team model | no licence; fast | splits the record between two systems (duplicate documentation) |

## Engineering impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (po).* Asked: (1) what B needs beyond home care (24-hour shift rosters from residents' care plans and qualification mix; a ward as a team; residents instead of visits), as a size estimate, not a design; (2) which home-care modules carry over unchanged; (3) for C, what an LTCF integration would require that is UNKNOWN without the licensed specification; (4) whether any of it changes the architecture's bounded contexts (Handbook).

## Clinical impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (cra).* Asked: (1) the clinical-governance hazards of a ward without a ward leader (24-hour responsibility, medication, night shift escalation) as a structure for the Clinical Owner, not as rules; (2) which NH proposals touch PAG, MQI or LTCF logic and therefore need the licensed specification and the Clinical Owner; (3) whether NH0's measures (direct care share per shift, staff satisfaction, quality events) are enough to stop a pilot early.

## Legal/compliance impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (lca).* Asked, with sources: (1) what the interRAI LTCF CH licence and RAIsoft rights would need to cover for NAVORA (B needs none; C does); (2) labour-law limits on team-made 24-hour rosters (rest periods, night work) as a question for D-23 or a new decision; (3) which parts depend on D-25 (cantonal leadership rules) and D-11; (4) the medical-device position of NH4 (coding draft) and NH5 (status-change sentinel), linked to D-34. No verdict is drafted by the PO.

## Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Expansion before home care works | timing condition: D-11, D-12 and one month of pilot data first |
| A ward without a leader breaches the canton's licence | D-25 answered for the pilot canton before any ward pilot |
| Licensed LTCF logic reproduced without a licence | NH2-NH7 only after a licence; B uses no LTCF content |
| Teams classify residents upward to raise revenue | D-14 constraint 2: no pay or fee rule reads the care-effort group |
