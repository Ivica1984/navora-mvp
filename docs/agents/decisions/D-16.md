# D-16 — Quality event types, severities, quality hold, and who lifts it

## Decision summary
Which quality event types NAVORA offers, what makes one serious, which ones set a quality hold, what a hold stops (today only billing), and who may lift it. **No recommendation yet.** The PO's earlier proposal (2026-10-22 16:15) stands as an option: a second registered nurse of the same team, not the author, lifts a hold with a reason; serious events are additionally reviewed at network level afterwards. Expected route: `clinical` (the named Clinical Owner, G-1).

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** the relaxation of QG1 (the lift role) and the clinical meaning of a hold. **Findings:** CGR-13 (closed for the wording "Abrechnungssperre"; the clinical meaning stays here); PS-5 (S1, open: a hold does not stop care); H-06.

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED, read in the repository by the PO):**
- Event types come from a table with a `serious_default` per type; the provisional short list from the decision row (fall, wound, medication, acute decline, social situation) is engineering's seed, not a clinical rule (0020 header).
- Severity `low`, `medium`, `high`, `critical`; serious = `high` or `critical` (0020 `private.is_serious`).
- Visibility: the assigned team; serious events also coach and admin (0020:58-65).
- CR-018: a hold attaches to one visit and blocks only billing; CR-047: a hold appends a not-billable fact, lifting the last hold restores it (0043 §5).
- CR-055: a hold is called "Abrechnungssperre" everywhere, with the sentence that it does not govern care.
- Lift: coach or admin only, with a reason, once (0020:281, provisional until D-16): a management step outside the team.
- CR-020 / CR-041: a serious event is an OPS1 exception, P2 → P1 after 4 h, and keeps escalating while `in_review`.

**Safe default in force:** the provisional types and severities; a hold blocks billing only and is lifted by a coach or admin; serious events escalate and cannot be closed by hand.

## Options considered

## Clinical impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (po → cra).* Please state: whether the provisional types and the serious threshold are safe interim values; whether B keeps patient safety a precondition (four-eyes within the team, review afterwards); what C would have to stop, and the hazard of stopping necessary care; PS-5 and H-06; exactly what the Clinical Owner must approve.

**cra 2026-10-24 06:30 (copied from `reports/CLINICAL_GOVERNANCE.md` by po, verbatim, from open PR #259 at `98541a0`). Confirmed `cra` input; advisory, not a clinical rule (D-27, G-1).**

- **Are the provisional types and the serious threshold safe interim values?** Only as interim values for synthetic data. The five types (fall, wound, medication, acute decline, social situation) are engineering's seed (0020), not a taxonomy. Severity `low` to `critical` has no definitions, so the serious threshold (`high` or `critical`) depends on the reporter. A medication error rated `medium` never escalates. Gaps the Clinical Owner should consider (not rules): near misses; suspected neglect or abuse, where `lca` names a possible KESB reporting route (ZGB Art. 443); infection; aggression towards staff. The choice of taxonomy (D option) is `clinical`.
- **Does B keep patient safety a precondition?** For the **billing** hold, yes. A hold blocks billing only (CR-018, CR-055), so who lifts it does not change care. Four eyes within the team (not the author, a registered nurse) is adequate for a billing lift. Conditions:
  - the lift never closes the quality event or its escalation (CR-041);
  - for serious events, the review afterwards is an OPS1 item that cannot be closed by hand until a review is recorded, so it cannot be forgotten.
- **Option C (hold governs care):** stopping a service automatically after an event can be more harmful than continuing. Examples: stopping medication administration after a medication error, or wound care after a fall. I do not support an automatic stop. The hazard in PS-5 is not that "care continues" but that "nobody reviews the care after a serious event". The control is a mandatory care-plan review signal for the affected case after a serious event (P2, not closable by hand until a review by a registered nurse is recorded). What the review must cover is clinical.
- **Clinical Owner must approve:** taxonomy and severity definitions; what "serious" means; the review after a serious event (who, content, time); whether any event type changes care.
