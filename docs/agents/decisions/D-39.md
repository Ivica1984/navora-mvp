# D-39 — Sensors in a client's home (IoT inbound) and sensor-based risk predictions

**Owner freeze (directive 2026-11-03 14:00, post-PR #822): specification only.** No implementation, no database table, no migration, no production architecture until the owner approves this package. Allowed: requirements analysis, specification, decision support. Source: owner issue #817 (draft specification of `iot_sensor_streams` and `ki_prediction_audit_logs`).

## Decision summary
May NAVORA receive measurements from devices in a client's home and record AI risk predictions derived from them? If so, on which legal basis, with whose consent, under which device-law route, and with which retention? **Recommendation: not before the owner approves it (frozen).** The package prepares the decision. The specification in #817 stays specification. Approval: `owner` (regulatory liability, a new data category, a possible medical-device function).

## Context and problem statement
- **Why the question exists:** owner direction #817 (2026-11-03): the data model should be ready in phase 1 for automated KVG billing in phase 2. Owner directive 14:00: frozen, specification only.
- **What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):**
  - no sensor data and no prediction;
  - the AIG1 suggestion ledger is append-only (0097) and is the place where any prediction would be written (AI-4, *AI-First Architecture Update*);
  - billable facts come only from a signed report without an open hold (J5).
- **Safe default in force:**
  - no sensor ingestion, no prediction, no table;
  - D-35 A (no data to an AI service in the pilot);
  - D-1 (no real data);
  - D-34 A plus C (interpreting clinical content only in the qualified module).
- **Blocks:** AI domain 4 *Check-In Services* with sensors; AI capability roadmap stage 3 sensor-based risk signals.

**Sources (read 2026-11-04; numbering shared with my D-34 section of this run):**

Scope: hospitals, nursing homes and other health facilities that monitor cared-for persons; **home care and private homes are not mentioned**. Consent is desirable but **neither required nor sufficient**; informing the person about purpose and effects is central; public institutions need a cantonal legal basis, private ones rely on the DSG; monitoring must serve the cared-for person's interest (organisational reasons alone are not enough), only where milder measures are insufficient; indirect or event-based ("situational") monitoring is preferred over real time; cameras, infrared and radar are treated alike; privacy by design, documented risk analysis, regular review. **The Merkblatt "Überwachung von betreuten Personen" itself (PDF, 16.09.2026) could not be opened in this run;** statements are from the release.

**Settled (on the sources read)**
- **Data category.** Health measurements of a client are health data, sensitive personal data ([P1] Art. 5 lit. c). Even logistics sensors in a home ("door opened", presence) are personal data about everyone who lives in or visits the household and reveal their behaviour.
- **Basis and proportionality.** For monitoring in care facilities, the federal and cantonal commissioners hold that consent is neither required nor sufficient; the measure must serve the monitored person's interest, be the mildest sufficient one, and prefer event-based over real-time data [N10]. Their guide does not address home care; but the reasoning rests on the general DSG principles (purpose, proportionality, transparency, [P1] Art. 6), which apply to a private home care provider as well. **Option B's stated purpose ("may reduce no-show uncertainty") is organisational, which on [N10]'s reasoning is the weakest justification.**
- **Statement v2 conflict.** Statement v2 says NAVORA "does not monitor a person's health condition or physiological values". Options C and D do exactly that, so they are outside the core's intended purpose by its own words and belong to the D-34 module (C). On [M3] (rule 11), software that monitors physiological processes is class IIa or IIb; on [M1] "Überwachung" is a listed medical purpose. That is a reading, not a classification.
- **AI Act (only with an EU nexus):** an option-D prediction inside an MDR device of class IIa or higher would be high-risk from 2 August 2028 ([M6], [M7]).
- **Staff monitoring.** A door or presence sensor also records when the nurse arrived and left. Systematic behaviour monitoring of employees is not allowed ([N11], ArGV 3 Art. 26, secondary); per-person output from such data would breach AI-9 and, on the sources read, labour law.
- **Billing (the #817 question).** The KLV services read for D-22 ([S1] Art. 7 Abs. 2, Art. 8, Art. 8a) are services given by nurses or organisations on a needs assessment and, for category B, an order. I found no service in that text that consists of receiving device data or a prediction. So **no source read supports billing on sensor data or a prediction without a nurse's service record.** Whether a nurse's own evaluation of remotely received values is a billable KLV service is **UNKNOWN** (tariff contract, D-18).

**UNKNOWN and why**
- Whether [N10] is applied by the EDÖB or a cantonal commissioner to home care in a private household; the guide's scope does not say.
- Whether a public-law provider (Basel-Stadt, if the pilot partner holds a public mandate) needs a **cantonal legal basis** for any sensor monitoring [N10]; IDG BS not read (D-11, D-12).
- How co-residents and visitors are informed, and on what basis their data is processed; the client's consent cannot cover them, and [N10] says consent is not the basis anyway.
- Retention: whether raw readings are part of the patient record under GesG BS § 29 (10 years after end of treatment, D-4), or should not be stored at all beyond the event that matters (minimisation). UNKNOWN; adviser with D-4.
- Whether a third-party sensor is itself a medical device, and whether NAVORA's integration makes NAVORA part of a device system (MepV not read on systems).
- Liability when a value is received and not acted on (H-28): contract and tort law not read; it depends on what the provider promises the client.

**Options against the sources read**
- **A (no sensors; specification kept): compliant under every plausible interpretation.** No new data category, recipient or device question.
- **B (logistics facts only): unresolved: needs legal adviser and owner.** No health measurement, but data from a private household, other residents and staff, an organisational purpose that [N10] treats as insufficient for care facilities, and `cra`'s finding that "no movement" will be read as a welfare signal (which pulls B towards monitoring of a person). Minimum conditions if pursued: a written purpose in the client's interest; event-based facts only, no stream; no per-person output about staff; information to client, household and staff; a DPIA ([P1] Art. 22); the "not watched, not a safety signal" text `cra` requires.
- **C (health values shown as facts): unresolved: needs regulatory adviser (D-34 module, MepV classification; adviser question 10), legal adviser and owner.** Contradicts statement v2.
- **D (values plus AI predictions): unresolved: needs regulatory adviser, legal adviser, the Clinical Owner and owner**, plus D-35 (provider) for any AI service. Highest exposure: device class, AI Act with an EU nexus, D-35, H-28.
- **The rule "no billable service rests on sensor data or a prediction without a nurse's signed service record" (AI-First §5.2, owner prohibition): compliant under every plausible interpretation.** It only adds a control; billing never exceeds what a signed record supports. The opposite (billing from sensor data) is **unresolved: needs legal adviser** (KLV, tariff contract D-18).
