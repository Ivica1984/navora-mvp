# D-23 — Working-time record derived from field events

## Decision summary
Can the working-time record that Swiss labour law requires for employed nurses be kept as a working day **derived from field events** (first departure to last completion, plus the return trip and report time) that the nurse confirms or corrects once a day, instead of a separate timesheet? What must the record contain, and how long is it kept? **The PO assumes no labour-law rule.** **PO working view, not a decision:** build nothing that replaces a timesheet until `lca` has read the sources; if the derived day is admissible, it removes the timesheet and makes the North Star (care time ÷ paid working time) measurable. **Preliminary route:** `legal`, escalated to the owner if the `lca` review leaves the interpretation unresolved (no legal adviser named). Inputs requested: Engineering, Legal & Compliance. Clinical input not needed (no care rule; the record is about the nurse, not the client).

## Context and problem statement
- **The North Star is not measurable today** (`docs/product/CARE_OPERATING_MODEL.md`, PO 2026-10-22 14:30): its denominator is paid working time, which NAVORA does not record. AN1 shows a *within-visit* care share instead, labelled as such and never compared with Buurtzorg's 60 %.
- **IMPLEMENTED (F1-F4, F7, PLAN2, ABS1):** append-only visit events with times (depart, arrive, start, pause, complete; time corrections with reason), signed reports with their sign time, planned visits per member, recorded absences. What is **not** recorded: start and end of the working day, breaks, travel outside a visit chain, non-visit work (team meeting, training, administration).
- **The challenge row** "Separate timesheets for paid working time" (`NEXT_CAPABILITY.md`) proposes the derived day; it waits for this decision.
- **Care-model relevance:** a timesheet is pure administration (value 5) and the measure it would feed is the one that decides whether NAVORA beats the benchmark (value 1). Getting the legal form wrong exposes the employer and the nurse (labour inspection, overtime disputes).
- **Who employs the nurse is open** (D-12: own entity, partner organisation, or self-employed nurses). The record duty may differ or not apply for self-employed nurses (UNKNOWN; `lca`).
- **Safe default in force:** NAVORA keeps no working-time record and claims none; the employer keeps whatever record it keeps today; AN1 shows only the within-visit share.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | No working-time feature (status quo); the employer's own timesheet | no legal exposure for NAVORA | double entry for nurses; North Star unmeasurable |
| B | Derived working day from field events, confirmed or corrected by the nurse once a day (with breaks and non-visit work added), stored as the employer's record | removes the timesheet; North Star measurable; one tap per day | admissibility, required content and retention are UNKNOWN; the derivation must be correct and correctable |
| C | Derived day as a **pre-filled proposal** only; the employer's record system stays the record | lowers the burden without NAVORA becoming the record keeper | the North Star still depends on another system's data |
| D | Derived day used only for the North Star statistic (team level, never per person), no record function | measurable without a record duty | still needs the employer's timesheet; per-person data still processed (privacy) |

**What is settled (on [W1]-[W3]; the ordinance text itself was not read)**
- For employees under the ArG, the employer must keep records showing at least the duration, start and end of daily and weekly working time, including compensatory time and overtime, and breaks of 30 minutes or more ([W1], [W3]). Retention: at least five years after they cease to be valid ([W2], [W3]).
- Electronic records are allowed if the authority can still inspect them in writing or an agreed form ([W2]). Recording may be delegated to the employee; the employer stays responsible for completeness and accuracy ([W3]).
- The waiver (Art. 73a) needs a GAV, a high income and a written individual agreement ([W3]). For nurses working planned visit schedules this is plausibly not available. The simplified form (Art. 73b) needs that the employee sets a substantial part of her working time herself ([W3]); whether a home-care nurse whose visits are planned by the team meets that is UNKNOWN. **The protective assumption is full recording under Art. 73.**

**(1) Admissibility of a derived day confirmed by the nurse (option B)**
- Nothing read requires a particular medium or a manual timesheet. A record that the system pre-fills and the employee confirms or corrects is plausibly a form of delegated self-recording ([W3]). But the content must be complete: start and end of the day, breaks of 30 minutes or more with their timing, and all work, including travel within working time and non-visit work. A day derived only from visit events lacks these unless the nurse adds them. B is plausibly admissible **only as a record the nurse completes, not a derived figure she merely approves**.
- Two points stay open: whether first departure to last completion (plus return trip and report time) is the correct boundary of working time for a home-care nurse (travel from home to the first client, and back, is a classic working-time question; not read), and whether a confirmation "once a day" is timely enough. Both are **UNKNOWN**.
- **Monitoring limit:** visit events with times are already processed for care (IMPLEMENTED). Using them to build a working-time record is a new purpose for employee data (OR Art. 328b, opening words only, [W6]; DSG purpose limitation, Art. 6 Abs. 3, cited in D-9, not re-read). ArGV 3 Art. 26 bars systems meant to monitor behaviour ([W6]). A daily derived day shown to the nurse and the record keeper only is plausibly a recording, not monitoring. A per-person view of gaps between visits, for a team or a coordinator, would plausibly be monitoring. No location data should feed it (none is IMPLEMENTED).

**(2) Simplified or waived recording:** see *settled*. Neither is a product question: it needs an agreement between employer and employee (73b) or a GAV (73a). NAVORA cannot assume either.

**(3) Retention:** five years after validity ([W2], [W3]), on the employer's side. If NAVORA holds the record (B), D-4 must carry that period for this data type and the employer must be able to produce it in readable form for the inspectorate, including after the contract with NAVORA ends (export; D-12 contract).

**(4) Self-employed nurses and partnership models (D-12):** the ArG duties bind employers towards employees; a self-employed nurse is not an employee (reading of ArG scope; ArG Art. 1 not read: UNKNOWN in the primary text). A public-law provider (municipal Spitex) may fall outside the ArG under its Art. 2 and follow its cantonal or municipal staff law instead ([W5], for homes; Spitex not named). **Which regime applies depends on D-12 and D-11.** [W7] would add sector rules for employers in care if enacted (status UNKNOWN).

**(5) Team-level statistic (D):** aggregated working time per team, without a per-person view, is a different purpose from the record (DSG Art. 6 Abs. 3, not re-read). It is plausibly covered if employees are informed (DSG Art. 19, read for D-9) and the AN1 small-group suppression applies, so that no person is identifiable. Whether the employer may use the data for this purpose without the employee's agreement is plausibly yes for organisational planning, but OR Art. 328b was read only in its opening words ([W6]): **UNKNOWN**.

**(6) Missing facts:** the employer and its legal form (D-12: private, public-law, self-employed); the pilot canton (D-11) and whether a cantonal staff law or a GAV applies; the employer's existing time recording and any Art. 73b agreements; the enacted state of [W7].

**Verdicts**
- **A (status quo):** **compliant under every plausible interpretation** for NAVORA: it records nothing as a working-time record and claims none. The employer's duty is unchanged.
- **C (pre-filled proposal; the employer's system stays the record):** **compliant under every plausible interpretation** on the sources read, provided (i) the proposal is shown only to the nurse (and passed to the employer's system on her action), (ii) employees are informed of this use of visit times (DSG Art. 19), (iii) it has no per-person view for colleagues or coordinators, and (iv) no location data feeds it. NAVORA then processes the data for the employer as a processor (D-2 position).

- **B (NAVORA holds the employer's record):** **unresolved: needs legal adviser** (with D-12): questions 1-4 below.

**What is settled (on [W10], [W11]; the ordinance text itself was not read)**
