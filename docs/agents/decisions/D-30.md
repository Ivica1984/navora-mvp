# D-30 — Correcting a visit report when its author has left the team

## Decision summary
Who may correct a final visit report, and sign that correction, once the nurse who performed the visit and signed the report is no longer a member of the visit's team; and what the corrected record then shows about authorship. **No recommendation yet.** Today the correction is refused (strict default), so the final version stays in force and a wrong record cannot be corrected at all. Expected route: `clinical` (the named Clinical Owner, G-1) for who may sign; `lca` for the documentation-duty and authorship question.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** the PROVISIONAL label of F6's correction path (CR-048). **Findings:** CGR-19 (closed with the interim refusal, provisional); PS-15 (S2, mitigated-in-code, interim); H-12; related CGR-18 (removal while reports are unsigned, CR-036).

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED, read in the repository by the PO):**
- CR-012: the performer (first `started` event) alone drafts and signs a report, including a correction version (0016; 0041 `sign_report` requires the performer).
- CR-043: a signed or final report locks the visit's times and observations; they reopen only through a correction draft (`correct_report`, any team member, with a reason).
- CR-048 (interim, D-30): `correct_report` is refused with `performer_not_in_team` while the report's performer is not a member of the report's team; nothing is written; the final version stays in force (0044 §1, pgTAP 044:143).
- CR-036: removal from a team is refused while she is performer of a completed team visit without a signed or final report (so unsigned reports cannot be orphaned by removal).
- Residual (PS-15): a correction draft opened **before** she left stays open and can never be signed; it raises `report_correction_open` (CR-053, P3, not closable by hand).
- CR-045: billing and working-time facts follow the final report version; a correction is a new version and a new fact.

**Safe default in force:** no correction of a departed author's report; the final version stays the record; a stranded draft is visible as an exception.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Keep the refusal (today) | no correction after the author left | no authorship question | a known error stays in the record; the stranded draft stays forever (PS-15) |
| B A registered nurse of the team corrects and signs as **corrector** | the correction is signed by a team RN who states she did not perform the visit; the record shows original author and corrector separately; the original version stays readable | the team owns its record (autonomy); errors can be fixed | the corrector attests to something she did not see; what she may change must be limited (UNKNOWN: `clinical`) |
| C B, limited to non-clinical fields | only times, category split or payer data may be corrected by another RN; observations and care given only by the author | protects clinical authorship | the author is gone, so clinical errors stay; needs a field classification (`clinical`) |
| D Addendum instead of correction | the final version is never changed; a dated addendum by a team RN is attached and shown with it | strongest integrity; the original stays untouched | billing facts follow the final version, so a billing error cannot be fixed by an addendum |
| E Contact the former author | she signs the correction with a time-limited access | the author signs | depends on her availability and on access for a non-member (D-3, privacy) |

**What is settled (for the cantons read; not for the pilot canton)**
- *Authorship must be visible on each entry.* ZH: "Die Urheberschaft der Einträge muss unmittelbar ersichtlich sein" (§ 13 Abs. 1 [S1]). This applies to Spitex institutions through § 39 with § 35 Abs. 2 lit. c [S1]. SO: "Die Urheberschaft und der Zeitpunkt der Einträge müssen unmittelbar ersichtlich sein" ([S4], citing § 18 Abs. 1 GesG SO). BE: an electronic system must ensure authenticity, integrity and auditability, i.e. who entered, changed or deleted which data and when (Art. 4 Abs. 1 [S3]).
- *The original must stay unchanged and readable.* ZH: electronic entries must be "datiert, unabänderbar gespeichert und jederzeit abrufbar" (§ 13 Abs. 2 [S1]). For hospitals and nursing-home beds only (§ 1 [S2]; **not Spitex**, but relevant for Horizon 3, D-24): "Die Berichtigung einer Eintragung erfolgt durch eine entsprechende Ergänzung" (§ 17 Abs. 3 [S2]).
- *Retention.* ZH: 10 years after the last treatment (§ 13 Abs. 3 [S1]). BE: at least 10 years, longer for minors and for late risks (Art. 6 [S3]). SO: 20 years ([S4]). This applies to the original and to every correction (D-4).
- *Who may correct.* None of the texts read limits a correction to the original author. What they require is that the author of **each entry** is visible.
- *The client's right to rectification.* The data subject may request that incorrect personal data be corrected unless a statutory provision prohibits it. If accuracy cannot be established, the data subject may ask for the data to be marked as disputed (DSG Art. 32 Abs. 1 and 3 [S5]). ZH patients may request an addition to their record (§ 17 Abs. 4 [S2], hospitals and nursing homes).

**UNKNOWN**
- The pilot canton's documentation rules (D-11). The three cantons read share one pattern, but that does not prove the pilot canton follows it.
- Whether a professional who did not perform the visit may correct clinical content (observations, care given), on what basis (the author's statement, other records), and how the basis must be stated. This is a professional-law and clinical question (`clinical`, G-1). No text read answers it.
- Whether a correction of a billing fact after the payer was invoiced needs a payer procedure. Not read; nothing is invoiced today.

**Option verdicts**
- **A (keep the refusal; today):** compliant while data are synthetic. **Unresolved, and risky with real data.** If a client asks for a known error to be corrected (DSG Art. 32 Abs. 1 [S5]), A offers neither a correction nor a "disputed" mark. No statutory provision read prohibits the correction. A cannot meet that request.
- **B (a team RN corrects and signs as corrector; the record shows author and corrector separately; the original stays readable):** consistent with every cantonal text read and with DSG Art. 32, provided that: the original version stays unchanged and readable (IMPLEMENTED per 0041); the correction is a new, dated, signed entry by an identified corrector; the record shows that she is not the performer and states the reason and the basis; and the audit log records it. **Unresolved: needs legal adviser** for the pilot canton (D-11), and `clinical` for which fields she may correct. Low risk on the sources read.
- **C (B limited to non-clinical fields):** same verdict as B for the fields it allows. Clinical errors stay uncorrectable, so A's DSG Art. 32 risk remains for clinical fields. A "disputed" mark or an addendum for clinical fields would close that gap.
- **D (addendum only; the final version never changes):** closest to the "Berichtigung durch Ergänzung" pattern ([S2] § 17 Abs. 3), so **compliant on the documentation side under every text read**. It does not fix a billing fact: under CR-045 billing follows the final version, and the payer contracts read for D-18 pay the services actually given. An addendum that corrects billing-relevant data must therefore also change the billing fact, or D is incomplete for billing. Unresolved for billing once invoicing exists (D-18).
- **E (contact the former author; time-limited access):** **unresolved: needs legal adviser.** She is no longer part of the treating team, so access to the client's record is a disclosure of health data outside it. She stays bound by professional secrecy (§ 15 [S1] for ZH; StGB Art. 321 not read). Her past authorship alone is not shown to be a legal basis (see the D-3 section on need-to-know). The less intrusive variant has the team ask her for a statement without giving her access: the team RN then records the correction under B and names her statement as the basis. That raises no new access question.

**Verdict on the most protective option:** B or D, built as **original kept and unchanged, correction as a new dated entry, corrector identified and shown as not the performer, reason and basis recorded, audited**. It is consistent with every text read (ZH, BE, SO; DSG Art. 32), but **unresolved: needs legal adviser** because the pilot canton's documentation law (D-11) was not read, and needs `clinical` for which fields a non-performer may correct.

**Exact questions for a legal adviser:**
1. Under the pilot canton's health law, may a registered nurse of the same organisation correct or supplement a visit report written by a colleague who has left? Must the correction be an addition (*Ergänzung*) rather than a new version replacing the old one in the active record?
2. What must the corrector's entry state (role, reason, basis) so that it does not attest facts she did not observe?
3. Is a client's request for rectification of a care record (DSG Art. 32) met by an addendum or a "disputed" mark, or must the record itself be corrected, also when the original author is unavailable?

**Note to po:** (1) Option A is safe while data are synthetic, but before real clients a path for a client's rectification request is needed: at least a disputed mark or an addendum. Otherwise the strict default itself is a DSG risk. (2) The "ask her, do not give her access" variant of E may be worth a row in the options; it avoids the D-3 question. (3) The ZH patient-law rule "Berichtigung durch Ergänzung" applies to nursing homes, so it matters for Horizon 3 (D-24, D-25).
