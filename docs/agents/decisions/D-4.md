# D-4 — Audit log retention and access

## Decision summary
How long NAVORA keeps its audit log (`audit_events`), who may read which part of it, and what happens at the end of the retention period, given that the log is append-only and several billing and safety rules depend on it being complete. **PO 2026-10-24 10:11: provisional rule decided by po** (no deletion before the final rule, written retention criteria, reading by purpose, client extract as a real-data gate); **final periods `awaiting-approval: owner`**, asked once D-11 and D-12 are answered. Earlier: no recommendation, waiting for inputs. Today's default (keep everything, admins only) stays. Expected route: the PO may set a **provisional** default if `lca` finds it compliant under every plausible interpretation; the final retention period is `legal` (no adviser named → `owner`).

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** production (no real data before a retention rule exists), the access part of D-3 for audit reads. **Findings:** none open; CGR-33 (closed) made the order history depend on the audit log.

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED, read in the repository by the PO):**
- `audit_events` (0004:20-31): who, when, which table and entity, command, idempotency key, and `changed` for allowlisted columns only, so no free text enters it (0004:5-9). Append-only for every role including the owner: update, delete and truncate are refused by triggers (0004:48-63).
- Read: organisation admins only (policy `org_admins_read_audit`, 0004:44-46, "Placeholder (Q4)"). Security-definer functions read it for derived facts.
- **Dependencies on completeness:** J5's order basis as of the visit date is reconstructed from the audited history (CR-057/058, 0051; pgTAP 053 guards the order columns). A retention rule that deletes rows would silently change how an old visit's order basis is reconstructed. HAND1 and the absence measures also read changes from audited tables.
- Allowlisted columns include insurance type and medical-order status (0013:81-82), which relate to a client's care: the log is not free of sensitive data.
- No retention job exists; nothing is ever deleted.

**Safe default in force:** keep everything; admin-only read.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Keep everything, admins read (today) | as built | complete history; no rule invented | unlimited retention of personal data may itself breach data minimisation; admins read all |
| B Fixed retention aligned with the care record's retention (the period the provider must keep the record), then delete or anonymise | one period, one job | consistent with the record | the record period is cantonal (D-11); deletion must not break billing reconstruction (facts store `order_recorded` since 0051, which reduces that dependency) |
| C Split: rows needed for billing and care-record history kept with the record; operational rows (logins, idempotency, plan moves) for a shorter period | minimises what is kept | two periods to justify |
| D Access by purpose: admins for compliance; the team for its own case history through derived views only; no direct audit read for others | least access | more readers to build |

- **Reversibility.** Narrowing reads and writing criteria are reversible. Deletion is irreversible; anonymisation is irreversible for the person but keeps the structure. Neither runs before the final rule.

- **Liability interest (reading, not a duty):** claims for bodily injury or death become time-barred 20 years after the harmful act at the latest ([R3]: OR Art. 128a, Art. 60 Abs. 1bis). Keeping care evidence that long is plausibly a legitimate purpose under Art. 6 Abs. 4. No source read makes it a duty.
- **Maximum:** no fixed maximum was found. The binding limit is Art. 6 Abs. 4: once no purpose remains, the row is destroyed or **anonymised**. Anonymising `actor_id` and the client reference keeps a row's structure for statistics.

**(2) Who may read it**
- **The client, on request:** audit rows about her case are her personal data. The access right covers "die bearbeiteten Personendaten als solche" and "die Aufbewahrungsdauer … oder … die Kriterien zur Festlegung dieser Dauer" (Art. 25 Abs. 2 lit. b, d). The controller may restrict access for overriding third-party interests, such as staff identities (Art. 26 Abs. 1 lit. b). So before real data, the provider must be able to (i) extract one client's rows and (ii) state the retention period or its criteria.
- **Staff:** each row names the acting employee. The employer may process employee data only "soweit sie dessen Eignung für das Arbeitsverhältnis betreffen oder zur Durchführung des Arbeitsvertrages erforderlich sind" ([R2]). Reading the audit log to rate individual performance, as opposed to compliance, billing or safety review, is plausibly outside that (UNKNOWN; ArGV 3 Art. 26 not read). AN1 and AN2 already suppress small groups (ADR-070), which is the protective direction.
- **Admins (today, all rows):** no source read forbids it. The proportionality principle (Art. 6 Abs. 2, cited for D-3) and DSV Art. 4 Abs. 5 for logs ("only those who check data protection or data security") point to purpose-bound reading. **Option D is the protective one.**
- **The team, for its own case history, through derived views:** consistent with its need-to-know (D-3 B). No objection found.

**(3) "Keep everything, admins only" (option A)**
- **Synthetic data (today): compliant under every plausible interpretation.**
- **Real data, as a permanent rule: unresolved, and likely incompatible with DSG Art. 6 Abs. 4.** Unlimited retention with no stated purpose end conflicts with Art. 6 Abs. 4. It also leaves the provider unable to answer Art. 25 Abs. 2 lit. d.
- **Real data, as a provisional rule for the pilot: compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read**, provided all of the following hold:
  - no deletion before the final rule, because every minimum found (1, 10, 20 years) is longer than any pilot;
  - the retention **criteria** are written down and can be told to a client: "care-record rows: the pilot canton's record period after the last treatment; billing evidence: 10 years after the business year; operational rows: until the final rule, at most N";
  - reading is narrowed by purpose (option D);
  - a client's rows can be extracted on request.
- The cantonal period of the pilot canton remains UNKNOWN (D-11). It can only lengthen the minimum, so it does not break this provisional rule.

**Options B, C, D (legal remarks)**
- **B** (one period, the record's): plausible for care-record rows. Too long for operational rows (Art. 6 Abs. 4) and possibly too short for billing evidence (10 years from the end of the business year can outlast 10 years after the last treatment).
- **C** (split periods): closest to Art. 6 Abs. 4. It needs the classification of each column as record, billing evidence or operational. That is `eng` and `cra` work, and the legal adviser confirms the two UNKNOWNs in the table.
- **D** (purpose-bound access): the protective option for access, combinable with B or C.
- **Deletion is irreversible.** Anonymisation is an allowed alternative under Art. 6 Abs. 4 and keeps the billing reconstruction testable for rows that are no longer personal. Any deletion of rows still needed for a billed visit within the 10 years would destroy evidence.

**What is settled (on the sources read):**
1. **An extract the system generates is an allowed form.** Information "erfolgt schriftlich oder in der Form, in der die Daten vorliegen", and request and answer "können auf elektronischem Weg erfolgen" ([V1] Art. 16 Abs. 2, 3). It must be "in einer verständlichen Form" (Abs. 4). Raw audit rows (`entity_type`, JSON `changed`) are not understandable without explanation. [A2] section II says unusual formats may be made understandable by explanations. So the extract needs plain labels or a legend.
2. **The rows alone are not the full answer.** "In jedem Fall" the client must also receive (Art. 25 Abs. 2, [A2]): the controller's identity and contact details (lit. a); the purpose (c); the retention period or its criteria (d: the provisional criteria of this package); the origin of data not collected from her (e); and the recipients or categories of recipients (g). Lit. a depends on who the controller is (D-12, UNKNOWN). The audit extract also covers only part of her data. The care record, visits and reports fall under the same right. The extract is one component of the answer, not the answer.
3. **Identification and delivery.** "Der Verantwortliche muss angemessene Massnahmen treffen, um die betroffene Person zu identifizieren" ([V1] Art. 16 Abs. 5). [A2] section II adds that the data must be protected from unauthorised third parties in transit (DSG Art. 8). The "recorded request" must therefore record how the identity was checked, and the delivery channel must reach only her.
4. **Deadline.** The answer is due "innerhalb von 30 Tagen seit dem Eingang des Begehrens" ([V1] Art. 18 Abs. 1; DSG Art. 25 Abs. 7). An extension must be announced with a new deadline (Art. 18 Abs. 2). A refusal, restriction or deferral (masking included) must be communicated "innerhalb derselben Frist" (Abs. 3). The request record needs its receipt time.
5. **Free of charge** (Art. 25 Abs. 6). **Health data** may, with her consent, be communicated through a health professional she designates (Art. 25 Abs. 3, [A2] IV.A). The extract should not rule that route out.
6. **Processor.** If NAVORA processes for a provider, the provider stays obliged to answer, and the processor supports it (Art. 25 Abs. 4; [V1] Art. 17 Abs. 2). A generator built into the platform is that support. Who answers depends on D-12.
7. **Completeness.** [A2] section III: the controller bears the burden of proving that information it describes as complete is complete. An extract labelled "all audit rows of your case" is a completeness statement. It is safer to label what it covers (for example "the change history of records X, Y between dates").

**Where point (c) as written does not yet fit:**
- **"No administrator reads its content on the way" against the weighing.** Masking staff identities is lawful only as a restriction weighed in the individual case, with reasons given (DSG Art. 26 Abs. 1 lit. b and Abs. 4, [A1]; my 10:47 point 1, which (c) adopts). To weigh, the request handler must at least see which identities and third-party mentions would be withheld. The same applies to free text in her record that names relatives or other persons. Read literally, (c) forbids that read. Read as "no read beyond what the recorded weighing for this request needs, through the purpose reader with `compliance`, recorded", it is consistent. **Whether a weighing on the masked extract plus the list of withheld categories is enough, without reading the free text, is UNKNOWN** (question 6).
- **AUD1-4 and the extract.** AUD1-4 withholds a team's free text from administrators outside the team. That is consistent with the extract **only** if the generator returns the client's own text to her and the handler's read is limited as above. I found no conflict in principle. Data protection does not forbid giving a person her own data, and StGB 321 protects her secret against others, not against her. StGB 321 was not re-read this run.

**Risks:**
- An extract that is silent about a mask breaks Art. 26 Abs. 4.
- An extract with rows only breaks Art. 25 Abs. 2 lit. a, c-e and g.
- A weak identity check discloses health data, which are particularly sensitive (DSG Art. 5 lit. c), to the wrong person.
- A request answered after 30 days with no notice breaks Art. 18.
- None of this applies on synthetic data. r1 is a real-data gate.
