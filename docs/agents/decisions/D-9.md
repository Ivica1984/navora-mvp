# D-9 — Legal basis for processing client data at intake

## Decision summary
On which legal basis NAVORA (and the provider using it) may receive, store and pass on a client's data from the moment a referral arrives: the client's explicit consent, the care mandate (treatment contract) with professional secrecy, a cantonal health-law duty, or a combination; who must capture that basis (the referrer, intake, the team) and in which form; and what may be stored before it exists. **PO 2026-10-24 10:11: option D (protective variant) recommended, `awaiting-approval: owner`;** the referral-stage real-data gates are decided by po. ## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Consent as the basis (today's gate) | explicit consent of the client (or her representative) before a case exists; recorded who, when, how | clear, visible to the client | consent can be withdrawn while care must continue; a person without capacity cannot consent; a hospital discharge on a Friday waits for a signature |
| B Care mandate / treatment contract | processing is necessary to perform the care the client asked for; consent only for what goes beyond it | matches how care works; no consent loop | needs a mandate before data flows; what exactly "necessary" covers is legal |
| C Statutory duty of the provider (cantonal health law, documentation duty) | the provider must keep a record once it treats | strongest for the record itself | does not cover the referral before the provider accepts; canton-specific (D-11) |
| D Combination: referral transfer on the referrer's basis (hospital discharge duty), the provider's processing on B/C, consent only for sharing beyond the team (e.g. COVER1, network) | each step on its own basis | fits REF2 and the care network | most to explain and build |

**What is settled (federal, on [L1])**
- Health data are sensitive personal data (DSG Art. 5 lit. c Ziff. 2, cited for D-3).
- A private controller needs no "legal basis" for each processing in the way the GDPR requires one. Processing is unlawful (a *Persönlichkeitsverletzung*) in particular when it breaches the principles of Art. 6 and 8, when it runs against the person's express objection, or when sensitive data are disclosed to third parties (Art. 30 Abs. 2 lit. a-c). A violation is justified by consent, an overriding private or public interest, or by law (Art. 31 Abs. 1).
- Processing data about the contract partner "in unmittelbarem Zusammenhang mit dem Abschluss oder der Abwicklung eines Vertrags" is a listed overriding interest (Art. 31 Abs. 2 lit. a). That is option B.
- Where consent is required, it is valid only "für eine oder mehrere bestimmte Bearbeitungen nach angemessener Information freiwillig". For sensitive data it must be **express** (Art. 6 Abs. 6, 7 lit. a). No form is prescribed, so express consent does not have to be written.
- Duty to inform: also when the data do not come from the person (Art. 19 Abs. 1). At minimum: identity of the controller, the purpose, and the recipients or categories of recipients (Art. 19 Abs. 2). It does not apply where the person already has the information, where processing is provided for by law, or where the controller "eine private Person … die gesetzlich zur Geheimhaltung verpflichtet ist" (Art. 20 Abs. 1 lit. a-c).
- Data no longer needed for the purpose are destroyed or anonymised (Art. 6 Abs. 4). Relevant to D-4 and to referrals that never become a case.
- A processor may process only as the controller itself may, and only if "keine gesetzliche oder vertragliche Geheimhaltungspflicht die Übertragung verbietet" (Art. 9 Abs. 1 lit. a-b). Sub-processors need the controller's prior approval (Art. 9 Abs. 3).
- Controllers and processors keep a record of processing activities (Art. 12 Abs. 1). The exemption for fewer than 250 employees is for the Federal Council to set (Abs. 5), and its conditions (DSV) were not read.
- Cantonal record duties exist in the cantons read: ZH § 13 [S1], SO § 18 [S4], BE PatV [S3]. They make the care record a **statutory** duty of the treating professional or institution, with retention of 10 to 20 years. That is option C, and it is canton-specific.

**What is settled for professional secrecy (cantonal guidance, examples only)**
- [L11] 1.5.1 lists *Pflegefachpersonen* among the professions bound by StGB Art. 321. Their *Hilfspersonen* are bound too, and holding a practice licence is not a condition. This answers the D-3 UNKNOWN in the same direction as [L5] 2.3. It is not a reading of the statute, which was not read.
- Disclosure to a follow-up provider that was not involved before needs the patient to be informed and to agree ([L11] 3.2.3). Referral to a doctor needs the patient's consent, which should be documented in the treatment record but need not be written ([L11] 3.2.1). Disclosure to relatives needs the patient's agreement, which may sometimes be tacit ([L11] 3.2.4). Without consent or a statutory basis, release comes from the cantonal authority ([L11] 3.1, 3.3).
- Neither guide addresses exchange **within** the treating team or institution, or a hospital passing a discharge report to home care. Both are UNKNOWN.

**(1) Which option is a valid basis for each step**
- **(a) Receiving the referral.**
  - Whether the referrer may send the data depends on the referrer's own secrecy and its basis. For a hospital or GP sending to a new home-care provider, the guide read requires the patient to be informed and to agree ([L11] 3.2.3). NAVORA cannot make that disclosure lawful afterwards.
  - On the receiving side, collecting the data is processing by the provider (or by NAVORA, see question 4). It needs the Art. 19 information (exceptions in Art. 20 Abs. 1) and data minimisation (Art. 6 Abs. 2-3; Art. 7 Abs. 3, cited for D-3).
  - **A referral that is never converted** falls under Art. 6 Abs. 4: it has no purpose left. Today nothing deletes it.
  - Option D (referrer's own basis, typically the patient's agreement to the referral) matches this. Verdict: plausible. **UNKNOWN** whether NAVORA must see evidence of the referrer's basis. REF2's "consent captured by the referrer" is the protective form.
- **(b) Creating the case and (c) the care record.** Once the client has asked for care, processing for that care is contract performance (Art. 31 Abs. 2 lit. a). Keeping the record is also a statutory duty in the cantons read (option C). Consent (A) is not needed for either if the processing stays within the principles (Art. 30 Abs. 2 lit. a). **Caveat (UNKNOWN):** Art. 31 Abs. 2 lit. a speaks of the "Vertragspartnerin". Where a representative contracts for a client without capacity, that link is not read (ZGB not read).
- **(d) Passing data on.**
  - Inside the same controller (another team of the same organisation): see D-3 (b′). Not a third party on the reading there, but under professional secrecy it is UNKNOWN without consent. Recorded consent is the protective form.
  - To a doctor or another provider: disclosure to a third party of sensitive data (Art. 30 Abs. 2 lit. c) needing justification. Under secrecy that means consent ([L11] 3.2.1, 3.2.3), a statutory basis, or an authority's release. Consent recorded per recipient category is the protective form.
- **Option A alone (consent as the only basis)** is valid for (b)-(d) when the consent meets Art. 6 Abs. 6-7. It has three legal weaknesses on the sources read:
  - it cannot cover a client who cannot consent;
  - it suggests that a withdrawal ends the record, which the statutory record duty does not allow;
  - it does not cover (a), which is the referrer's disclosure.
- **Option D** assigns each step to the basis the sources read actually support: (a) referrer, (b)/(c) contract and statutory duty, (d) consent. It is the closest fit.

**(2) Today's gate (boolean "consent confirmed", who and when)**
- **Synthetic data (today): compliant under every plausible interpretation.** No real person's data are processed.
- **Real data: unresolved: needs legal adviser.** The gate is not unlawful in itself. Express consent may be oral, and the gate records who confirmed it and when. But it does not show what was consented to (Art. 6 Abs. 6: "bestimmte Bearbeitungen"), what information was given (Art. 19 Abs. 2), or who gave it (client or representative). It also has no withdrawal state. And it sits **after** the referral's free-text need data are already stored.

**(3) What a withdrawal of consent requires**
- From then on, processing that rested only on consent must stop: the cover read (D-3 b′), sharing with a doctor or network, any optional use. Continuing it would run against an express objection (Art. 30 Abs. 2 lit. b).
- Processing on another basis continues: the care record under the cantonal duty and its retention, and billing evidence (D-4). The withdrawal does not delete them. The client is told which processing continues and why (Art. 19, Art. 25 Abs. 2 lit. c, d).
- Ending the care contract itself is a separate act. Whether care can continue without the consent depends on what was consented to.
- **UNKNOWN:** the pilot canton's rule on withdrawal for record contents (D-11).

**(4) Missing facts, and who supplies them**
- The pilot canton: its record, secrecy and, where a public mandate applies, data-protection law (D-11, `owner`).
- The provider, and whether NAVORA is its processor or a controller of its own (D-12, `owner`). If NAVORA's operator holds referrals before any provider accepts them (the `partner_requests` stage), it may be a controller for that step, with its own Art. 19 duty. That is UNKNOWN.
- Whether a home-care provider may use a processor at all under professional secrecy (Art. 9 Abs. 1 lit. b; D-12 question 1).
- Whether a hospital needs the patient's agreement before referring (pilot canton; [L11] 3.2.3 suggests yes in BE).

**Verdict on the most protective option.** The protective variant of D is:
- (a) the referrer confirms, per referral, that the client was informed and agreed (REF2), and a referral that is not converted is deleted after a short, stated period;
- (b)/(c) the case and record on contract and statutory duty, with the Art. 19 information given at intake and recorded;
- (d) express consent recorded per recipient category (other teams of the organisation for cover, the treating doctor, the network), each refusable and withdrawable, with the withdrawal stopping only those flows;
- the representative recorded where the client cannot consent.

On the federal sources read, it is **compliant under every plausible interpretation for the steps NAVORA controls**. It stays **unresolved: needs legal adviser and owner** on three points outside NAVORA's control:
1. professional secrecy and the processor question (D-12);
2. the pilot canton's law (D-11);
3. the referrer's disclosure.

## Recommended default for NAVORA
**PO 2026-10-24 10:11 (escalated, `awaiting-approval: owner`). Recommendation: option D, the protective variant `lca` describes**, as the intake model for real clients. Each step rests on its own basis:
- **(a) Referral:** the referrer confirms per referral that the client was informed and agreed (REF2). A referral that is not converted expires and is anonymised after a stated period.
- **(b)/(c) Case and care record:** on the care contract and the provider's statutory record duty. The DSG Art. 19 information is given at intake and recorded (text version, when, by whom).
- **(d) Sharing beyond the treating team** (other teams for cover, the treating doctor, the network): express consent per recipient category, each refusable and withdrawable. A withdrawal stops only those flows.
- **Representative:** recorded where the client cannot consent.

*Why D:*
- It is the only option that fits a Friday hospital discharge (no signature loop before care starts: continuity, value 2) **and** a client without capacity.
- It does not suggest that a withdrawal erases the record.
- It removes intake's job of "ticking consent" (challenge row 05:15): the basis is captured where it arises, by the referrer and at the care contract.
