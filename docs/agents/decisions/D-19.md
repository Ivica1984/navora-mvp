# D-19 — On-site protocol when the client does not open the door

## Decision summary
What a nurse must do on site when a client does not open: whom to call, in what order, how long to wait, when to call emergency services, and whether a care assistant may handle it alone. This is a **clinical protocol**; only the named Clinical Owner can approve it (D-27). NAVORA invents none of it. The package prepares the decision: what is built, which parts are product (how NAVORA records and shows the steps), and which legal questions sit inside (disclosure to an emergency contact, entering the home, who is reachable). Inputs requested: Engineering, `cra`, `lca`.

## Context and problem statement
- IMPLEMENTED (C1, 0024): `record_no_show` from `on_the_way` only, with a note; `record_no_show_outcome` with `client_reached`, `emergency_contact_informed`, `emergency_services_called` or `other` plus a note; a no-show without an outcome is a P1 exception (0024:534-536); since 0060 a P1 `no_show` safety signal goes to the responsible member and the present team members, then Operations (SIG1). CR-014 (UNVALIDATED, owner D-19). PS-4 (S1, open): "no protocol".
- **CGR-43 (`cra` 14:27, PR #294):** any recipient, a care assistant included, can take a P1 no-show signal and stop the step to Operations. The PO decided a protective interim on 2026-10-24 15:10 (SIG1-C1: a take by a member who is not a registered nurse does not stop the step for `no_show` and `visit_interrupted`) and put the question to the Clinical Owner in D-28 and here (question 4).
- PO 2026-10-22 14:30 (row): please confirm that a no-show may be recorded once the nurse has set off (`on_the_way`), not only after arrival; C1 allows that because earlier escalation is the safe side.
- Safe default in force: C1 records and signals only; NAVORA gives no instruction of its own; the provider's existing on-site practice applies (UNKNOWN to NAVORA).

**Legal check (step 4, po 2026-10-24):** yes. (a) Telling an emergency contact or a neighbour that the client did not open is a disclosure of health-related data and touches professional secrecy (StGB Art. 321) and the DSG; (b) entering the home (key safe, locksmith, police) touches the client's rights; (c) whether staff must remain reachable for the follow-up after working time is the Pikett question of D-32 (`lca` 10:47). So `lca` is asked.

**What is settled (on [N2], with [N3]-[N5] as cantonal readings)**
- Registered nurses are bound by professional secrecy (Art. 321 Ziff. 1 names "Pflegefachpersonen", version in force 2026). Their *Hilfspersonen* are bound too ([N2]; defined in [N3] 4.1 and [N5] 1.5.1 as persons who support the professional in her work). Read together, this covers every member of the team, care assistants included. That a care assistant counts as a *Hilfsperson* of a nurse is a reading of the guides, not of a court decision.
- Disclosure is not punishable with the client's consent, or with a written release by the authority (Ziff. 2). Federal and cantonal reporting rights stay reserved (Ziff. 3). A release takes a written, reasoned request and a hearing ([N3] 7), so it does not help at the door.
- An act that saves a person from an immediate danger that cannot be averted otherwise is lawful if it protects higher-value interests ([N1] Art. 17). [N4] 6 says reliance on it must be limited to such cases.
- Under data-protection law, disclosing sensitive data to third parties needs a justification: consent, an overriding interest or a law ([L1] Art. 30 Abs. 2 lit. c, Art. 31 Abs. 1). Consent to processing sensitive data must be express ([L1] Art. 6 Abs. 7).

**(1) Telling an emergency contact or a neighbour that the client did not open**
- That a person receives home care, and missed a visit, is information the team has because of its work. On the protective reading it is a secret under Art. 321. Whether that small a fact is a secret was not checked in case law (UNKNOWN).
- **With consent recorded at intake** for a named person: covered by Art. 321 Ziff. 2 and DSG Art. 31 Abs. 1, within the scope the client agreed to. This is the only basis that works in every canton read.
- **Without recorded consent, the cantons read differ:**
  - BE: consent to inform relatives can be tacit, for example when the client herself involved them ([N5] 3.2.4). If the client cannot be asked, it depends on the circumstances, and is more likely if relatives were involved while she was still capable ([N5] 3.3.5.1).
  - BS: presumed consent needs that the patient would have consented ([N4] 3.2). Necessary medical information to the closest relatives is a reporting right only "sofern der Patient oder eine vertretungsberechtigte Person" has not objected (GesG BS § 27 Abs. 2, as quoted in [N4] 4.2; the quote is cut there).
  - SO: the guide reads the presumed consent for relatives in GesG SO § 31 Abs. 2 as applying to a **deceased** person ([N3] 5). On that reading there is no presumption for informing a living client's relatives.
- **A neighbour** is not a relative in any rule read. Without consent, the only basis read is Art. 17. Whether calling a neighbour (for example for a key) is "nicht anders abwendbar" when emergency services could be called instead is UNKNOWN.
- **Emergency services (144) or police (117)**, when a person may be lying injured: Art. 17 is a plausible basis, and very little needs to be disclosed (address, that a planned visit was not answered). None of the guides read addresses this case directly ([N5] does not mention the emergency services; [N4] refers to Notstand). So it is plausible, not settled.
- A danger report to the adult-protection authority (KESB) is a different step. In BE the professional must first be released, except in a serious danger under ZGB Art. 453 ([N5] 3.3.2, 2.4.4; Art. 453 not read).

**(2) Entering the home; calling police or emergency services; documentation**
- Entering without the client's agreement touches StGB Art. 186 (not read) and cantonal police law (not read). Who may force entry is therefore UNKNOWN to `lca`. One provider's terms read ([N6] Ziff. 5.3) say: if the door cannot be opened and something may have happened, the police are involved, and the client bears the cost of an emergency opening. This shows that the provider's contract governs it (D-12), not NAVORA.
- Entry with a key the client deposited is plausibly covered by the client's consent in the care contract. The scope of that consent depends on the contract (UNKNOWN, D-12).
- Documentation: the nursing record must show the course of care (cantonal record duties read for D-30 and D-9: ZH § 13, SO § 18, BE PatV). What a no-show entry must contain is not stated in any source read. The protective content is: time of arrival, attempts made, who was contacted and on which basis (recorded consent, or emergency), whether anyone entered, and the outcome. C1's outcome codes and note cover part of it (IMPLEMENTED); B would cover the rest (PLANNED).
- Key-safe **codes** are security data. Storing them in NAVORA would make NAVORA a key holder (DSG Art. 8 data security, not re-read). The protective form records only that an arrangement exists and who holds the key. That is a reading, not a rule.
- Whether a nurse who leaves after a no-show without further steps risks criminal or civil liability (StGB Art. 128, a duty of care under the care contract) is UNKNOWN: not read. It is a reason for B, not a rule for its content.

**(3) Follow-up after working time**
- Answered for SIG1/SIG2 in D-32 (`lca` 10:47, 15:05; [P12]). Time spent on the follow-up, on site or on the phone, is working time. An **expectation** that a member reacts outside her planned working time is, in substance, Pikett: planned, limited and paid by the employer. The PO's SIG2-12 rule (outside the app only within recorded watched hours) applies to the no-show signal too. No new legal question.

**(4) A care assistant handling a no-show alone (CGR-43)**
- No source read makes this a legal rule. Secrecy binds her like the nurse (above). Who may take the decision to call emergency services or the police is a clinical and organisational rule (D-28, D-10 on delegation and supervision). SIG1-C1 (the Operations step is not stopped by a non-RN take) adds no legal risk; it adds a recipient within the provider.

**Verdicts**
- **Option A as built, plus SIG1-C1:** **compliant under every plausible interpretation** for what NAVORA itself does. It records outcomes within the provider's care record and signals within the provider (owning team, then Operations). It discloses nothing to anyone outside the provider. It does not make a nurse's disclosure at the door lawful or unlawful; that stays with the provider and the canton. (The caveat on exchange between teams of the same provider, D-3 and D-9, applies to the Operations step as it applies to every P1.)
- **Option B (the Clinical Owner's protocol, shown and recorded):** the product shape is compliant as a recording mechanism. Any step that discloses to a contact, a neighbour, the police or the emergency services, or that enters the home, is **unresolved: needs legal adviser** for the pilot canton and the provider's contract (questions 1-4 below).
- **Option C (intake data: emergency contact with consent, key arrangement, habits):** recorded, express consent per named contact is **the most protective basis read**, valid in every canton read. Its form and scope are **unresolved: needs legal adviser** (question 3), and it depends on D-9. Key-safe codes should not be stored.

**Exact questions for a legal adviser (for the pilot canton, D-11, and the provider's contract, D-12):**
1. A home-care nurse or care assistant arrives for an agreed visit and the client does not open. No consent was recorded. May she tell (a) a relative named in the record, (b) a neighbour, that the visit did not take place, or ask them for a key? On which basis: StGB Art. 321 Ziff. 2-3, the cantonal health law (presumed consent, reporting rights), or StGB Art. 17?
2. May she call the emergency services or the police and give the client's name, address and the fact of home care, without consent? Is StGB Art. 17 the basis, or a cantonal reporting right?
3. What consent, taken at intake, covers question 1 and entry with a deposited key: its wording, recipients, scope and form? Is express oral consent, documented in the care record, enough (DSG Art. 6 Abs. 7)?
4. Who may decide to force entry (only the police, under the cantonal police law)? Who bears the cost? Must the care contract say so?
5. Can a nurse who leaves after a no-show without further steps be liable, under StGB Art. 128 or the duty of care under the care contract?
