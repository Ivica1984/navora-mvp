# D-1 — Live database: real people? key rotation, leaked-password protection

## Decision summary
Three things about the live Supabase project that the repository describes but does not manage: (1) a **fact only the owner knows**: do the 6 cases and 3 care notes (and the referral, matching and quality rows) concern real people? (2) **rotate** the anon/JWT key (SR-12); (3) **enable leaked-password protection**. The PO's first triage: (2) and (3) are protective and cheap, but they are changes to a live system, which only the owner may execute (COORDINATION rule 5; agents never touch the live database). (1) decides whether a data-protection assessment is needed and, through D-7, whether the repository may stay public. **Preliminary route: `owner`** (facts only the owner knows; live system). Inputs requested: Engineering, Legal & Compliance. No clinical input: no care rule is involved.

**Settled (on the sources read)**
- If **any** row concerns a real, identifiable person, it is personal data ([S1] Art. 5 lit. a); care notes and cases about a person's care are health data, so **sensitive** (Art. 5 lit. c Ziff. 2). A real person under a changed name is still personal data ([S2] Ziff. 1.6.1: pseudonymised data remain personal data).
- The pre-containment state (anyone with the public anon key could read, insert and modify) is, for real data, a situation the definition covers: data "made accessible" to unauthorised persons is a breach in its own right ([S1] Art. 5 lit. h; [S2] Introduction). Whether proof of actual access is needed is not addressed by [S2]; that access cannot be ruled out (24 h of logs) is a fact, not an answer: UNKNOWN, legal adviser.
- If it is a breach likely to cause a high risk, **the controller** reports it to the FDPIC **as soon as possible**, with no fixed deadline, and **doubt is not a reason to wait** ([S1] Art. 24 Abs. 1; [S2] Ziff. 1.3, 1.6). For health data the FDPIC assumes a high risk "in many cases" ([S2] Ziff. 1.6.1). Data subjects are informed when necessary for their protection or when the FDPIC requires it, possibly even without a high risk ([S1] Art. 24 Abs. 4; [S2] Ziff. 2.1). A processor reports to the controller, not to the FDPIC (Art. 24 Abs. 3; [S2] Ziff. 1.2).
- The controller documents every breach (facts, effects, measures) and keeps the documentation at least two years from the report ([S3], DSV Art. 15 Abs. 4, secondary). The documentation duty concerns the **facts**, not keeping the affected rows themselves.
- Measures taken **before** a report count in the risk assessment; measures taken later do not ([S2] Ziff. 1.6). Rotating the key and enabling leaked-password protection are measures of the kind Art. 8 Abs. 1-2 asks for ([S1]).
- If every row is invented test data (no real person behind any row, not even renamed), the DSG does not apply to those rows ([S1] Art. 5 lit. a), and none of the duties above arises.

**UNKNOWN**

The source decides who the **controller** is: the owner personally, an employer or care organisation whose data they are, or a future NAVORA entity (D-12). - Whether "made accessible" without proof of access is enough here, and whether the risk is high (number of persons small; data sensitive; exposure duration unknown, at least until 2026-10-21; [S2] Ziff. 1.6.1 criteria).
- The consolidated DSG and the DSV on fedlex (not read).
