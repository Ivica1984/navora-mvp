# D-33 — Safety-signal push to Safari/iOS and Firefox

## Decision summary
D-32 (decided by po 2026-10-24 09:12) allows web push of the fixed SIG1 text only to Google and Microsoft push endpoints. This package asks whether members on **Safari (macOS, iOS, iPadOS) and Firefox** may also receive it. `lca` 08:47 found that **unresolved: needs legal adviser** (question 1 below). No legal adviser is named, so it goes to the `owner`. **Recommendation:** for the pilot, keep those members in-app only (the D-32 default) and give question 1 to a legal adviser once the pilot entity is chosen (D-12), because the answer likely depends on who the controller is. If most pilot members use iPhones, the owner may prefer to ask the adviser earlier.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** outside-the-app delivery for members whose browser uses Apple's or Mozilla's push service; lifting go-live condition SIG1-8 for teams where those members carry the early signals. **Finding:** CGR-37 (open). **Today (IMPLEMENTED):** nothing leaves the app; SIG1 itself is approved and unstarted. **Safe default in force:** those members get signals in the app only; SIG1-8 stays (a named person has `/field`, `/team` or OPS1 open in every planned hour); `/team` shows per member whether outside delivery is active (D-32 point 4).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Keep in-app only for Safari/iOS and Firefox (D-32 default) | no send to Apple or Mozilla endpoints | compliant under every interpretation (`lca`); no adviser cost now | iPhone users (likely many) get no outside signal; SIG1-8 stays longer for teams that rely on them |
| B Send with the member's express opt-in consent (DSG Art. 17 Abs. 1 lit. a) | the information text names the vendor and country; opt-in per member | covers every device | consent in an employment relationship is a known point of doubt (`lca`); unresolved |
| C Rely on the vendor's own SCCs (Art. 16 Abs. 2) | no own safeguard | no contract work | `lca`: not shown that the vendor's SCCs cover a third party's send; unresolved |
| D Native app or SMS instead for these members | another channel | avoids the browser push vendors | paid contract or an app build; `owner` (cost) |

## Legal/compliance impact
From `lca` 2026-10-24 08:47 in [D-32](D-32.md) (*Legal/compliance impact*, re-check), not repeated here. **Verdict:** A compliant under every plausible interpretation; B and C **unresolved: needs legal adviser**. Question 1 (verbatim from `lca`): "A Swiss controller sends a web-push message (fixed text, end-to-end encrypted payload per RFC 8291) to an endpoint run by Apple, Mozilla or another vendor that is not DPF-certified for Swiss data. The vendor sees the endpoint, the time and the length. (a) Is that a disclosure abroad under DSG Art. 16? (b) If yes, does the vendor's own SCC framework cover it, or must NAVORA conclude its own safeguard? (c) Is the member's express opt-in consent (Art. 17 Abs. 1 lit. a) a sufficient basis in an employment relationship, when the in-app channel stays available?" **Missing facts:** whether Apple's push service for Swiss senders is operated by Apple Distribution International (Ireland) or Apple Inc. (`eng`, from Apple's developer terms); the pilot members' devices; the controller (D-12).
