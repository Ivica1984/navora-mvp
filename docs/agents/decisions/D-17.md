# D-17 — Safety alerts a nurse sees before the first visit

## Decision summary
Which safety information must a nurse see on her visit card before she enters a client's home for the first time (examples in the row: allergies, fall risk, infection precautions, emergency contact, access or key arrangement)? Who records it at intake, in which form, and when it is reviewed. **Which items are clinically required is a clinical rule: the PO sets none.** **PO working view, not a decision:** a short, fixed list of **categories** (each with a structured yes / no / unknown and a short note), captured at intake, shown on the `/field` card of every visit of that case, changes visible in HAND1; **nothing diagnostic, no scoring, no recommendation** (D-34). Separate the **non-clinical** items (access or key arrangement, emergency contact, language) that are organisational and already partly captured, from the **clinical** items, whose list only the Clinical Owner approves. **Preliminary route:** `clinical` for the clinical list; the PO may decide the non-clinical part and the display mechanics if `cra` confirms they are not a clinical rule. Inputs requested: Engineering, Clinical & Regulatory. Legal input requested only on one point (health data at intake, linked to D-9 and D-3).

## Context and problem statement
- **IMPLEMENTED:** J1 intake captures `access_notes` and `language_notes` as free text on the case (migration 0017); the baseline client table has `emergency_contact_name` and `emergency_contact_phone` (0001); the no-show outcome codes include `emergency_contact_informed` (0024). No structured safety alert exists; the J1 brief criterion "Safety alerts for the first visit (the list `clinical` sets in D-17) … appear on the nurse's visit card" is open (J1 accepted 2026-10-21 with that criterion waiting for D-17).
- **What is shown today on `/field`:** what the field slice shows from the case (F1-F4, HAND1); whether `access_notes` reach the card is to be confirmed by `eng`.
- **Care-model relevance:** the first visit is the riskiest (H-hazards: the nurse does not know the client); a self-organised team has no coordinator who briefs her by phone, so the card must carry what a briefing would (values 1, 2 and patient safety). The challenge: replace the intake coordinator's phone briefing with structured data recorded once.
- **D-15 overlap:** D-15's recommendation adds three `cra` readiness items (treating doctor named, emergency contact, access notes) to the J1 checklist. D-17 must reuse those fields, not create a second record of the emergency contact or access arrangement.
- **Related:** D-34 (medical device: alerts must not interpret clinical information); D-3 (who may read health data; a covering nurse's read, COVER1); D-9 (legal basis at intake); D-15 (J1 readiness checklist: is a completed alert list a readiness item?).
- **Safe default in force:** no structured alerts; free-text access and language notes; the nurse learns the rest on site or from the referral.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Status quo: free-text notes only | nothing to approve | no structure; easy to miss; no review |
| B | Fixed list of categories with yes / no / unknown plus a short note, captured at intake, on every visit card, changes in HAND1; the clinical categories approved by the Clinical Owner | one recording replaces the phone briefing; "unknown" is visible, not silent; reviewable | needs the clinical list; health data at intake (D-9) |
| C | B, plus "unknown" for any clinical category blocks readiness (J1) | forces the question before the first visit | may delay urgent starts (hospital discharge); readiness rules are D-15 |
| D | Alerts derived from the assessment (interRAI) | no double entry | interprets clinical information (D-34); assessment instrument open (D-13) |

## Clinical impact
*requested 2026-10-24 (po).* Asked of `cra`: (1) which of the row's examples are clinical items (allergies, fall risk, infection precautions) and which are organisational (access, key, emergency contact, language) and so within product authority; (2) hazards of A and of an "unknown" shown without a block (B) versus a block (C), including urgent discharges; (3) whether yes / no / unknown with a note is a safe form or whether a category needs a structured detail (for example, which precaution); (4) the review trigger (each care-plan review, a quality event, a hospital return?); (5) what exactly the Clinical Owner must approve (the list, the form, the review trigger), and the related findings (CGR, H, PS).

**cra 2026-10-24 22:26 (copied from `reports/CLINICAL_GOVERNANCE.md` by po)** (branch `gov/review-2026-10-24-2226`, PR #321, not yet merged; copied verbatim):

- **(1) Classification of the row's examples.** **Clinical:** allergies, fall risk, infection precautions. Their list and their form are for the Clinical Owner, who may add items; I add none. **Organisational (product authority, with D-9 and D-19 for consent):** access or key arrangement (never a key-safe code), emergency contact (with the "may be informed" flag), language. They are recorded once and reused, never duplicated (D-15).
- **(2) Hazards.**
  - **A (free text only):** a missing entry reads as "nothing known". That is the main cause of H-03 (S1).
  - **B ("unknown" shown, no block):** the gap is visible, and the nurse can ask before or at the visit. Two failure modes. If "no" and "unknown" are not visually distinct, "unknown" reads as "no". If most items are "unknown", nurses learn to ignore the panel (alert fatigue).
  - **C (an "unknown" clinical item blocks readiness):** it forces the question, but it can delay the first visit after a hospital discharge. That brings back H-01 (missed care after discharge, the reason `visit_uncovered_hospital` is P1). A block must therefore never stop a hospital-discharge first visit. Whether "unknown" instead requires something at the first visit (for example, who performs it) is a clinical rule; I set none.
- **(3) Form.** Yes / no / unknown plus a note is a safe display form under three conditions: (a) "unknown" is distinct from "no" and shown first; (b) each value carries who recorded it, when, and from which source (referral, client, relative, doctor); (c) a "yes" requires the note. Whether a category needs structured detail (for example the type of precaution, or the allergen and the kind of reaction) is `clinical`. The product should store the value as dated, append-only rows, so a change never overwrites the earlier value.
- **(4) Review trigger.** Candidates the Clinical Owner can choose from, none decided: each care-plan review (the date signal `care_plan_review_overdue` exists); a return from hospital (a new referral or a returning-client link); a quality event of a relevant type (the type list is empty, CR-016); any team member marking a value as changed. Product part: a change shows in HAND1 as a new row.
- **(5) What the Clinical Owner approves:** the list of clinical categories; whether each needs structured detail; what "unknown" requires, if anything; the review triggers; who may record a clinical category (registered nurse only, or any member). Related: H-03 (S1), PS-4, D-15 (readiness), D-19 (contact and access data), D-3 (the cover read's field list includes these alerts), D-9 (legal basis at intake).
- **D-34 input class:** N (a care-context fact recorded by a nurse). As long as an alert is **only displayed**, it is not marked "for the adviser". If an alert ever ranks, routes or blocks a visit, it is marked, and it needs the adviser before real clients.

**Answer to the one question.**
- **Mostly covered.** Allergies, fall risk and infection status are health data of the client (DSG Art. 5 lit. c, as cited in D-9). Recording them at intake, for the care mandate, is the same processing as the rest of the intake: the D-9 legal basis and gates g1-g5 cover it, and no separate consent is needed for these items beyond D-9. Who may read them is D-3's read scope (owning team; a covering nurse only under the D-3 conditions, consent recorded). D-17 adds no new legal basis question.
- **Three points D-17 does add:**
  1. **Minimisation (DSG Art. 6 Abs. 2-3, as cited in D-9):** a category with yes / no / unknown and a short note is proportionate for a safety purpose. The note should name the precaution, not a diagnosis (for example "contact precautions", not the organism or the disease), unless the Clinical Owner finds the detail necessary. That is a protective product shape, not a rule read.
  2. **Emergency contact: data about a third person.** Name and phone of a contact are that person's personal data (not sensitive). Storing them for the client's care is plausibly covered by the client's mandate. Whether NAVORA or the provider must inform the contact person (DSG Art. 19, and the exceptions in Art. 20 Abs. 1 read for D-9) is **UNKNOWN**; the protective form is that the client agrees to name the contact and the intake records it. **Telling** the contact anything is D-19: recorded, express consent per contact is the only basis valid in every canton read (D-19, `lca` 16:05). D-17 should reuse the D-15 field and the D-19 consent flag, not create a second record.
  3. **Access or key arrangement:** record only that an arrangement exists and who holds the key; **never store a key-safe code** (D-19 note 1; DSG Art. 8 data security).
- **Option D (alerts derived from an assessment)** would interpret clinical information: a D-34 question, not answered here.

**Verdicts:** **B, with the clinical list approved by the Clinical Owner, the D-9 gates, the D-3 read scope, the emergency contact reused from D-15 with the D-19 consent flag, and no key-safe code stored: compliant under every plausible interpretation on the federal sources read**, cantonal law UNKNOWN (D-11, D-12), as in D-9 and D-3. **C** adds no legal question (a readiness rule is D-15 and clinical). **D: unresolved: needs owner with a regulatory adviser** (D-34). The information duty towards the contact person (point 2): **unresolved: needs legal adviser**, question: "Must a home-care provider that records, at the client's request, a relative's name and phone number as emergency contact inform that relative under DSG Art. 19, or does an exception in Art. 20 apply?"

**Note to po:** no new escalation; the one open question can join the D-9 adviser questions.
