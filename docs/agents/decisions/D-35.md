# D-35 — AI services: provider, basis and governance

## Decision summary
Before any agent in the [AI Operating Model Review](../../product/AI_OPERATING_MODEL_REVIEW.md) touches real data, NAVORA needs answers to two questions:
- whether client or staff data may be sent to an AI service at all;
- if so, to which provider and hosting country, on which legal basis, minimised how, recorded how, and for which agent levels (L, G, H; D-34).

**PO first triage:** the choice of provider, contract and country is `owner` (funding, partnership, possibly regulatory liability). The legal basis is a legal question for `lca` and, if unresolved, a legal adviser. Which agents may assist clinical documentation is `clinical` and D-34.

**Safe default in force:**
- no client or staff data reaches any AI service;
- agents run only on synthetic data;
- agents that need no AI are built as deterministic rules.

  - [A1] EDÖB, "KI und Datenschutz" (page published 24 September 2025) and "Update – Geltendes Datenschutzgesetz ist auf KI direkt anwendbar", 8 May 2025. The DSG is "technologieneutral formuliert" and applies directly to AI-supported processing. Manufacturers, providers **and users** must make the purpose, the functioning and the data sources transparent, and must secure informational self-determination "bereits bei der Entwicklung … und bei der Planung ihres Einsatzes". Users of a conversational system must be told that they are dealing with a machine **and whether their inputs are used to improve the software or for other purposes**. High-risk AI processing is permitted with appropriate measures **and a Datenschutz-Folgenabschätzung**. No article numbers are cited on either page; health data is not addressed.
  - [A2] Onlinekommentar zum DSG, Obrecht/Kuhn, commentary on **DSG Art. 21** (Stand 27 July 2026; PDF 6 October 2026), with the statutory text quoted: Art. 21 Abs. 1 covers a decision "die ausschliesslich auf einer automatisierten Bearbeitung beruht und die für sie mit einer Rechtsfolge verbunden ist oder sie erheblich beeinträchtigt"; Abs. 2 gives the right to state one's point of view and to have the decision reviewed by a natural person. The commentary: where a system only **prepares** a decision and a responsible natural person makes it, that is partial automation and falls **outside** Art. 21 Abs. 1 (the authors call the gap regrettable); the test is whether a person is legally and factually able to overturn the machine's result; the Austrian AMAS case is cited for assessing the overall circumstances rather than only whether a person was involved.

**Settled (on the sources read)**
1. **The DSG applies to AI processing without any AI-specific law** [A1], [M9]. There is no Swiss AI statute today and none is expected before the consultation draft announced for the end of 2026 [M9]. So the question "may client data reach an AI service" is answered today by the ordinary DSG, professional secrecy and contract, not by an AI act.
2. **Client care data is sensitive personal data** (health, [P1] Art. 5 lit. c Ziff. 2). Sending it to an AI provider that processes only on NAVORA's instruction is **Auftragsbearbeitung**: permitted only as the controller itself may process, and **only if no statutory or contractual secrecy duty forbids the transfer**; the controller must ensure data security; a **sub-processor needs the controller's prior approval** ([P1] Art. 9 Abs. 1-3).
3. **The controller here is the care provider, not NAVORA** (D-12 option B, as analysed on 2026-10-24). Therefore an AI provider would be NAVORA's **sub-processor** and needs the licence holder's **prior approval** ([P1] Art. 9 Abs. 3). It is not a decision NAVORA can take alone for pilot data, whatever the owner chooses commercially.
4. **Professional secrecy is the narrow gate.** Nurses are inside StGB Art. 321 [B1]; a *Hilfsperson* is not an unauthorised third party ([E7]), but the conditions include that the data is used **only to perform the contract** ([E7] p. 29). An AI provider that uses inputs to train or improve its model, or for any purpose of its own, is on that reading **not** acting purely as a *Hilfsperson*, and the transfer becomes a disclosure that needs consent or a statutory basis ([B1] §1.1, §3.1). "No training on NAVORA data" is therefore not only a commercial term; on the sources read it is a condition of the only reading that makes option B lawful at all.
5. **Transparency is a duty of the user of the AI, not only of the provider** [A1]: purpose, functioning and data sources must be transparent, and people must be told whether inputs are used to improve the software. For NAVORA this is an information duty of the provider towards clients and staff, with NAVORA supplying the text ([P1] Art. 19; D-9 g4, D-32 note 4 pattern).
6. **A DPIA is required for high-risk AI processing** [A1]; NAVORA already needs one for health data at scale (D-3, D-9, [P1] Art. 22). An AI processor adds a section to it, it does not create a separate regime.
7. **Cross-border** [A3]: a transfer to a state in DSV Annex 1 needs no further safeguard; otherwise the EU SCC recognised by the EDÖB, with NAVORA responsible for the recipient actually honouring them, technical measures where foreign law allows disproportionate state access, the country named in the information text (Art. 19 Abs. 4) and recorded in the register (Art. 12). Which states are in Annex 1 was not verifiable in this run [A4]; the US is recognised only through DPF-certified companies.
8. **Art. 21 (automated individual decision) does not bite on a proposal a person confirms** [A2]: where the system only prepares and a responsible natural person decides, that is partial automation, outside Art. 21 Abs. 1. Two provisos from the same source: the person must be **legally and factually able to overturn** the result, and the overall circumstances are assessed (AMAS). An agent whose proposal is in practice accepted unread moves towards Art. 21 and, more importantly for NAVORA, towards automation bias (H-03, `cra`).
9. **Staff data in prompts is employee data** ([W6], [W9] as read for D-23): using visit and schedule events for a new purpose needs the employee information and the ArGV 3 Art. 26 limit; a system that produces **per-person** output about conduct or performance is behaviour monitoring and in principle not allowed. The review's "not to build: per-nurse scoring" is on the sources read the legally correct line, not only a cultural one.
10. **Regulatory (D-34, not this package):** an AI module that interprets clinical information is the D-34 option C question. If such a module were an MDR device in class IIa or higher **and** had an EU nexus, the AI Act's high-risk duties would apply from **2 August 2028** [M6], [M7]; with NAVORA offered and used only in Switzerland and no output used in the EU, the Act is plausibly not engaged at all [M8] (a reading; the territorial article was not read).

**Options against the sources read**
- **A (no AI service with real data; agents on synthetic data; rules without AI where possible): compliant under every plausible interpretation.** No new processor, no transfer, no secrecy question, no new information duty. It is also the only option that needs nothing from the pilot provider. Agents A1 and A3-A6 built as deterministic rules raise no question under this package; they stay inside D-34's input-class control and the normal brief process.
- **B (Swiss or EU provider, processor contract, no training, minimised payloads, ledger; L and G agents first):** the shape is right, and I found no source that forbids it. It is **not** compliant under every plausible interpretation, because four things are missing and three of them are facts, not readings: the controller's (the provider's) **prior approval** of the sub-processor; a provider whose terms actually exclude training and own-purpose use; the country and the Annex-1 or SCC basis; and an answer on whether an AI provider is a *Hilfsperson* under StGB Art. 321 for health data. **Verdict: unresolved: needs legal adviser** (the Art. 321 question) **and owner** (provider, contract, country, and the provider's approval). Minimum conditions I would attach if the owner pursues B, each testable:
  1. **No clinical content, and no client identifier, in any prompt** unless the adviser has answered the Art. 321 question. Until then only staff-facing, non-clinical text (the drafting of a document the user is writing, with no client data) would be inside the owner's own risk.
  2. **A written processor agreement plus a confidentiality agreement**, with: processing only on instruction; **no training, no model improvement, no own purpose, no human review of inputs by the provider**; deletion within a stated short period; no onward transfer without approval; the EU SCC where the provider is outside DSV Annex 1 ([P1] Art. 9; [A3]; [E7] conditions).
  3. **The provider's prior written approval** of the AI service as a sub-processor, per organisation ([P1] Art. 9 Abs. 3), named in the D-12 partnership terms.
  4. **Information text** for clients and staff naming that an AI service is used, for what, which provider, which country, and that inputs are not used to train it ([A1]; [P1] Art. 19, Art. 19 Abs. 4).
  5. **DPIA section** and register entries updated before the first real-data call ([P1] Art. 22, Art. 12; D-3, D-9).
  6. **A person confirms every output that enters the record**, able in fact to reject it, with the source of each claim shown ([A2]; H-03). No agent output is a decision.
  7. **No per-person output about a staff member's conduct or performance** ([W9]; ArGV 3 Art. 26).
  8. **Ledger without payload copies outside the scope**: who asked, which agent and version, which provider and region, when, what was confirmed or rejected by whom — not a second copy of the client's data. Retention under D-4 ([P1] Art. 6 Abs. 4).
- **C (self-hosted model in NAVORA's own hosting region):** legally the **least exposed** of the options that use AI, because it adds no third party beyond the hosting processor already analysed in D-2: no new sub-processor approval beyond hosting, no training question, no new transfer. The secrecy question reduces to the one D-12 already asks about NAVORA itself. Model quality and operating cost are not legal questions. **Verdict: no legal obstacle found beyond those already open in D-2 and D-12; the Art. 321 *Hilfsperson* question for NAVORA itself stays open there, so: unresolved for the same reason as D-12, not for a new one.**
- **D (any provider, including outside the EU and CH): not recommended, and unresolved at best.** It adds the Art. 16 transfer question that D-32 and D-33 left unresolved for push vendors, on data far more sensitive than a push endpoint, plus the training question. On the sources read I would not call it compliant under any variant without an adviser.

**Verdict on the recommended default:** **A as the default in force: compliant under every plausible interpretation.** **B as the target: unresolved: needs legal adviser and owner**, on conditions 1-8 above. The safe default costs nothing legally and blocks only the agents the review itself puts at levels G and H.
