# D-14 — Platform fee and team surplus model

## Decision summary
How does NAVORA earn its money from a team's work (the platform fee), and what happens to what is left after pay and costs (the team surplus)? **PO working view, not a decision:** a fixed, published platform fee per billable hour (not a share of revenue), versioned and visible to every team member; the surplus is split by a written rule into a part distributed to the team's nurses and a part the team reinvests, and the distributed part follows **direct care delivered with quality held**, never hours present, visit count, category mix or (in nursing homes) the care-effort group. The **amounts** (fee level, distributed share) are pricing: `owner`. What the PO can fix within product authority is only the set of **design constraints** in *Recommended default*, which bind J7 whatever the owner chooses. Inputs requested: Engineering, Legal & Compliance (pay, profit share, KVG financing), Clinical & Regulatory on one point (perverse incentives on care).

## Context and problem statement
- **What it blocks:** J7 (team economics transparency) stays a draft (`briefs/J7.md`); without a model, the promise that recruits nurses ("higher income for more direct care") cannot be shown or checked. Value 3 (nurse income) of the owner's order, and the lever behind value 1 if income follows direct care.
- **IMPLEMENTED today:** nothing for fees or pay. J5 derives billable visit facts and a monthly revenue **estimate** (reference rows `validated = false`, no invoice, D-18). AN1 shows a within-visit care share; the North Star (care ÷ paid working time) is not measurable until D-23. No payroll data, no fee parameter, no surplus calculation exists in `supabase/migrations/` or `src/` (searched 2026-10-24 for `platform_fee`, `surplus`: no match).
- **Who pays whom is not settled:** the employer of the nurses and the holder of the OKP admission are D-12 (owner). Under D-12 option B (licensed partner organisation), NAVORA is a software and operating-model supplier to that organisation, and the "platform fee" is a contract price between two companies; the team's pay is the partner's employment matter. Under C (own entity), NAVORA is the employer. The model must work in both, or it must be decided after D-12.
- **Earlier PO notes (row):** pay more for more direct care, not more hours (2026-10-22); in nursing homes the care-effort group sets the tariff, so income must never follow the classification level (2026-10-22 15:05).
- **Safe default in force:** no fee or surplus is computed or shown; J7 draft; nobody's pay depends on NAVORA data.

## Options considered
**Fee (how NAVORA is paid):**
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Fixed fee per billable hour, published, versioned | predictable; the team keeps every efficiency gain; easy to check against J5 facts | NAVORA's income rises with volume, so it has a reason to favour more billed hours |
| F2 | Percentage of revenue | simple; NAVORA shares tariff risk | NAVORA gains from higher categories and higher rates; weakens the "income follows care, not classification" rule; opaque when residual financing differs by municipality |
| F3 | Fixed fee per team member per month (subscription) | no link to volume or category at all; strongest neutrality | small or part-time teams pay relatively more; NAVORA's revenue does not scale with care delivered |
| F4 | Fixed fee per client under care per month | follows clients, not hours; neutral to visit length | a team with heavy clients pays the same as one with light clients |

**Surplus (what the team does with what is left):**
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | All reinvested (training, equipment, reserve) | simplest; Buurtzorg-like | no direct income effect; value 3 unmet |
| S2 | A fixed share distributed equally per paid hour | simple, perceived as fair | rewards hours present, not care delivered (against the North Star) |
| S3 | A fixed share distributed by the team's own written rule, within guard rails set by NAVORA (no rule may use visit count, category mix or classification level) | team autonomy (value 4); the team owns fairness | needs a rule editor and an audit; risk of conflict inside a team |
| S4 | A share distributed in proportion to the team's **direct care share** above a floor, paid equally to members, only while quality and continuity guards hold | ties income to value 1; equal split avoids competition inside the team | the North Star needs D-23 first; guard thresholds are partly clinical |

**(1) Pay that depends on a team result**
- Settled ([E1]): a contractual share of profit, turnover or "sonst am Geschäftsergebnis" is lawful as part of pay. If agreed, it is calculated on the business year's result under accounting rules, and the employee has a right to the information needed to check it. A team surplus is plausibly a share "am Geschäftsergebnis" of a business unit; whether a team result counts as such under Art. 322a, or as a bonus or *Gratifikation* (Art. 322d, not read), is **UNKNOWN**. The difference matters: under Art. 322a the employee has a claim and a right to check the figures.
- What the written rule must contain, on the protective reading of [E1]: the basis (which revenue and which costs of which unit), the period (business year, or a shorter one if agreed), the method, the split, and the employee's right to information. **Design constraint 1 (published, versioned) and constraint 4 (nothing paid from an estimate) match [E1]: the result must be "festzustellen" under accounting rules, which an estimate is not.**
- Constraint 5 (no individual pay visible to colleagues) sits with the right to check under Art. 322a Abs. 2. An employee may need to see the team's figures, not colleagues' pay. A team-level result shown to every member, with individual amounts only to the person, is consistent with both. That a *team* rule (S3) may set individual shares is plausible under contract freedom but needs each employee's contract to refer to it (UNKNOWN: OR Art. 322 ff. not read in full).

**(2) Distributing a surplus at a provider financed by OKP and residual financing**
- The federal sources read ([E2]) do not forbid a provider from making a profit; they limit the *services* to what is needed. [E3] and [E4] show cantons financing private providers, some without a service mandate, through maximum rates or lump sums. Neither source states a non-profit requirement. On these sources, **distribution is not shown to be forbidden**; it is also **not shown to be permitted** where a *Leistungsvereinbarung* finances actual (not maximum-rate) costs, because a surplus there may have to be repaid or reinvested under that agreement (no agreement read: UNKNOWN). A provider with tax exemption for non-profit status may not distribute (tax law not read: UNKNOWN).
- **This depends on D-11 (canton and its financing rules), D-12 (legal form; non-profit or not; with or without a mandate) and the service agreement.**

**(3) KVG questions for the fee**
- KVG Art. 56 Abs. 1 ([E2]) is addressed to the provider's services, not to its supplier contracts. A fee paid by the provider to a software supplier is a cost of the provider. On the sources read, no rule forbids F1-F4 as such.
- The risk is indirect: a fee that rises with billed hours (F1) or revenue (F2) gives the supplier an interest in volume, and if the product nudges planning (PLAN2) towards more hours, services beyond the necessary are the provider's exposure under Art. 56 Abs. 2 (refusal and recovery). F3 and F4 avoid that link. Design constraint 2 (no rule reads category mix or classification) addresses upcoding (LT-2).
- Whether a supplier's fee tied to the provider's revenue must be shown separately in the cost accounting that feeds cantonal residual financing is **UNKNOWN** (KVV/KLV cost rules and cantonal rules not read).

**(4) Pay data inside a team:** pay is personal data of the employee (not listed as sensitive in DSG Art. 5 lit. c, as cited in D-19, not re-read). Disclosure to colleagues needs a basis: the employment contract or consent. The protective form is constraint 5: team figures for all members, individual amounts only to the person and the payroll role. If a team rule (S3) were to use per-person inputs (for example direct care share per person), showing them to the team would be a disclosure of per-person performance data; S4's equal split avoids that. ArGV 3 Art. 26 (behaviour monitoring; read in excerpt for D-23 today) is a further reason to keep per-person care-share figures out of the team view.

**(5) Missing facts:** D-12 (who employs, legal form, non-profit status, OKP admission); D-11 (canton; residual-financing rules; service mandate); the service agreement, if any; any GAV or public staff law; the employment contracts' pay clauses.
