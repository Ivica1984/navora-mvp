# D-36 — Scope of NAVORA's interRAI licence for product development and governance support

## Decision summary
The owner states (2026-10-27) that NAVORA has access to interRAI documentation and assessment material under a licence, and instructs that interRAI may **inform, but not replace,** clinical governance decisions. Before any agent uses that material, `lca` checks whether the licence permits the following uses. The final reading belongs to a legal adviser if it stays unresolved.

The uses to check:
1. **Reference use in governance documents.** Citing interRAI domains, CAPs, scales and indicators by name and section in decision packages, the Clinical Owner briefing pack and the Clinical Rule Register. No item text, no coding, no algorithm.
2. **Verification.** A person entitled under the licence checks the *interRAI reference* sections of D-15, D-16, D-17, D-28, D-30 and D-31 against the licensed documentation and marks each line *verified* or *not supported*.
3. **Agent use.** Agents (`po`, `cra`, `lca`) read licensed documentation or excerpts in their sessions, including through an AI service (D-35).
4. **Product-development use.** Designing NAVORA screens, fields or rules with reference to licensed content. This includes the D-13 B assessment record, which stores no items or scores.
5. **Any reproduction, derivative work or computation** in NAVORA's code, data or documentation. This is excluded today (D-13 B, D-34 exclusion list v2, TECHNICAL_DECISIONS); it is listed only so the licence answer covers it.

## Context and problem statement
- **IMPLEMENTED today:**
  - NAVORA stores no interRAI item, scale, CAP or score.
  - The Clinical Rule Register (*Not in the code*) keeps it so.
  - D-13 (po-decided, option B) records an assessment only as a fact: date, instrument and version, assessor, reference, and minutes per KLV category.
  - D-24 (awaiting owner) is about an interRAI LTCF licence for nursing homes, a different instrument.
- **UNKNOWN:**
  - who holds the licence: NAVORA, the owner personally, or a partner organisation (D-12);
  - who granted it: interRAI itself or a national licensee for the Swiss home-care edition;
  - which instrument and edition it covers, and its terms (field of use, confidentiality, derivative works, software integration, sublicensing, use by contractors and AI services, term and territory).
- **Not in the repository:** the licence text. The owner gives `lca` the terms named above; the licence itself is not committed if it is confidential.
- **Why this matters:**
  - Using licensed content outside its permitted scope is a contract and copyright risk for the owner.
  - Uploading licensed manuals into an AI service may be a disclosure that the licence does not allow, whatever the purpose.
  - Computed outputs would also change NAVORA's intended purpose (D-34).

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Name-level references only, no licensed material in agents or repository | as in force now: agents cite names from general knowledge; only an entitled person verifies against the documentation, outside the repository | no licence risk; enough for the Clinical Owner | verification needs a person; agents cannot check details |
| B A and agent reading of licensed excerpts | if the licence permits it: agents read named excerpts to prepare the interRAI sections; nothing reproduced in the repository | better preparation, less work for the Clinical Owner | depends on the licence allowing AI processing and use by contractors |
| C B and product development with licensed content | if permitted: design fields and screens against the instrument (still no computation, D-34) | closer fit to providers' tools | licence and D-34 risk; out of scope until D-13 A |

**Sources read (2026-10-27):**
- [I1] interRAI, "Guidelines for the Use of interRAI Intellectual Property", catalog.interrai.org, updated 27 January 2021: use "is governed by contracts and licenses"; "you must be licensed to use any version of interRAI forms"; use of the interRAI logo by others is prohibited without written approval; copyright attribution "© interRAI <year>" on interRAI intellectual property; electronic manuals may be downloaded to "a single computer or mobile device" and cannot be copied or printed; licensed software vendors may embed manual text, under interface restrictions.
- [I2] interRAI, "Licensing", catalog.interrai.org (no page date): "Use is permitted only by written license with interRAI; the purchase of a User's manual does not provide a use license"; licences usually cover individual instruments and individual nations; "The scales, algorithms, and case-mix measures based on these assessment instruments are not copyrighted", but the items they are built from are; royalty-free licences require not incorporating the instrument into products sold to or paid for by others; software vendors usually must offer sublicensing, data export and must not host licensed products on the open web.
- Earlier `lca` read: D-12 [E2] (Thurgau licence form lists an interRAI HC contract as a licence document for a home-care organisation; an example, not Zurich).
- **Not read:** the owner's licence; any Swiss national licence or distributor terms for interRAI HCSuisse; the Swiss Copyright Act (URG, SR 231.1) in the primary text (fedlex not reached in this run); the Zurich guidance on "anerkannte Instrumente" ([Z1] A.3.5.3, D-11) beyond its examples.

**Answers to the five questions**
1. *Uses 1-4 permitted by the licence?* **UNKNOWN for each** (terms not provided). On [I1]/[I2]: use of forms and manuals needs a written licence; buying a manual is not a licence; an electronic manual is tied to a single device and may not be copied. Use 3 (agents reading licensed excerpts) involves copying text into a session and, through an AI service, transmission to a third party (D-35); nothing in [I1]/[I2] permits that, and the single-device rule points against it. Use 4 (designing fields against the instrument) touches the items, which are copyrighted [I2]; scales and algorithms as such are not, but computing them is excluded for other reasons (D-34, D-13 B).
2. *AI service or contractors?* **UNKNOWN**; [I1]/[I2] do not mention AI. Contractor use is addressed only for licensed software vendors, under their own vendor licence. No basis read for uploading licensed text to an AI service.
3. *Confidentiality, derivative works, attribution?* Attribution: settled on [I1]: "© interRAI" with year on any interRAI intellectual property, and no logo without approval. Derivative works: [I2] allows no substantial change of an instrument except identifiers and demographics (additional items allowed); applied to a royalty-free licence. Confidentiality: not stated in [I1]/[I2]; **UNKNOWN** (licence terms).
4. *Is the licensee the user?* **UNKNOWN.** [I2]: licences are per instrument and per nation and often held by governments, care providers or software vendors. If the licence is held by the owner personally or by a partner provider (D-12 B), NAVORA and its agents are third parties to it unless it covers contractors. This is the first fact needed.
5. *Is option A compliant before the terms are read?* For **uses 1 and 5 as defined in option A** (names of domains, CAPs, scales and indicators from general published knowledge; no item text, coding, algorithm or excerpt; nothing taken from licensed material): **compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read**, on three terms: (a) the content comes only from published, non-licensed sources (no agent has licensed material, IMPLEMENTED per D-36 *Context*); (b) "interRAI" is used only as a name, with no logo [I1] and with a note that it is a mark/copyright of interRAI where an instrument is named; (c) no claim that NAVORA is licensed, endorsed or "interRAI-based". Names and the fact that a scale exists are not the copyrighted items [I2]; my reading of the URG (copyright protects expression, not ideas or methods) is from general knowledge, not from the primary text read today.
   For **use 2 (verification by an entitled person)**: the person reads her own licensed copy outside the repository and records only *verified* / *not supported* per line. That transfers no licensed text; whether her licence lets her use it for a third party's (NAVORA's) governance work is **UNKNOWN** (terms). Low risk, but not "every interpretation" until the terms are read.

**Verdict:** Option A (in force) **compliant under every plausible interpretation on the sources read** for name-level references from published sources, under terms (a)-(c). Verification (use 2): **unresolved: needs owner** (the licence terms; then possibly a legal adviser). Options B and C: **unresolved: needs owner** (terms) **and, if the terms are silent on AI or contractors, a legal adviser**. Nothing in [I1]/[I2] supports B or C today; the safe default A stays.

**Exact questions for the owner (facts) and, where the terms are silent, a legal adviser:**
1. Who is the licensee (NAVORA, the owner personally, a partner provider), who is the licensor (interRAI or a Swiss national licensee), which instrument and edition (HC, HCSuisse version), which territory and term?
2. Does the licence allow use by the licensee's contractors and agents, and the processing of licensed text by an AI service (a third-party provider, possibly abroad)?
3. Does it allow use of the documentation for designing software (fields, screens), as distinct from performing assessments? Does any software use require a software-vendor licence ([I2])?
4. Does it contain a confidentiality clause covering the manual or the item set, and may a licensed person confirm or reject statements prepared by others (use 2)?
