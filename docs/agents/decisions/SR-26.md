# SR-26 — Foreign `orders` table in the live database

## Decision summary
The live NAVORA Supabase project also holds `public.orders` from another application: 10 Stripe test-mode checkouts with e-mail addresses (VERIFIED by schema and counts, 2026-10-21; the owning app is inferred). Should it be moved to that app's own project and then deleted from NAVORA's, deleted outright, or left contained? The PO's first triage: removing it is an irreversible change to a live database, so only the owner may execute it. **Preliminary route: `owner`.** Inputs requested: Engineering, Legal & Compliance. No clinical input needed.

## Context and problem statement
- **VERIFIED:** `engineering/SECURITY_RISKS.md` SR-26 (Medium, OPEN — owner): mixed purposes in one database break data minimisation and widen the impact of any NAVORA access flaw. Containment C-1 removed anon access to `orders` too (10 of 10 tables). The repository's migrations do not create `orders` (ADR-022: the repository schema is the target), so a future deploy from this repository would not manage it.
- **Not known:** which application owns `orders`, whether it still writes to this project, and whether the e-mail addresses are test or real people's (Stripe test mode says nothing about whose e-mails were typed in).
- **Link:** D-1 (same live project; the D-1 answer and the key rotation also cover this table).
- **Safe default in force:** contained (anon has no access); no agent touches the live project.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Owner moves `orders` to the other app's own project (if it is still used), verifies the copy, then deletes it from NAVORA's project | one purpose per database; nothing lost | two projects to touch; the owner must know the other app |
| B | Owner deletes `orders` (after a private export kept outside the repository, if wanted) | simplest; minimisation | irreversible; breaks the other app if it still writes here |
| C | Leave it contained | nothing to do | SR-26 stays open; personal data with no NAVORA purpose stay in a health-data project |

**Settled (on the sources read)**
- An e-mail address of a real person is personal data ([S1] Art. 5 lit. a); not sensitive (Art. 5 lit. c), but with purchase data it can create a protection need (phishing; [S2] Ziff. 1.6.1). Stripe test mode says nothing about whose addresses were typed in (*Context*).
- Personal data are destroyed or anonymised once they are no longer needed for the purpose they were collected for ([S1] Art. 6 Abs. 4). The purpose here is the **other application's**, not NAVORA's. So the question is not whether NAVORA may keep them, but whether the other application still needs them.
- If the addresses are real and were reachable through the pre-C-1 grants, they fall under the same breach assessment as D-1 (non-sensitive data; [S2] Ziff. 1.6.1 and 2.1: information of persons may be required even without a high risk).
- The documentation duty covers facts, not the rows ([S3]).

**UNKNOWN**
- Which application owns `orders`, **who its controller is** (the owner or someone else), whether it still writes here, and whether the addresses are test addresses or real people's.
- Whether `orders` was actually readable by `anon` before C-1 (RLS state on that table; an `eng` read-only fact without contents).

**Options**
- **C (stay contained):** **compliant as a short interim** while the facts above are gathered. **As a standing state: unresolved: needs owner**: if the other application no longer needs the data, Art. 6 Abs. 4 points to destruction or anonymisation, and a contained table is still a second purpose inside a health-data project (a risk factor under Art. 8, not a breach in itself).
- **A (move to the other app's own project, verify, then delete here):** **compliant under every plausible interpretation**, provided (i) the other application's controller is the owner (or agrees to the move), (ii) the target project is at least as protected (no anon access), and (iii) the facts for D-1 (row count, field names, exposure window, no contents) are documented first.
- **B (delete outright):** compliant with Art. 6 Abs. 4 **if** the other application no longer needs the data and its controller is the owner; otherwise it deletes another controller's data. **Unresolved: needs owner** (those two facts).
- **Deleting before D-1 is answered:** no source read requires keeping the rows, but if the addresses are real and were exposed, they are the contact data needed to inform those persons (Art. 24 Abs. 4). So: **delete only after D-1 is answered** (A or synthetic), or after the D-1 legal adviser says no information is needed.

**Missing facts and who supplies them:** owner (the other application, its controller, whether it still uses the table, real or test addresses); `eng` (read-only: RLS state of `orders` before C-1, latest row timestamp without contents).

**Exact questions**
1. (owner) Which application wrote `orders`, is it yours, does it still use this project, and were the e-mail addresses your own or test addresses, or customers'?
2. (legal adviser, only if the addresses are customers' and were readable before C-1) Must those persons be informed under DSG Art. 24 Abs. 4?

**Verdicts:** C: **compliant as a short interim**; as a standing state **unresolved: needs owner**. A: **compliant under every plausible interpretation** under conditions (i)-(iii). B: **unresolved: needs owner**. Deletion before D-1: **not recommended**; see above.

**Note to po:** (1) Yes, the addresses widen D-1: add `orders` to D-1's question 1 and to its assessment. (2) The PO's working view (A, after D-1, same owner session as the key rotation) matches these conditions; add condition (iii), document the facts first.
