# D-15 — Readiness checklist, care-plan fields and the KLV category of a service

## Decision summary
Three approvals the Clinical Owner must give: (1) the J1 readiness checklist that lets a case be offered to teams; (2) the J3 care-plan fields; (3) how a planned service gets its KLV category (A assessment, advice and coordination; B examination and treatment; C basic care: the three groups of KLV Art. 7 Abs. 2 as `lca` read it for D-22, advisory). **PO 2026-10-24 10:11: recommendation written (option C under three `lca` conditions), `awaiting-approval: clinical`.** Earlier: no recommendation. PO direction for (3): NAVORA proposes the category from an approved service catalogue and the team may change it only with a reason; the catalogue itself is a clinical and billing rule, so it is drafted by `cra`, checked by `lca` and approved by the Clinical Owner. Approval: `clinical` in any case.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** clinical sign-off of J1 and J3; billing gates BL-6 (billing ignores qualification, needs assessment and review date) before any invoice; registers C-1 (S1: category chosen freely), C-2, C-3, C-6 (care-assistant scope for category A, CR-010, undefined); hazard H-04 (care by a person without the required qualification). Rules waiting on it (all UNVALIDATED): CR-001, CR-002, CR-003, CR-005, CR-007, CR-008, CR-009, CR-010, CR-033, CR-052.

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED):** the team chooses the KLV category per care-plan service, with no service→category mapping (CR-001, J3 deviation accepted by the PO); needs assessment first, category A, by a registered nurse (CR-002); category B only with an order `present` (CR-003); B and needs-assessment visits started, resumed and completed by a registered nurse only (CR-007, enforced at every transition since 0038); readiness checklist: consent, residence, insurer, insurance type, care need, requested start, with no clinical item (CR-033); care-plan fields: KLV category, planned minutes, frequency, review date, care goal (J3). **Safe default in force:** synthetic data only; the category drives the registered-nurse gates and the billable facts, so a wrong category is both a safety and a billing error.

## Options considered
| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Approve as built | the team keeps choosing the category freely | no change; full team autonomy | C-1 stays S1: a B service labelled C bypasses the registered-nurse gate and is billed wrongly |
| B Fixed catalogue | every service comes from an approved catalogue that carries its category; the team cannot change it | category errors disappear; no classification work for nurses | a catalogue never fits every situation; a missing service blocks planning |
| C Catalogue proposes, team decides with a reason | the catalogue sets the default category; the team may change it with a reason code (audited); the change raises a review signal for a registered nurse of the team | removes classification work in the common case; keeps professional judgement; every deviation is visible | needs the catalogue (clinical work) and a review rule |
| D Free text plus later coding | nurses describe services, coding happens later (back office or AI) | no up-front structure | the category gates the visit before it happens, so later coding is too late for safety; re-creates a back office |

Readiness checklist (1) and care-plan fields (2): options are "approve as built" or "approve with additions named by `cra`" (for example a clinical item in readiness such as known safety alerts, which is D-17, or a required assessment instrument, which is D-13).

**Settled (federal, [K1])**
- The law fixes the category **by service type**: each service is listed under exactly one letter of Art. 7 Abs. 2 (a, b or c), and the OKP contribution differs by letter (Art. 7a, [S1]). The category of a service is therefore not a free provider choice. What is the provider's professional assessment is which listed service an actual activity is, and whether it is needed (Art. 7 Abs. 1: on the basis of the needs assessment under Abs. 2 lit. a and Art. 8).
- Abs. 2bis sets two **qualification conditions in federal law**: a3 (coordination in complex situations) only by a nurse with two years of practice in interdisciplinary cooperation and patient management in networks; the assessment of whether b13, b14 or c2 are needed only by a nurse with two years of practice in psychiatry.
- Abs. 1: the services are provided by nurses (KVV Art. 49), home-care organisations (KVV Art. 51) and nursing homes. Within an organisation the federal condition read is staff "with training corresponding to the field of activity" ([L8] quoting KVV Art. 51). No source read says that every category B service must be performed by a registered nurse.
