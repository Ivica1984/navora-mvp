# D-22 — Valid form of a medical order and its renewal (OKP billing)

## Decision summary
Which form of medical order (and renewal) makes a home-care service billable to the mandatory health insurance (OKP): handwritten signature, an electronic signature of a given level, or a confirmation through a secure link; how the ordering doctor is identified; whether categories A and C need an order at all; and from which moment an order counts for a visit (on file before the visit, at the end of the visit date, or later). **Recommendation (po 2026-10-24 05:15):** care A/C without order with a recorded GP information step; billing only with a signed, identified order issued before the visit (a1/b2/c2). Awaiting approval by the `owner` (no legal adviser named). The strict defaults below stay in force until then.

## Context and problem statement
**Blocks:** ORD1 (draft: order renewal without chasing the GP), the relaxation of J5-C2 (order cut-off) and the order part of J5-C1; any real invoice (with D-12 and D-18). **Findings:** CGR-07 (closed with a strict default, CR-046 PROVISIONAL).

**What NAVORA does today (IMPLEMENTED, read in the repository by the PO):**
- An order is a recorded status (`present` or not) with an end date on the case (J1, J3). NAVORA stores no order document, no signature and no doctor identity beyond what J1 records. CR-003: category B may be planned only with the order `present` (0014:232).
- CR-004: categories A and C need no order; J1 creates an "inform GP" task (0014:263). Source in the register: a PO statement ("since 1 July 2024"), **UNVALIDATED**; pgTAP 043:107 asserts A and C without an order are billable. This is the open legal question R-8.
- CR-005: a B visit after the order end date is allowed and flagged; PLAN2 drops B after expiry (0014:148; 0030:103-107).
- CR-046 (PROVISIONAL): not billable (no estimate) when the order is `present` without an end date, expired on the visit date for any category, or the insurance type is not KVG (0043:198-213).
- CR-057 / CR-058 (PROVISIONAL): a billing fact's order basis is the order as recorded at the end of the visit date (Europe/Zurich), reconstructed from the audit history; a later order change never makes an earlier visit billable; `order_recorded_after_visit` marks the difference.

**Safe default in force:** orders stay as recorded in J1/J3; ORD1 stays a draft; NAVORA never makes an earlier visit billable from a later order change; the cut-off is the end of the visit date. Nothing is invoiced.

## Options considered
Sub-questions: **(a)** valid form and doctor identification; **(b)** A/C need an order; **(c)** cut-off.

| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| a1 Paper order, scanned and referenced | the order is a signed document; NAVORA records a reference and who checked it | no electronic signature question | phone, fax, re-typing stay; ORD1 impossible |
| a2 Qualified electronic signature of the doctor | GP signs electronically at a recognised level | ORD1 becomes possible end-to-end | GP adoption; which level is required is UNKNOWN |
| a3 Confirmation through an authenticated secure link (doctor identified, e.g. by a professional register number) | GP confirms in a NAVORA page; NAVORA stores who, when, what | lowest friction; ORD1 as briefed | validity for OKP UNKNOWN; doctor identity verification needed |
| b1 A/C need no order (today) | current CR-004 | less administration | source unvalidated |
| b2 A/C need an order like B | strict | no billing risk | adds GP work for every case |
| c1 End of visit date (today) | CR-058 | protects against back-dating | a delayed but valid order may lose revenue |
| c2 Order on file before the visit | strictest | simplest audit | more lost revenue |
| c3 Order may be recorded later if it covers the visit date | lenient | less lost revenue | back-dating risk; validity UNKNOWN |

## Clinical impact
*cra 2026-10-24 02:26 (copied from `reports/CLINICAL_GOVERNANCE.md` by po). Confirmed `cra` input, not a PO draft; copied verbatim.*

- **Hazards.**
  (1) *Care continues without medical oversight.* CR-005 lets B visits continue after the order end date with a visit flag (0014:147-148), and OPS1 raises `order_expiring` (P3, 7 days, closable by hand) per case. An order `present` without an end date raises nothing (CGR-38), so B care can continue with no renewal prompt at all.
  (2) *Care stops abruptly.* After expiry PLAN2 leaves B services out of generated visits (`order_missing_or_expired`, 0030:104-116). For B services such as wound care or medication, an unnoticed renewal gap means the care simply stops being planned. The prompt that prevents both is the expiry warning, which is P3 and closable by hand; whether that suffices is `clinical` (D-28, C-9).
  (3) *The doctor is not informed about A/C care.* The `inform_gp` task cannot be completed (CGR-36), so b1 as built has no evidence of the information `lca` reads as required (Art. 8a Abs. 1bis).
  (4) *Back-dating.* Back-dating is a documentation-integrity hazard as well as a billing one. CR-058 measures the order against the moment it was **recorded in NAVORA**. Per `lca`, the contracts measure retroactivity against the order itself, i.e. the doctor's issue date, which NAVORA does not store.
- **Findings that apply.** CGR-07 (closed, strict default CR-046); CGR-33 (closed, order history); CGR-36, CGR-38 (new, advisory); BL-4, BL-15.
- **Does b1 hide a safety-relevant GP notification gap?** Yes, today: CGR-36. b1 is only safe with a recorded, audited "doctor informed" step. b2 (an order or the doctor's *Kenntnisnahme* also for A/C) removes the gap by construction, at the cost of GP work. From a safety view I see no hazard in b2. b1 is acceptable only with CGR-36's remedy, and its legality is `legal`.
- **Sub-question (c).** Clinically neutral between c1 and c2. Back-dating protection matters for documentation integrity; c3 weakens it. If the doctor's issue date is recorded (`lca` note 2), store it as a separate, immutable fact next to the recording time, never overwriting it.
- **Safe default.** a1 (signed order referenced, signer identified) or "no electronic form" as today; b as built **plus** CGR-36; c1 as built (strictest built option); CGR-38 before go-live.
- **What only the Clinical Owner must approve.** Whether an expiry warning of P3, 7 days ahead and closable by hand is sufficient for B care, or must become a stronger signal (D-28). Whether B care may continue on an expired order while renewal is pending, and for how long (CR-005: a clinical and legal rule, not a product choice). The content of the information sent to the doctor for A/C (`clinical`, with `legal` for the duty). The form, the A/C order question and the cut-off are `legal`, not clinical.

**(a) Valid form of the order; identification of the doctor**
- Settled (federal): the KLV sets **no form** for the order: the doctor decides the need and the services (Art. 8 Abs. 1 [S1]); neither Art. 8 nor 8a says "schriftlich" or "Unterschrift" [S1]. Duration of an order for category B: max. **nine months**, renewable; acute and transitional care max. two weeks; open-ended for persons with a medium or severe *Hilflosenentschädigung*, renewed after its review (Art. 8 Abs. 2-4 [S1]). B services need the doctor's **explicit consent** to the needs assessment (Art. 8a Abs. 2 [S1]); the assessment result goes on a **uniform form developed by providers and insurers**, with the expected time (Art. 8a Abs. 4 [S1]).
- Settled for the two contracts read (both 2023): the order is evidenced by the standardised *Bedarfsmeldung*; for B the doctor must **sign** it, for A and C the doctor's **Kenntnisnahme** suffices; the provider's responsible nurse also signs; the doctor is identified by **ZSR and/or GLN** (GLN mandatory for electronic reports [S4]); the form **may be transmitted electronically in structured form**; for B the signed form must be shown on request; **without a valid Bedarfsmeldung no service is paid** ([S3] Art. 7.2; [S4] Ziff. 6.2.2-6.2.5).
- Settled (private law): a qualified electronic signature with a qualified time stamp under ZertES is equivalent to a handwritten one, **"subject to any statutory or contractual provision to the contrary"** (OR 14 Abs. 2bis [S5]).
- UNKNOWN: (i) whether the payers accept a qualified electronic signature as the "Unterzeichnung" of [S3]/[S4] (neither contract defines a signature level); (ii) whether a confirmation through an authenticated NAVORA link (a3) is a signature at all for B (it is not a qualified electronic signature in the sense of OR 14 Abs. 2bis unless built on a ZertES-recognised provider); (iii) whether a3 suffices as *Kenntnisnahme* for A/C (plausible, since no signature is required there, but not confirmed); (iv) which administrative contract applies to the pilot (depends on D-12: the admitted provider and its association membership, and on the payers).
- Option verdicts: **a1 (signed order, reference recorded): compliant under every plausible interpretation** for the form question, provided NAVORA records the signer's ZSR/GLN, the order's own date and validity, and keeps the document available for the payer (the storage itself raises D-3/D-4/D-9 questions, not settled here). **a2: unresolved: needs legal adviser.** **a3: unresolved: needs legal adviser** (for B likely insufficient on the contract wording read; for A/C plausible).

**(b) Do A and C need an order, and since when?**
- Settled (federal): since **1 July 2024** [S2, S6], A (assessment, advice, coordination) and C (basic care) "können ohne ärztliche Anordnung oder ärztlichen Auftrag" be provided; B only on order (Art. 7 Abs. 4 [S1]). Conditions: a needs assessment; the result is sent **promptly** to the treating doctor for information (Art. 8a Abs. 1bis [S1]); a report to the doctor **after 27 months** of care without an order (Art. 8a Abs. 8 [S1]; full wording not read, see below). Secondary [S6]: the invoice must show which services were given with and without an order. The PO statement behind CR-004 is therefore supported by the federal text; CR-004's source can cite KLV Art. 7 Abs. 4 and 8a Abs. 1bis.
- UNKNOWN: (i) whether the administrative contracts were amended after 1 July 2024: [S3] and [S4] (2023) still require an order for all nursing services and the doctor's *Kenntnisnahme* on the Bedarfsmeldung for A/C, and contain an adaptation clause ([S4] Ziff. 16); (ii) the exact content and trigger of the 27-month report (Art. 8a Abs. 8 read only in part); (iii) the invoice-marking rule (only from a secondary source).
- Option verdicts: **b2 (an order or the doctor's Kenntnisnahme also for A/C): compliant under every plausible interpretation** (it meets the 2023 contracts and the 2024 law). **b1 (today): unresolved: needs legal adviser**: compliant with the federal KLV read, but possibly non-billable under a payer contract not yet adapted. NAVORA's "inform GP" task (CR-004) matches Art. 8a Abs. 1bis in substance, but it is "never required" today, while the law says the result **is** sent; whether an unsent notification affects billing is UNKNOWN.

**(c) May an order recorded after a visit cover it?**
- Settled for the two contracts read: **"Rückwirkende Verordnungen sind nicht zulässig"** ([S3] Art. 7.2 Abs. 1; [S4] Ziff. 6.2.2). The Bedarfsmeldung goes to the payer within one week of the doctor's signature or Kenntnisnahme, at the latest with the first monthly invoice; the needs assessment is completed within 14 calendar days after care starts; the payer may object within 15 working days ([S3] Art. 7.1 Abs. 4, 7.2 Abs. 1, 3; [S4] Ziff. 6.1.4, 6.2.2, 6.2.4).
- UNKNOWN: what "rückwirkend" is measured against (the date the doctor issued the order, the date the provider received it, or the date the payer received the Bedarfsmeldung), and how it relates to care that starts before the assessment is complete (14 days). The federal KLV read says nothing on this.
- Option verdicts: **c3 (later order covers earlier visits): not compliant** with the wording of [S3]/[S4]; unresolved only if the pilot's contract differs. **c2 (order on file, and issued, before the visit): compliant under every plausible interpretation.** **c1 (today: as recorded at the end of the visit date): unresolved: needs legal adviser**: an order issued by the doctor after the visit but recorded on the same day would count under c1 and may be "retroactive" under the contracts.
