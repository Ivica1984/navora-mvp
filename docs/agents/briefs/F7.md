**Why changes-requested.** The direction is right: documentation becomes part of finishing the visit. One gap is about what a signature means. The sign form shows times, minutes, services and only the **number** of observations; the observation and handover text she signs is not visible. A signature must attest to content the nurse can see. This is a documentation-integrity rule, not a clinical rule. Concrete, testable changes:

- [ ] **She sees what she signs.** The sign form shows the full text of every observation and handover note that goes into the report, with category and time, before the confirm button. e2e: an observation recorded during the visit is visible in the sign form.
- [ ] **A failed draft is visible.** If the automatic draft fails after completion, the card says so and offers "Bericht erstellen", instead of silently showing "Bericht fehlt". Unit or e2e test.
- [ ] **Countersignature queue e2e:** a registered nurse opens `/field`, sees the queue entry, follows it and countersigns; the report becomes final.

**Deviations, decided:** summary save and signature are two calls without a transaction: **accepted**, the failure mode is "not signed", which is safe. The unreachable edit mode is engineering's clean-up, no product impact.

## Problem
**Challenge finding (PO, 2026-10-22).** F6 made the signed report correct. But the report is still a separate step after the visit, in the traditional order: do the visit, document it later, sign it later. OPS1 needs a "report not final after 24 hours" exception precisely because the step is separate. All the content of the report already exists at the moment the nurse completes the visit (F1–F4 events, observation, handover, planned minutes per category). The separate step is pure administrative delay.

## Users and journey step
The nurse who performed the visit. Journey: Visit → Documentation.

## Outcome
Completing the visit and signing its report are one action. Most reports are final before the nurse leaves the client's home.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] When the nurse completes a visit, the completion screen shows the report draft built from the visit data (as F6). She reviews it and signs it with **one confirmation**, with no re-entry.
- [ ] She can still sign later; then the existing F6 flow and the OPS1 "report not final" exception apply unchanged.
- [ ] A countersignature (D-10 rule) is requested from the team at the same moment, and the countersigner sees it in her day view.
- [ ] Nothing in the F6 guarantees changes: the signature records who and when, a signed report is immutable, corrections create versions with a reason, and only the assigned team can read it.
- [ ] Measured in AN1: median time from visit end to final report, and the share of reports signed at completion. Pilot target: half of reports final within 10 minutes of visit end.
- [ ] Works at phone width (e2e).

## Out of scope
AI-written report text (Gate 4); voice input; changing the countersignature rule itself (D-10).

## Constraints and references
