**Why changes-requested.** The safety core (no-show → P1) works, and the product value is real: plans and exceptions follow the client's situation. But C1 is the safety precondition for the whole journey, so it is accepted only when the brief's negative tests exist and two behaviours are fixed. Concrete, testable changes:

- [ ] **Close withdraws matching:** closing a case with an open matching request or open offers withdraws them (no team can still accept). pgTAP proves it.
- [ ] **Close leaves records untouched:** pgTAP proves that open case tasks are closed and that final reports, quality events and billing facts of the case are unchanged after close.
- [ ] **Late team cancellation stays visible until handled:** the P2 continuity exception stays open until someone closes it with a reason in OPS1 (or the visit is replanned), instead of disappearing after a day. pgTAP proves it is still open two days later.
- [ ] **No-show produces no report draft and no billing fact:** pgTAP.
- [ ] **Suspended visits are marked on `/field`** ("pausiert", with the pause reason category, no clinical detail) and have no depart button, instead of failing after the tap. e2e proves it at phone width.
- [ ] **Negative tests:** a member of another team in the same organisation cannot pause, resume, cancel or record a no-show; another organisation cannot cancel, close or reopen; a member of neither the assigned team nor Operations cannot reopen. One pgTAP assertion each.
- [ ] **Audit:** one pgTAP assertion that pause, close and no-show each write an `audit_events` row with the reason.

**Deviations, decided:**
- *Resume without a reason:* **accepted.** Resuming restores the agreed plan; the pause already carries the reason. ADR-041 is correct to narrow the F0 rule here.
- *No-show can be recorded from `on_the_way` (after departure, before a recorded arrival):* **accepted provisionally**, because recording earlier escalates earlier, which is the safe side. Routed to `clinical` inside D-19 for confirmation.
- *The notice "until then your team rules apply":* **accepted**; it gives no clinical instruction.
- *Visit states await D-6:* unchanged, `clinical`.

## Problem
The journey can start a case and complete visits, but nothing can end or interrupt them. In home care this happens every week: a client goes to hospital, moves to a nursing home, reaches the care goal, declines care, or dies. Visits are cancelled by the client or the team, and sometimes nobody opens the door. Today none of these commands exist (F0 report, ADR-034 guard). The consequences:
- planned visits stay on the plan for a client in hospital;
- OPS1 raises false "uncovered visit" exceptions;
- the continuity measure (J6) and the revenue estimate (J5) are distorted;
- a client who does not open the door is a possible emergency with no workflow at all.

## Users and journey step
The assigned team (pause, resume, cancel, no-show, close); NAVORA operations (close and reopen, exceptions). Journey: Planning → Visit → Documentation → Billing, plus the end of care.

## Outcome
The team can interrupt or end care and cancel or record a missed visit in a few taps, with a reason. Plans, exceptions, continuity and billing then follow the real situation without manual clean-up.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] **Pause a case** with a reason from a list (hospital stay, rehabilitation, holiday or absence of the client, other) and an optional expected end. Planned visits inside the pause are suspended (kept, not deleted), raise no uncovered-visit exception and produce no billing fact. **Resume** reactivates them, or the team replans.
- [ ] **Close a case** with a reason from a list (care goal reached, moved to inpatient care, moved away, client declined further care, client died, other). On close:
    - all future planned visits are cancelled with the close reason;
    - open case tasks and the primary-nurse assignment end;
    - the case leaves offers and the team's active list.
    Signed reports, quality events and billing facts stay unchanged.
- [ ] **Reopen** a closed case with a reason, by the assigned team or Operations. Reopening runs the J1 readiness check again before any new visit can be planned.
- [ ] **Cancel a visit** before it starts, with a reason from a list (cancelled by client, client in hospital, cancelled by team, other). A cancelled visit is not uncovered, not billable, and excluded from the continuity measure. A cancellation by the team less than 24 hours before the visit appears in OPS1 as a continuity exception (P2).
- [ ] **No-show (client not reached)** after arrival at the address. It is recorded with a mandatory note and creates a **P1 safety exception** in OPS1 at once. The exception stays open until the team records the outcome (client reached, emergency contact informed, emergency services called, other). The steps the nurse must take on site follow D-19 (`clinical`). Until D-19 is answered, the app only records and escalates. It gives no instructions of its own.
- [ ] No-show and cancelled visits produce no billable fact (J5) and no report draft (F6).
- [ ] Every one of these commands needs a reason (F0 rule; the ADR-034 guard already expects them) and is audited and idempotent.
- [ ] Negative tests: nobody outside the assigned team can pause, resume, cancel or record a no-show; only the team or Operations can close or reopen; other organisations get nothing.

## Out of scope
Cancellation fees charged to the client, notifications to relatives or authorities, the death certificate process, automatic hospital-admission feeds.

## Constraints and references
D-6 (`clinical`): visit states, including cancelled and no_show. D-19 (`clinical`): the on-site protocol when a client does not open the door. F0 mandatory-reason rule. OPS1 priorities: P1 safety, P2 continuity (Q9).
